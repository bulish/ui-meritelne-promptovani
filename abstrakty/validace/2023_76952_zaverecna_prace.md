This bachelor thesis delves into the European Union regulations, specifically focusing on the legal and tax implications for Slovak businesses operating in Germany. It comprehen- sively analyses the necessary documents and information crucial for these businesses and their employees. The theoretical part of the thesis outlines the freedom of movement for labour and services across EU countries and the legal frameworks governing these free- doms. The study compares the tax systems in Slovakia and Germany, highlighting the differences in income tax and social security contributions through case studies of self- employed individuals like Ján and Tomáš. The analytical section evaluates various taxa- tion scenarios for these individuals, aiming to identify and prevent common reporting errors and tax discrepancies. The thesis contributes significant new insights to the field of EU market operations, providing empirical, data-driven understanding beneficial for businesses navigating the complex European market.
