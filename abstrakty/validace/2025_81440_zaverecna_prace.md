Ghana’s economy has long depended on gold, timber, and crude oil, with gold alone generating about USD 13 billion in 2025. Severe environmental damage from illegal mining highlights the need to expand Non-Traditional Agricultural Exports (NTAEs) such as palm olein, processed cocoa products, shea butter, coconuts, cashew, and yams. This thesis analyses the socio-economic and ecological impacts of NTAEs using 2000-2025 data, assessing effects on GDP, employment, and the ecological footprint within Ghana’s diversification agenda. Using ARDL, and ECM models, the study shows NTAEs enhance economic resilience but face challenges including environmental degradation, quality-control issues, and exchange-rate volatility.
