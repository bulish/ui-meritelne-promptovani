This thesis investigates food security and nutritional status in Ghana, focusing on four main objectives: assessing current food security trends, identifying key influencing factors, evaluating policy interventions, and examining progress toward Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 1, 2, and 3. The study employed secondary data analysis using national surveys and international databases, including the Global Food Security Index (GFSI). Descriptive and trend analyses were conducted across key food security dimensions: availability, affordability, quality, and sustainability. The analysis shows modest progress in food availability, with Ghana’s farm infrastructure score improving by 48.2 points, and volatility in agricultural production remaining low (score: 91.8). However, affordability dropped by 4.4 points, and food prices surged by over 30%, contributing to food insecurity among 49.1% of Ghanaians. Nutritional outcomes remain concerning, with 66% of children under five affected by anemia, and stunting prevalent in 17.5% of children. The absence of a national nutrition plan led to a drop in nutritional standards from 57.1 to 29.8 points. On sustainability, Ghana scored poorly at 45.1, reflecting weak disaster preparedness and adaptation capacity. Government efforts, such as the Planting for Food and Jobs initiative, improved supply sufficiency, yet gaps in safety nets and regional disparities persist. Food insecurity affects 46.7% in the north compared to 13.8% in the south. Although Ghana ranks 3rd in food security in Sub-Saharan Africa, it is still off-track for achieving SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030. The study recommends enhancing post- harvest infrastructure, implementing a national nutrition policy, scaling social protection, and adopting climate-smart agricultural strategies.
