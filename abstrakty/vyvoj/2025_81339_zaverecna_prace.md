This thesis explores whether there are systematic patterns in cybercrime cost reporting that may suggest the presence of publication bias. It focuses on how selective data use and inconsistent methodologies may influence which estimates are more likely to be published and cited. By analyzing both academic and industry studies, we identify that reports with higher loss figures are more frequently visible in the public domain. This visibility can shape political narratives, media coverage, and business decisions. The thesis proposes several ways to address this imbalance, including improved transparency and more consistent reporting frameworks. Our findings suggest that we should not only look at the largest numbers but also critically assess where these figures come from and how they are constructed.
