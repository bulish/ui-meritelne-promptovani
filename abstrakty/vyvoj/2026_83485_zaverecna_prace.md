This thesis aims to explore the lived experiences of LGBTQ community members in a country where same-sex relationships are prohibited. This is exacerbated by the Constitution of Zimbabwe Section 78, which defines marriage as exclusively between a man and a woman. The aim of this research is to find out how the LGBTQ community navigates their daily life and how this has impacted them and to understand how they have been resilient. Discrimination and social stigma continue to shape everyday life, and this is further necessitated by religious and cultural norms. This is shown by the fact that 32 out of 54 African countries criminalize same-sex marriages. The study employed qualitative research design and snowball sampling was used to recruit participants after GALZ provided the first participants who then later started referring to other participants. The researcher conducted 18 telephonic interviews as well as a few on Microsoft teams. The data gathered was analyzed using thematic analysis from recurring issues mentioned by the study participants. The research sought to explore the personal experiences of LGBTQ persons. This was done by asking them a set of questions in which the researcher was able to probe for more answers by asking follow-up questions. The study determined that religious and cultural norms are the drivers and major perpetrators of discrimination and stigma of the LGBTQ community in Zimbabwe. The LGBTQ community still suffer from persecution as they are always being told that homosexuality is a western norm and it is un-African hence cannot be accepted. The findings of the research showed that LGBTQ community suffers rejection from family, and the society at large hence they conceal their sexuality which is a survival mechanism. Some of them come out of the closet but they face family rejection and this creates stress leading to depression. Most of the LGBTQ members have resorted to use of drugs and alcohol so that they can cope and not stress as much.
