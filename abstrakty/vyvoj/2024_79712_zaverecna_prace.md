The quick advancements in natural language processing (NLP) have enabled significant improvements in text annotation tasks, including sentiment analysis and named entity recognition (NER). This thesis explores improvements that could be made regarding user-friendly interface, collaboration between annotators, ensuring high quality of annotations, and reducing the manual annotation workload through automation. Text annotation is a critical step in various NLP tasks, providing structured labels that enable downstream applications like machine translation, text classification, and information retrieval. The thesis also investigates the performance of these LLMs in handling sentiment analysis and NER tasks across different datasets, comparing their accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-scores. GPT-4, with its generative capabilities, is compared with the fine-tuned BERT and TinyBERT models, which are traditionally used for classification and token-level prediction tasks. The evaluation is conducted using real- world datasets such as the Twitter Sentiment Analysis dataset and NER datasets comprising documents from diverse domains. The models' outputs are benchmarked against ground truth labels to determine their efficacy in automating these annotation tasks.
