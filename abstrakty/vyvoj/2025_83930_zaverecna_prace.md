This bachelor’s thesis explores how the Czech Republic’s foreign policy toward the Srebrenica genocide has developed between 1995 and 2025. Drawing on a qualitative case study approach, it examines official documents, museum resources, and expert interviews to trace the historical, political, and international influences that have shaped Czech diplomatic responses. The research particularly highlights the impact of Czech historical experiences of oppression, the significant role of prominent political leaders such as Václav Havel, and the involvement of the Czech Republic in international organizations like NATO and the United Nations. The findings reveal a gradual progression from initial diplomatic caution to explicit acknowledgment of the Srebrenica genocide and alignment with European Union stances on human rights and transitional justice, as demonstrated through comparative analysis. The thesis contributes to understanding, how smaller European states negotiate their moral responsibilities concerning sensitive issues such as genocide, within complex international contexts.
