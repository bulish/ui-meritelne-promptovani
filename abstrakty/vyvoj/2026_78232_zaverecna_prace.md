
Climate change (CC), driven mainly by fossil fuel consumption, requires urgent action to avert irreversible consequences. One promising mitigation strategy is the exploitation of forest biomass for energy production. Due to its renewability, versatility, and availability, forest biomass has become one of the most utilized alternative energy sources. However, its carbon neutrality status as well as its overall environmental impacts remain subjects of debate. The overarching goal of this thesis is to advance the understanding of the role of forest biomass exploitation for bioenergy production as a CC mitigation option by (1) evaluating its carbon neutrality status, including its limitations and underlying assumptions, (2) conducting a life cycle assessment of heat production in Russia via combustion of pellets derived from sawmill residues, (3) providing a framework for monetary valuation of ecosystem services related to bioenergy generation. This thesis employs a scoping review methodology to analyze the carbon neutrality concept and identify methods for monetary valuation of ecosystem services. The life cycle assessment methodology is used to carry out the environmental impact analysis. The results of this thesis show that (1) carbon neutrality status is methodologically contingent, requiring rigorous evaluation, (2) decisions in supply-chain design, such as transportation distance and the heat source used for drying in pellet production, significantly influence environmental effects of bioenergy generation, and (3) while a number of monetary valuation approaches can be utilized for ecosystem services assessment, a standardized framework is required to address inconsistencies in terminology. This thesis confirms the overarching hypothesis that exploitation of forest biomass for bioenergy can contribute to climate change mitigation, but that its sustainability depends on rigorous assessment. The findings of this thesis provide a methodological framework and empirical evidence to support sustainable bioenergy
development for climate change mitigation, while highlighting the need for harmonized environmental and economic assessment.
