This bachelor’s thesis explores the dependency of Zambian participants in development projects on development assistance, with a focus on both financial and non-financial support. Using an ethnographic methodology, comprising semi-structured interviews, focus groups, and participant observation, the study examines how local actors of development projects perceive and justify their involvement in donor-driven initiatives. The findings reveal a persistent reliance on external assistance, shaped by short-term project logic and institutional expectations. This research contributes to the debate on assistance effectiveness, highlighting the need for more sustainable and locally embedded approaches to development cooperation.
