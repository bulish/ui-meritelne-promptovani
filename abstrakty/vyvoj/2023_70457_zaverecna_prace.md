Despite technological advances offering a wide range of materials, wood remains the most widely used material for the production of musical instruments. This is primarily due to its unique mechanical properties. These properties are related to the specific structure of wood, particularly selected species that have, over the course of the development of musical instruments, become established as reference materials in terms of sound quality. However, these high-quality woods are becoming increasingly valuable, which increases the need for their efficient use and at the same time emphasizes the importance of suitable alternatives.
The aim of this dissertation thesis is the investigation of key parameters for the qualitative evaluation of materials for musical instruments and of the influences that may affect their reliable determination. For the extension of existing methods, the following were assessed: 1) the influence of the dimensions of the tested samples; 2) the influence of material characteristics and of factors that affect them (e.g. moisture, degradation, modification); 3) the influence of the vibration mode from which the parameters are determined; 4) the influence of the measuring technique, including the instruments used as well as the measurement methodology; 5) the characteristic of the vibration spectrum.
The work consists of four publications in peer-reviewed journals and one conference proceeding paper. Various wood species (spruce, beech, ash, oak, lime, alder, larch, and maple) and wood-based composite materials (plywood made from pine, birch, and poplar) were tested. The main method used for determining vibro-acoustic parameters was the frequency resonance technique, which was complemented in individual studies by static, dynamic, and impact bending tests, as well as by numerical modal analysis using the finite element method.
The results show that when using the frequency resonance technique, it is necessary to consider specimen dimensions and to to choose an appropriate measuring system. The investigated vibro-acoustic parameters allow for distinguishing between different wood species and wood-based composites and are sensitive to moisture-related and structural changes. Comparison with other methods revealed that parameters obtained under different loading conditions cannot be used interchangeably. It was found that the shape and order of vibration modes influence the determination of the evaluated parameters and that the spectral distribution of these modes provides information that can be used for the qualitative assessment of materials for musical instruments.
