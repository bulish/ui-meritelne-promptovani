This thesis aims to investigate income inequality in the EU-27 from 2007 to 2023 using three inequality measures: the Gini coefficient, Atkinson Index and General- ized Entropy indices. It reviews trends, patterns and disparities between countries to show how using multiple measures can give a better picture of inequality in the income distribution. The study uses income shares data from Eurostat and other sources to measure inequality in each country. The results show significant varia- tions among member states with great inequality reductions in countries like Poland and Romania. In contrast disparity in Sweden and Malta has increased. For big EU economies, Germany and France the results reveal stable inequality levels over the time period. This study analyzes inequality within a diverse regional framework and emphasizes the importance of using complementary measures for a more thorough analysis.
