Since the fall of the Soviet Union, both Ukraine and Poland have faced significant challenges and transformations. Despite starting from similar economic conditions in the early 1990s, Poland has achieved substantial economic growth, particularly through European Union (EU) integration, while Ukraine has struggled with internal and external challenges that have slowed its progress. This thesis aims to identify the key factors that enabled Poland to surpass Ukraine in economic development, despite their comparable starting points. Through a comparative analysis of economic indicators such as GDP growth, inflation, and unemployment rates, the study focuses on the period from approximately 1989 to 2023. The empirical research will analyze time series data for both countries to highlight differences in economic performance. The findings indicate that Poland’s timely EU integration, successful market reforms, and strong institutional frameworks facilitated its transition to a market-driven economy. Conversely, Ukraine's economic development has been hindered by political instability, incomplete reforms, and corruption.
