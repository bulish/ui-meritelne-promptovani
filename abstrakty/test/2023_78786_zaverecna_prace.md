This thesis investigates the factors that contribute to Nigeria's high unemployment rates, a serious
problem that has seriously impeded the nation's socioeconomic advancement. The study looks into both cyclical and structural causes of long-term unemployment, such as macroeconomic volatility, poor educational systems, restricted access to capital, and a mismatch between labor force capabilities and market demands. It also looks at how economic policies, demographic pressures— particularly the youth bulge—and political instability affect employment trends. This study identifies the main obstacles to job creation using a combination of qualitative and quantitative methods, such as data analysis, literature review, and interviews with important stakeholders. It also suggests policy recommendations meant to promote sustainable economic growth, enhance education and skill development, and create an environment that is favorable to job creation. The results demonstrate the intricacy of Nigeria's unemployment issue and the necessity of all- encompassing, multidimensional actions to deal with the underlying reasons and lower the unemployment rate.
