The present work is concerned with the topic of organizational commitment and the question, how this highly relevant construct can be fostered by leaders and managers in corporate settings. The contemporary business environment is shaped by a strong focus on employees, their skills, knowledge and subsequently motiva- tion, to achieve favorable goals for their respective companies. With regard to the war for talent this thesis showcases, how a focus on attracting and subsequently re- taining highly qualified and highly motivated employees can be a feasible strategy for businesses in different fields.
Organizational commitment is herein described as a complex construct consist- ing of various facets that are in relationships with each other. Especially the role of behavioral and emotional commitment are highlighted as predictive valid for vari- ous organizational outcomes. The leading research question of this thesis therefore is, how organizational commitment can in a first step be operationalized and meas- ured and in a second step fostered by leaders.
Following an extensive literature review on the concepts of leadership, organi- zational commitment and personality and values as moderating factors between the two first mentioned constructs, this thesis followed an empirical approach to ad- dress the research question presented therein. A total of three publications was de- veloped in order to foster understanding of the complex relationship between lead- ership and organizational commitment. These three publications employ the per- spective of employees from different industries and are based on a cumulative sam- ple of more than n = 900 employees, spread between the three individual studies.
In a first step, the factor structure of a newly developed measure for organiza- tional commitment was assessed and validated. Based on pre-existing items and a complex analysis strategy following the framework of a confirmative factor analysis, the two-factor structure of organizational commitment as a construct consisting of emotional and behavioral aspects could be established.
The subsequent publications utilized this newly developed measure in order to assess the predictive power of leadership variables and personality traits. Therein it could be shown, that while personality traits and personal values do take on a
moderating role, it is ultimately (transformational) leadership that has the strongest predictive power in regard to organizational commitment. This, subsequently is also the main contribution of the present work, which is described throughout this thesis both in regard to potential limitations and to the contribution concerning the current state of research.
