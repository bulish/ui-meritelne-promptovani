Agriculture is a critical sector in Nepal, contributing significantly to employment and food security. Tomato is one of the most widely cultivated crops; however, it is highly vulnerable to various diseases that can drastically reduce yield and quality. Early detection of such diseases is essential for effective crop management.
Traditional disease detection methods rely heavily on manual inspection, which is time- consuming, requires expert knowledge, and is often inaccessible to farmers in rural areas. This research proposes a lightweight deep learning-based system for real-time tomato leaf disease detection using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), along with a conceptual treatment recommendation component.
Three models were developed and evaluated: a custom CNN, a MobileNetV2-based transfer learning model, and a fine-tuned MobileNetV2 model. The models were trained on the PlantVillage dataset and evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrices. The fine-tuned MobileNetV2 achieved the highest performance, with a test accuracy of 93.44%, demonstrating the effectiveness of transfer learning combined with fine-tuning.
The results demonstrate that lightweight pretrained models combined with fine-tuning provide an effective balance between accuracy and computational efficiency. However, limitations were observed in real-world generalization, particularly when non-target plant images were used. The trained model was also deployed and tested in a mobile environment, demonstrating its ability to perform real-time predictions with practical applicability. The proposed system shows strong potential for integration into mobile applications to assist farmers in real-time decision-making.
