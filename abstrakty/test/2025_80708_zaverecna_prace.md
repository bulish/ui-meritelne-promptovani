The export of tea and coffee allows the agricultural sector in Kenya to expand its productive capacity and generate additional income. In this thesis, I determined the factors influencing the export of tea and coffee from Kenya via the ARDL cointegra- tion methodology. The current level of Kenya tea exports is influenced by previous period tea exports, current tea price, current and lagged tea production, current and lagged hectares under tea, current and first lag of global income and local tea con- sumption. In the long run, tea exports are negatively influenced by the price of tea and local tea consumption and positively influenced by hectares under production and global income. In the short run, tea exports are positively influenced by lagged tea production and local tea consumption and negatively influenced by the hectares under tea production and the global income levels. The speed of adjustment of short-run fluctuations in tea exports to long-run equilibrium is -0.777 per year. The current export of coffee from Kenya is positively determined by the price of coffee, current and lagged coffee production and negatively determined by income in the OECD countries and precipitation. In the long run, coffee exports are positively in- fluenced by the price of coffee and coffee production and negatively influenced by income in OECD countries and precipitation. In the short run, coffee exports are neg- atively influenced by the volume of coffee production. The speed of adjustment of short-run coffee export fluctuations to the long-run equilibrium is -0.826 per year.
