Floodplain forests are among the most dynamic and productive ecosystems in Europe, yet their persistence is increasingly threatened by climate change, river regulation, and groundwater depletion. These pressures alter site hydrology and intensify drought, driving growth decline, shifts in composition, and species dieback. This thesis investigates the growth responses, hydraulic strategies, and resilience of two key floodplain species, pedunculate oak (Quercus robur L.) and narrow-leaved ash (Fraxinus angustifolia Vahl.), across local and continental scales.
In floodplains of the Czech Republic, tree-ring analyses revealed that groundwater and drought indices were the main determinants of radial growth, whereas temperature played a weaker role. Oak displayed more stable growth and higher drought resistance, while ash exhibited greater growth variability and a stronger dependence on precipitation. In contrast, wood anatomical analyses highlighted that vessel traits in both species were shaped mainly by groundwater and precipitation. Oak maintained consistent xylem anatomical features across years and sites, while ash showed less stability, reinforcing its sensitivity to fluctuating water conditions. These contrasts motivated a continental-scale analysis focused on oak, using tree rings from 33 populations across a wide latitudinal and temperature gradient.
Across this network, responses varied geographically, shaped by long-term adaptation to local environments. Drought sensitivity strengthened from north to south, while temperature influence increased with elevation. Recovery was highest in northern populations, whereas high-elevation populations showed the greatest drought resistance.
Together, these results reveal species-specific strategies and population-level adaptations that govern resilience to hydrological change and drought. They provide empirical support for adaptive, water-sensitive management, demonstrating the potential of targeted hydrological measures, including artificial flooding, to mitigate water stress in vulnerable stands. At the same time, this study suggests the potential of oak populations with traits favourable for assisted population migration. Combining these approaches offers insights
into management strategies to sustain productivity and resilience in future floodplain forests under changing climates.
