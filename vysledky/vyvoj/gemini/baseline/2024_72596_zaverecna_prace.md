## Summary

This dissertation addresses the lack of a uniform definition and measurement framework for digital sovereignty, specifically within European Union (EU) states. The author establishes a foundational literature review exploring state sovereignty from historical models (such as Westphalian and Bodin frameworks) to modern governance via Foucault, alongside the evolution of industrial revolutions and information technology.

Synthesizing these concepts, the author defines digital sovereignty through a European value-based lens, emphasizing strategic autonomy, "good governance," and control over Key Enabling Technologies (KETs) rather than total digital autarky. To operationalize this definition, the study constructs a hierarchical model containing three main sub-indices: state sovereignty, focus on KETs, and technological sovereignty.

Using secondary data across approximately 200 nations, the author develops a composite Digital Sovereignty Index (DSI) using robust statistical methods like normalization, weighting, and missing data imputation. The resulting index is verified through simulation and validated against existing country metrics. Finally, the study clusters state performances and provides actionable policy recommendations and fields of action for European decision-makers to enhance strategic autonomy.
