Summary of the Article
This diploma thesis investigates the changes and impacts of the Fourth Industrial Revolution (Industry 4.0) on the labor market within the retail sector, focusing specifically on Brno, Czechia. Using a quantitative study design, data was gathered through structured surveys administered to 399 retail workers.

The findings reveal that a strong majority of retail employees experience operational modifications due to new technologies, with many acknowledging that automation alters daily workflows, handles tasks such as inventory and self-checkout, and reduces repetitive manual duties. However, these technological advancements also generate widespread concern regarding job security. A significant proportion of respondents believe that Industry 4.0 will decrease the overall number of retail jobs and make future employment uncertain, while simultaneously demanding new technical skills and adaptability.

To mitigate these disruptions, the author recommends that employees develop essential soft skills, that the Czech government integrate adaptive educational models to prepare the workforce, and that stakeholders collaborate closely to support occupational mobility and continuous training.
