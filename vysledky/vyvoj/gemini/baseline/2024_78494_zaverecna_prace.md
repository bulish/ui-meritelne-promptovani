This study investigates income inequality across all 27 European Union (EU) member states from 2007 to 2023. It utilizes three principal measurement frameworks—the Gini coefficient (alongside Lorenz curves), the Atkinson index, and Generalized Entropy (GE) measures—to capture structural shifts and regional variations that a single metric might overlook.

The findings indicate that while aggregate EU inequality remained relatively stable on average, individual member states followed divergent trajectories. Substantial reductions in inequality were observed in Eastern European economies such as Romania and Poland, driven by narrowing income gaps and gains in lower-income deciles. Conversely, historically egalitarian countries like Sweden, as well as Malta, experienced notable increases in inequality, largely propelled by income concentration at the top. Meanwhile, nations with robust welfare systems or strong structural foundations, such as Germany, France, the Czech Republic, and Slovakia, maintained stable or consistently low levels of inequality. Ultimately, the research emphasizes that combining complementary indices is vital for identifying where disparities concentrate across different segments of the income distribution.
