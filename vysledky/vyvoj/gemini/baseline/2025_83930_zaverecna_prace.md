This bachelor's thesis by Eliška Císařová analyzes the evolution of the Czech Republic's foreign policy regarding the Srebrenica genocide between 1995 and 2025, evaluating the historical, political, and international influences on Czech diplomatic discourse.

Initially, Czech foreign policy displayed hesitation and minimal engagement following the 1995 massacre. A significant shift occurred in 2005 on the tenth anniversary when the Ministry of Foreign Affairs officially recognized the atrocity as genocide. Over the subsequent decades, the Czech stance increasingly aligned with European Union norms, supporting international criminal tribunals (ICTY and ICC), backing regional reconciliation, and robustly rejecting genocide denial. This trajectory was reinforced internationally by UN and NATO commitments, and domestically by human-rights-focused political leadership. In 2024, the Czech Republic further solidified this stance by voting in favor of the UN General Assembly resolution establishing the International Day of Reflection and Remembrance for the Srebrenica Genocide. While Czech policy closely mirrors the EU's overall diplomatic and legal framework, minor differences persist, particularly regarding the EU's more institutionalized and systematic approach to educational and preventive initiatives compared to Czechia's more ad hoc efforts.
