## Summary of the Article

This diploma thesis evaluates and compares the operational performance and energy efficiency of two distinct grid-tied photovoltaic (PV) power plants located in close geographic proximity in the Czech Republic over a two-year monitoring period (2023–2024).

* **Power Plant A**, situated in Sentice, is a smaller-scale installation with an installed capacity of 39.8 kWp mounted on an industrial food-processing facility at a 25° tilt. It exhibits high self-consumption (74.84%), low grid dependency (41.35%), and a stable performance ratio (PR) ranging between 0.615 and 0.622.


* **Power Plant B**, located in Čebín, features a larger capacity of 139.44 kWp with a 15° panel tilt, supplying an energy-intensive metal-processing facility. While its PR improved significantly from 0.656 in 2023 to 0.770 in 2024, it suffers from recurring weekend overproduction mismatches and seasonal inefficiencies due to its flatter tilt and inflexible industrial demand profile.



Both systems were analyzed using key performance indicators (KPIs) aligned with ČSN EN IEC 61724-1 standards and benchmarked against PVGIS simulation data. The study concludes that Power Plant A operates near optimal efficiency requiring only minor software adjustments, whereas Power Plant B would benefit from operational planning, soiling mitigation, and medium-scale battery energy storage.
