## Summary

The diploma thesis investigates the work-study-life balance (WSLB) and well-being of university students who combine studies with employment, comparing students from Mendel University in the Czech Republic and Pukyong National University (PKNU) in South Korea. Utilizing a quantitative, cross-sectional design via online questionnaires, the study examines time allocation, stress factors, emotional well-being, cultural perceptions, and life satisfaction.

The empirical findings reveal significant structural differences between the two student groups. Korean students allocate significantly more time to daily studying, whereas Czech students spend more hours on paid employment and household duties. Despite these objective differences, both groups report similar levels of subjective time availability and rest ability. However, study-work demands spill over differently: Czech students experience a more pronounced negative impact on their physical health and relationships, whereas Korean students report higher anxiety, overload, and stress driven by academic pressures, social expectations, and future uncertainty. Furthermore, substantial cultural differences emerged, with Korean students perceiving a much stronger societal and familial emphasis on performance-based success and status. Ultimately, Czech students report higher overall satisfaction with their work-study-life balance compared to their Korean counterparts.
