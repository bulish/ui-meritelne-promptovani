This diploma thesis investigates the development and establishment of European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) qualification standards for harvesters, forwarders, and skidders. Driven by high transnational mobility, an absence of unified vocational qualifications, and rising accident rates across Europe, the study addresses the critical need for transparent, standardized Pan-European certification.

The methodology combined literature reviews, stakeholder engagement, and a comparative mapping analysis of national occupational standards (NOS) from the United Kingdom, the Czech Republic, and Slovenia. By aligning learning outcomes with the European Qualifications Framework (EQF Level 4) and utilizing guidelines from the International Labour Office and UNESCO, the author formulated harmonized competence levels structured around five core operational phases: work planning, pre-use inspection, safety checks, operations, and post-use inspection.

The newly established EFMO standards were pilot-tested in practical field trials—primarily in Ireland—to verify assessment criteria, refine diagnostic tools, and eliminate ambiguity. Ultimately, the research successfully implemented the framework via the awarding body ABA International, resulting in independent digital certifications for dozens of operators across Europe.
