This diploma thesis investigates the empirical relationship between banking sector development and innovation activities across 24 European Union countries from 2010 to 2020. Utilizing dynamic short panel data, the research models patent application volumes (as a proxy for innovation) against various indicators of banking development using the two-step system General Method of Moments (GMM).

The econometric findings demonstrate that banking sector development—measured via indicators such as private credit, bank credit to deposits, and deposit money bank assets relative to GDP—exerts a statistically significant positive impact on innovation activities. This confirms that a robust and well-functioning banking system provides critical financial resources that foster national innovation. Additionally, control variables such as GDP per capita, tertiary education enrollment, and public governance quality generally exhibit positive relationships with patent generation, while providing a comprehensive framework for understanding macroeconomic innovation drivers.
