This study analyses food security and nutritional status in Ghana, evaluating current trends across availability, affordability, quality, safety, and sustainability dimensions. Although Ghana ranks relatively high within Sub-Saharan Africa, it faces significant domestic challenges. Food affordability has deteriorated due to surging food prices, inflation, currency depreciation, and weakened social protection systems. While agricultural production stability has improved, critical supply chain inefficiencies lead to extensive food loss. Nutritional standards have sharply declined in the absence of a comprehensive national nutrition strategy, leading to a rising double burden of malnutrition characterized by child stunting, anemia, and increasing adult obesity. Furthermore, sustainability and climate adaptation remain weak due to inadequate water resource management and limited disaster readiness. Government interventions like the "Planting for Food and Jobs" program have successfully boosted yields, yet structural constraints and sharp regional disparities persist—particularly affecting northern rural areas. Consequently, Ghana remains off-track to fully achieve United Nations Sustainable Development Goals 1, 2, and 3 by 2030.
