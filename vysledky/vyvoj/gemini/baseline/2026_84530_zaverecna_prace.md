This bachelor’s thesis examines the role of student movements in Thailand’s pro-democracy protests from 2020 to 2021, applying Social Movement Theory (SMT). The study highlights how the dissolution of the reformist Future Forward Party (FFP) and public dissatisfaction with the 2019 general election served as primary triggers for mass mobilization. Utilizing a decentralized network structure, student activists heavily relied on digital platforms like Twitter, Facebook, and Telegram for coordination, real-time documentation, and transnational solidarity through the Milk Tea Alliance. The movement framed its grievances through diagnostic, prognostic, and motivational strategies, shifting from early demands for the prime minister’s resignation, parliamentary dissolution, and a new constitution to breaking long-standing taboos by openly calling for limits on the political and economic influence of the monarchy. State authorities responded primarily via repressive measures, including emergency decrees, police crackdowns, and legal prosecutions under lèse-majesté laws. Despite state repression and structural limitations, the movement significantly influenced public discourse and translated its reformist agenda into electoral success during the 2023 general election via the Move Forward Party (MFP).
