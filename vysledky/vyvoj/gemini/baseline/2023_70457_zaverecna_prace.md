Summary of the Scientific ArticleThe thesis explores advanced methods for analyzing the vibro-acoustic and mechanical properties of materials used in musical instruments, primarily focusing on wood and wood-based composites. It investigates how factors such as specimen dimensions, moisture content (MC), thermal modifications, fungal degradation, and vibrational modes influence dynamic properties like the dynamic modulus of elasticity ($MOE_{dyn}$) and the damping coefficient ($\tan\delta$).   Using the frequency resonance technique (FRT) alongside dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) and numerical modeling, the research highlights that while specimen geometry and slenderness primarily govern modal intervals and frequencies, material characteristics heavily dictate damping and energy radiation. Elevated moisture content and fungal decay (Kretzschmaria deusta) drastically decrease elastic moduli and increase internal friction, whereas controlled thermal modifications improve dimensional stability and acoustic conversion efficiency ($ACE$) depending on treatment temperature and species. Furthermore, the study introduces multimodal aggregate parameters ($ACE_{combi}$ and $RACE_{combi}$) to provide a more comprehensive, robust material evaluation across longitudinal, bending, and torsional modes than single-mode assessments.
