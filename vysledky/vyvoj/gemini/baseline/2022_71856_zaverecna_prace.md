This study evaluates the relationship between tax policy, government expenditures, and economic growth in Ghana using time-series data from 1990 to 2022. Employing econometric tools such as the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test, Johansen cointegration test, Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), and Granger causality test, the research explores both long-term equilibrium dynamics and short-term causal connections.

The cointegration results indicate that capital expenditure and total tax revenue share a positive long-term relationship with real GDP, whereas inflation and revenue expenditure exert a negative impact on economic growth. The VECM analysis demonstrates that deviations from the long-term equilibrium are corrected at an annual adjustment speed of approximately 4.3%. Furthermore, the Granger causality test reveals a unidirectional short-term relationship running from total tax revenue to real GDP, indicating that tax revenue acts as a predictive factor for economic growth in Ghana. The author concludes that strengthening domestic resource mobilization and prioritizing capital over revenue expenditures are essential for fostering sustainable economic expansion.
