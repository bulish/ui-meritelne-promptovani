## Summary

This Ph.D. thesis investigates the environmental and economic context of exploiting forest biomass for bioenergy, focusing on climate change mitigation. Through a compilation of published studies, the research evaluates the carbon neutrality (CN) concept, performs a life cycle assessment (LCA), and reviews monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services (ES).

First, a scoping review demonstrates that CN is not an inherent property of biomass. Instead, CN status depends on methodological factors, including feedstock origin, temporal and spatial boundaries, and scenario assumptions. Eight distinct CN concepts were identified, revealing a lack of a universal definition. Second, an LCA of residential heat production using wood pellets derived from sawmill residues in Northwest Russia indicates that pellet combustion and waste disposal are the most impactful stages across most environmental categories, whereas transportation plays a minimal role. Third, a scoping review of monetary valuation methods classifies over twenty approaches into direct market, indirect market, non-market, and alternative categories, highlighting terminology inconsistencies and hybridization. Ultimately, the thesis concludes that forest biomass can effectively contribute to climate change mitigation if evaluated through a transparent, systematic framework like LCA and supported by harmonized economic assessments.

## Supporting Source Sections

* **Chapter 1 (Introduction):** Outlines the background of climate change, the reliance on forest biomass as a primary bioenergy feedstock, and the structural logic of the thesis.


* **Chapter 2 (Methodology):** Details the specific methodologies utilized, including the scoping review framework and the standard LCA approach under ISO guidelines.


* **Chapter 3 (Carbon neutrality of forest biomass for bioenergy: a scoping review):** Analyzes the eight distinct concepts of CN, temporal/spatial boundaries, and scenario-based assumptions.


* **Chapter 4 (Life cycle assessment of residential heat production from wood pellet combustion in the Northwest region of Russia):** Details the gate-to-grave LCA model, inventory data, environmental impact assessment results, and sensitivity analyses.


* **Chapter 5 (Methods for monetary valuation of ecosystem services: a scoping review):** Classifies and describes direct market, indirect market, non-market, and other valuation approaches.


* **Chapter 6 (Is forest biomass used for bioenergy production a viable climate change mitigation option?):** Synthesizes the core findings regarding CN and LCA to discuss bioenergy's viability for climate mitigation.


* **Chapter 7 (Conclusions):** Summarizes the fulfillment of research objectives, addresses the working hypotheses, and provides final takeaways.
