* **Goal:** This diploma thesis evaluated and compared the real-world operational performance, energy efficiency, and key performance indicators of two photovoltaic (PV) power plants with distinct capacities and technical configurations in the Czech Republic over a two-year period (2023–2024).


* **Methods:** The study utilized a comparative analytical approach based on retrospective monitoring data extracted from SolarEdge platforms for Power Plant A (Sentice) and Power Plant B (Čebín). Operational parameters, energy yields, and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) were calculated and evaluated in accordance with the ČSN EN IEC 61724-1 ed. 2 standard and compared against PVGIS simulation data.


* **Main results:** Power Plant A (39.8 kWp capacity, 25° tilt) generated an annual energy output of 86.38 MWh, consuming 64.648 MWh (74.84%) on-site with an overall self-sufficiency of 58.65% and a grid dependency of 41.35%. Its specific yield was 807.493 kWh/kWp in 2023 and 816.839 kWh/kWp in 2024, with a Performance Ratio (PR) of 0.615 and 0.622, respectively. Power Plant B (139.44 kWp capacity, 15° tilt) produced 254.062 MWh, consuming 197.368 MWh (77.68%) on-site with a self-sufficiency of 46.68% and a grid dependency of 53.32%. Power Plant B's specific yield rose from 838.043 kWh/kWp in 2023 to 983.976 kWh/kWp in 2024, while its PR increased from 0.656 to 0.770.


* **Conclusions:** Both plants operate efficiently within their design constraints, but Power Plant B exhibits seasonal inefficiencies—such as weekend overproduction and autumn output drops—that can be mitigated through recommendations like integrating a medium-sized battery energy storage system (BESS) storing 10–15% of daily output, installing soiling sensors, applying hydrophobic coatings for winter, and implementing adaptive load-shifting strategies. Power Plant A is already close to optimal, requiring only minor software-level refinements.
