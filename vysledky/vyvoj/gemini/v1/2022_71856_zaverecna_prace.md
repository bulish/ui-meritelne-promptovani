## Summary of the Study

* **Goal**: To assess the role of taxation as a catalyst for economic growth in Ghana by analyzing its association, as well as the linkage of capital and revenue expenditures, with the country's economic advancement.


* **Methods**: The study utilized annual time-series data spanning from 1990 to 2022 sourced from the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA), the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, and the World Development Indicators (WDI). The general analytical approach employed multivariate time-series techniques, including the Augmented Dickey-Fuller unit root test, Johansen cointegration test, Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), and Granger causality test.


* **Main results**: The Johansen cointegration test revealed a long-term relationship where a 1% increase in both capital expenditure and total tax revenue corresponds to an approximately 20% increase in Ghana's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), while a 12% inflation rate and a 9% revenue expenditure adversely affect economic growth. The VECM indicated an error correction term coefficient of -0.043, showing an annual adjustment rate of approximately 4.3% back toward equilibrium. Furthermore, the Granger causality test established a unidirectional short-term relationship indicating that total tax revenue Granger-causes real GDP (F-statistic = 3.905, p-value = 0.037), whereas inflation, capital expenditure, and revenue expenditure do not show immediate short-term causality with GDP.


* **Conclusions**: The authors conclude that there is a statistically significant positive causal link between taxation and economic growth in Ghana in both the short and long terms. Consequently, they recommend that the government intensify domestic revenue mobilization by expanding the tax base and modernizing administration, prioritize public spending toward productive capital and growth-fostering sectors, and manage the public sector wage bill through productivity-based reviews.
