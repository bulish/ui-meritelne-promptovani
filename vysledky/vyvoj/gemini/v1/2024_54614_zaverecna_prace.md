**Goal**

The research evaluates the efficacy, surface properties, and weathering behavior of traditional wood preservation techniques used before the industrial revolution.

**Methods**

European beech and oak wood samples were subjected to various historical treatments, including one-sided surface charring at temperatures of $200^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $400^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 0.5 to 20 minutes, ammonia gas fuming, iron nanoparticle impregnation, and coating with formulations of linseed oil and plant resins. Analyses utilized X-ray densitometry, contact angle measurements, mechanical testing, UV-accelerated ageing chambers, and ATR-FTIR spectroscopy.

**Main results**

One-sided charring of beech reduced lightness ($L^*$) by approximately 50% for radial and 42% for tangential specimens. Radial specimens exhibited smoother surfaces and improved fire resistance, showing weight losses as low as 6.8 wt.% compared to 16.6 wt.% for references. Decay resistance tests revealed lower weight loss against *Coniophora puteana* (average 11.61 wt.%) than *Trametes versicolor* (average 18.04 wt.%). Water uptake in radial specimens decreased significantly (e.g., by 56% for the $200^{\circ}\text{C}/20\text{ min}$ group), whereas tangential groups showed no statistically significant decrease due to cracking. For oak wood, both ammonification and iron nanoparticle (INP) treatments significantly darkened the wood, reducing lightness by about a third. Total color change ($\Delta E^*$) correlated strongly with extractive and phenolic content ($R = 0.72$ to $0.86$). After six years of natural weathering, wood painted with opaque linseed oil formulations demonstrated superior color stability and durability, whereas lazure coatings and unpigmented oil formulations experienced extensive degradation and paint loss.

**Conclusions**

Traditional methods such as surface charring, ammonia/iron treatments, and oil-resin coatings effectively enhance the aesthetic and functional properties of wood. The authors recommend optimizing charring and treatment conditions to balance wood stability, minimize cracking on tangential surfaces, and improve resistance against environmental weathering.
