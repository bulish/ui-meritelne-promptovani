**Goal**
The research examines the current state of Vietnam's wood industry and its exports to Europe to identify influencing factors and propose effective solutions for future export growth.

**Methods**
The study utilizes documentation research, synthesis, analysis, comparison, logical evaluation, and international model referencing based on market, trade policy, and export data spanning recent years (including 2018–2020).

**Main Results**
Vietnam's total export turnover to Europe reached USD 56.45 billion in 2020 (a 35.9% increase). Total wood and wood-product exports reached USD 12.37 billion in 2020, representing a 16.2% growth from the previous year. Primary input cost components for Vietnamese wood exports include primary source of income (55–62%), salary and insurance costs (15%), power and energy costs (5–7%), repairs and depreciation (1–2%), and transportation and other non-manufacturing costs (6–16%). Furthermore, the EU imports approximately $85 billion USD in wood commodities annually, whereas Vietnam exports around $700–800 million USD to the EU each year.

**Conclusions**
The authors conclude that boosting Vietnam's wood exports requires transitioning from small-scale manufacturing to large-scale production, establishing rigorous company standards, and securing legal domestic and certified forest raw materials. Recommendations include improving labor productivity, enhancing compliance with the EU-Vietnam Free Trade Agreement (EVFTA) and Voluntary Partnership Agreement (VPA/FLEGT) standards, upgrading technology, and strengthening trade promotion and supply chain linkages.
