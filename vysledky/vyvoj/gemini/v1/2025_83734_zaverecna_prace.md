* **Goal:** To explore how university students in South Korea (PKNU) and the Czech Republic (Mendel University) perceive and experience work-study-life balance (WSLB) across multiple domains and how this balance relates to their overall well-being.


* **Methods:** A comparative, cross-sectional quantitative questionnaire survey was conducted among 547 employed students (132 from Mendel University and 141 from PKNU in the analytical sample, plus broader group samples for specific items) during 2025 using online forms and on-site distribution. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, independent-samples $t$-tests, Spearman correlations, multiple linear regressions, and chi-square tests in IBM SPSS Statistics.


* **Main results:** Korean students reported significantly higher study hours ($M = 6.60$ hours/day) compared to Czech students ($M = 5.62$ hours/day) ($t = -6.845, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = -0.73$). Conversely, Czech students spent significantly more time working or interning ($M = 4.25$ vs. $3.28$ hours/day) ($t = 4.298, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.45$) and on household duties ($M = 1.73$ vs. $1.33$ hours/day) ($t = 4.197, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.45$), while sleep duration was comparable (median 7 hours/day). Czech students experienced significantly higher health-related strain ($M = 3.49$ vs. $3.11$) ($t = 3.208, p = 0.002$) and relationship interference ($M = 3.12$ vs. $2.75$) ($t = 3.090, p = 0.002$), alongside higher emotional exhaustion ($M = 3.51$ vs. $3.15$) ($t = 2.501, p = 0.013$) and work-related stress ($M = 3.32$ vs. $2.45$) ($t = 7.377, p < 0.001$). Meanwhile, Korean students scored significantly higher on academic stress ($M = 4.11$ vs. $3.86$) ($t = -2.775, p = 0.006$), social expectation stress ($M = 3.84$ vs. $2.95$) ($t = -6.420, p < 0.001$), future uncertainty stress ($M = 3.48$ vs. $2.79$) ($t = -4.629, p < 0.001$), and anxiety/overload ($M = 3.89$ vs. $3.57$) ($t = -2.345, p = 0.020$). Overall life satisfaction with WSLB was significantly higher among Czech students ($M = 2.75$) than Korean students ($M = 2.47$) ($t = 2.394, p = 0.018$). Furthermore, mental health service utilization differed significantly ($\chi^2 = 40.300, p < 0.001$), with Czech students showing greater willingness to seek support.


* **Conclusions:** Student well-being is not governed by a single factor but emerges from complex interactions among academic structures, employment conditions, cultural norms, and individual coping strategies. The authors conclude that institutional flexibility and support structures are vital for mitigating role overload, and recommend that universities address cultural stigmas surrounding mental health care while providing targeted relief from academic intensity in South Korea and workload pressures in the Czech Republic.
