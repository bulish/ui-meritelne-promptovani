## Summary

* **Goal**: This study evaluates Ghana's current food security and nutritional status, examines major influencing factors, analyzes government policies, and assesses progress toward United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 1, 2, and 3.


* **Methods**: The research utilized secondary data from national surveys, international organizations (such as the FAO and WHO), and government reports. Descriptive statistics and trend analysis were applied to evaluate historical patterns and population-wide metrics across various dimensions of food security.


* **Main Results**: Ghana ranked 83rd out of 113 countries globally with an overall food security score of 52.6 out of 100, while ranking 3rd out of 28 countries in Sub-Saharan Africa. Food insecurity affected 49.1% of the population, with profound regional disparities showing 46.7% affected in the north compared to 13.8% in the south. Average food costs deteriorated by -31.5 points, and food inflation reached 30.1% in 2022. Farm infrastructure improved by +48.2 points to 58.6, and agricultural production volatility scored 91.8. Conversely, food loss scored 0 points (ranking 113th globally), and nutritional standards dropped from 57.1 points in 2021 to 29.8 points in 2022. Furthermore, 66% of young children under five were affected by anemia, and stunting stood at 17.5%.


* **Conclusions**: The authors conclude that despite incremental progress in agricultural productivity and poverty reduction, Ghana remains off-track to achieve SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030. They recommend reducing post-harvest losses through infrastructure investments, implementing a comprehensive national nutrition strategy, scaling up social protection programs like LEAP, strengthening climate-smart agricultural practices, and deploying localized interventions to bridge north-south regional disparities.
