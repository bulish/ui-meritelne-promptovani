## Goal

The study investigates the day-to-day personal experiences, systemic challenges, coping strategies, and support systems of LGBTQ+ community members in Zimbabwe.

## Methods

The research employed a qualitative design utilizing an interpretive approach and narrative inquiry. Data were gathered through 18 in-depth, semi-structured interviews conducted via WhatsApp and Microsoft Teams with self-identified LGBTQ+ Zimbabwean citizens and residents aged 18 to 45, recruited via snowball sampling through local organizations like GALZ and PAKASIPITI.

## Main Results

Participants reported pervasive marginalization driven by legal, cultural, and religious hostility. Key quantitative and qualitative findings include:

* **Demographics and Employment:** The study comprised 18 participants (aged 18–45), encompassing lesbians, gay men, transgender individuals, and intersex persons across various occupations (e.g., unemployed, students, athletes, a banker, and a teacher).


* **Violence and Exclusion:** A 2023 GALZ report cited in the text noted that 65% of LGBTQ+ individuals experienced familial rejection, 40% faced workplace discrimination, and 30% endured physical violence.


* **Harassment and Blackmail:** Gay men in commercial sex work reported physical abuse and an inability to seek police help due to criminalization under Section 73 of the Criminal Law Code. Participants also faced extortion and blackmail threats regarding their sexual orientation, with one case detailing a demand for US$300.


* **Coping and Support:** To mitigate minority stress, participants utilized concealment, selective disclosure to trusted friends, and safe spaces provided by organizations like GALZ, PAKASIPITI, and TRANS_SMART.



## Conclusions

The authors conclude that LGBTQ+ individuals in Zimbabwe experience severe structural violence and systemic oppression resulting from the intersection of colonial-era laws, political homophobia, and conservative religious norms. They recommend comprehensive societal sensitization across schools, churches, and media; the establishment of decentralized mental health facilities; legal reform allowing transgender and intersex individuals to update gender markers on identity documents; and public health provisions for gender-affirming care.
