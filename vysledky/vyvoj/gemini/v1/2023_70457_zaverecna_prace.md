**Goal:** To extend the current description of acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE) by investigating factors affecting its reliable determination and expanding its meaning toward a more comprehensive, robust qualitative assessment of wood and wood-based composite materials intended for musical instrument construction.

**Methods:** The study utilized multiple solid wood species (such as Norway spruce, European beech, and ash) and engineered wood products (plywood) prepared as specially orthotropic specimens across varying dimensions. Experimental evaluation integrated the **Frequency Resonance Technique (FRT)**, **Dynamic Mechanical Analysis (DMA)**, **three-point static and cyclic bending tests**, and **three-point impact bending tests under dynamic loading** monitored via high-speed cameras and digital image correlation (DIC). Material modifications included fungal degradation using *Kretzschmaria deusta* and controlled thermal modification in superheated steam at $180^\circ\text{C}$ and $220^\circ\text{C}$. Additionally, **Numerical Modal Analysis** via ANSYS (Block-Lanczos solver) was conducted to predict modal shapes and frequencies.

**Main results:** Fungal degradation reduced the median of $MO_{ED_{BLT}}$ by $65\%$ and $MO_{ED_{BLR}}$ by $61\%$, while increasing the damping coefficient ($\tan\delta$) by over $288\%$ for $\tan\delta_{BLT}$ and $279\%$ for $\tan\delta_{BLR}$. Across thermal modification, dynamic bending moduli ($MOE_{dyn}$) increased slightly following low-temperature treatment ($180^\circ\text{C}$) but decreased significantly at $220^\circ\text{C}$. A strong linear correlation ($R^2 = 0.84$) was established between $MOE_{dyn}$ determined via FRT and DMA, although median $MOE_{DMA}$ values were systematically lower by $17.6\%$ to $36.9\%$ depending on the species and moisture content group. Furthermore, linear regression linking impact bending strength (IBS) to $MOE_{LT}$ yielded coefficients of determination ranging from $R^2 = 0.11$ to $R^2 = 0.68$ depending on the species and treatment level.

**Conclusions:** Non-destructive FRT effectively captures material changes and supports timber health and quality grading, though non-destructive stiffness testing cannot reliably predict strength-related parameters like impact strength alone. Aggregated multi-modal descriptors ($ACE_{combi}$ and $RACE_{combi}$) successfully integrate longitudinal, bending, and torsional vibration data to provide a comprehensive evaluation of material suitability for musical instruments.
