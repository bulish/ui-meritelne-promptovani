## Goal

This study explores and identifies the potential positive impacts of banking sector development on innovation activities across European Union (EU) countries.

## Methods

The research utilized balanced panel data covering 24 out of 27 EU countries (excluding Cyprus, Ireland, and Slovenia due to data shortages) over the 2010–2020 period. To capture the time lag of banking effects, banking sector data spanned from 2009 to 2019. The analysis employed pooled Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), Fixed Effects, Random Effects, and the two-step system GMM models.

## Main results

The two-step system GMM estimations confirm a statistically significant, positive relationship between banking sector development indicators and the number of patent applications ($lpa$). Specifically, a 1% increase in **Deposit money banks assets to GDP** leads to a 0.98% expected increase in patent applications. Similarly, a 1% increase in **Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP** yields a 0.54% increase, **Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP** results in a 0.51% increase, and **Bank credit to bank deposits** drives a 0.12% increase. Among control variables, **GDP per capita** and **gross school enrollment ratio in tertiary education** showed significant positive coefficients across models, whereas **R&D expenditure** unexpectedly exhibited a negative coefficient in certain specifications.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that a well-functioning, developed banking system and broader financial markets play a crucial role in providing necessary resources and support to foster national innovation activities. It is recommended that EU policymakers prioritize increasing specific financial ratios—ranked from highest to lowest impact: *Deposit money banks assets to GDP*, *Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP*, *Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP*, and *Bank credit to bank deposits*—to create an environment more conducive to innovation.
