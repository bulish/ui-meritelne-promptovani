**Goal:** To compare income inequality across European Union (EU) member states between 2007 and 2023, evaluate how multiple measures capture variations in income distribution, and highlight regional differences within and across countries over time.

**Methods:** The study utilizes disposable income share data by deciles from Eurostat (2024) covering the uniform period from 2007 to 2023 for all 27 EU member states, supplemented by the Croatian Bureau of Statistics for Croatia prior to 2010. The overall analytical approach applies the Gini coefficient, Atkinson Index (with aversion parameters $\varepsilon = 0.5, 1.0, 2.0$), and Generalized Entropy (GE) indices (with sensitivity parameters $\alpha = 0.0, 1.0, 2.0$) calculated using R's "ineq" library.

**Main Results:** Income inequality trends varied significantly across the EU. Romania achieved the largest Gini reduction, dropping from $0.375$ in 2007 to $0.305$ in 2023, driven by lower-income deciles gaining income shares while the top decile lost $6.3$ percentage points. Poland's Gini fell from $0.315$ to $0.265$, with its GE(2.0) dropping by $-32.9\%$, indicating reduced wealth concentration at the top. Conversely, Sweden experienced a Gini increase from $0.229$ to $0.286$, alongside a $65.7\%$ surge in GE(2.0), driven by decreasing income shares among low-income deciles. Bulgaria and Lithuania consistently exhibited high inequality, with Bulgaria's Atkinson index rising from $0.106$ ($\varepsilon=0.5$) to $0.351$ ($\varepsilon=2.0$).

**Conclusions:** The authors conclude that while overall EU average inequality remained relatively stable, this masks divergent national trajectories. They recommend employing complementary measures—such as the Gini, Atkinson, and GE indices—to capture distribution extremes, and advocate for tailored policies involving strong redistributive mechanisms, educational investments, and inclusive labor markets to foster social cohesion.
