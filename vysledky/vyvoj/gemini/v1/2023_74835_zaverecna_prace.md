* **Goal:** To explore smartphone and digital technology usage patterns, primary application preferences, and their role among internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by the conflict in northern Ethiopia.


* **Methods:** A cross-sectional study was conducted using a chain-referral sampling technique to survey 300 adult IDPs aged 18 to 65 across Addis Ababa and various towns in the Amhara region (including Bahir Dar, Debre Birhan, Dessie, and Woldia) between November 2022 and 2024, employing structured questionnaires analyzed via descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and machine learning models (Decision Tree and Random Forest) in Python.


* **Main results:** Among the 300 participants, 51.33% were female, 54% had no formal education, and 84% became jobless post-displacement. Smartphone usage increased for 68.33% of respondents post-migration. Primary reasons for phone use included making regular calls and SMS (98.67%), internet access (71.67%), taking pictures (64.67%), music (64%), and video recording (57%). A total of 72% used social media, with Facebook (64.33%) and Telegram (55.33%) being the most popular platforms, primarily utilized to connect with friends and family (208 participants) and obtain information (204 participants). Furthermore, 57.14% used mobile phones for money transactions, and 45.71% combined in-person banking with mobile banking. Major technology hurdles included internet connection problems (57.67% strongly agreeing) and power shortages for charging (23.67% strongly agreeing). The predictive models achieved an overall accuracy score of approximately 88% to 89%.


* **Conclusions:** Smartphones act as vital lifelines for IDPs, supporting communication, financial inclusion, and information access during crises. The authors recommend that policymakers and humanitarian organizations implement targeted digital literacy initiatives, improve internet and electricity infrastructure, and facilitate affordable smartphone access to better support displaced populations.
