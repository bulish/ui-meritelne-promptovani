## Goal

This study assesses the changes in the Czech retail labor market associated with technological advancements of the fourth industrial revolution.

## Methods

The quantitative research design utilized a structured descriptive survey administered via an online questionnaire to a random sample of 399 retail workers in Brno and its surroundings. Statistical analyses were conducted using Microsoft Excel, Gretl, and SPSS software.

## Main Results

Among the respondents, 82.95% were female, and 84.71% held full-time employment contracts. A significant positive correlation was established between the implementation of Industry 4.0 and its perceived impacts, with $r(397) = 0.91$, $p < .001$. Furthermore, 71.6% of employees agreed that new technologies altered their workload, and 82.9% expected significant changes in how their jobs are performed. Additionally, 85.7% of respondents agreed that the fourth industrial revolution would lead to fewer jobs in the retail sector, while 69.2% agreed that it poses a threat to their job security. Moreover, 72.7% indicated that new skills will be needed.

## Conclusions

The fourth industrial revolution significantly transforms the retail labor environment, creating widespread concern over job security and labor reduction. To navigate these changes, the author recommends that retail employees acquire soft skills, that the government modify educational models to incorporate digital training, and that integrated stakeholder engagement be established to support occupational mobility and manage labor market disruptions.
