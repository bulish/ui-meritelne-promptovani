Here is the summary of the provided scientific article:

* **Goal:** To conceptually define digital sovereignty from the perspective of the European value system, operationalise it through a qualitative component model and secondary data, assess states quantitatively via a composite comparative index (DSI), and propose actionable recommendations to strengthen European digital sovereignty.


* **Methods:** The study utilizes a mixed-methods research design comprising etymological analysis, historical synthesis, structured expert interviews with five participants, and a quantitative index-building framework based on 30 secondary data parameters collected across approximately 200 countries. The quantitative analysis incorporates linear regression, missing data replacement (kNN and mean imputation), normalisation, factor and AHP weighting techniques, and extensive sensitivity and validation analyses.


* **Main results:** The empirical analysis establishes a 30-parameter dataset grouped into three sub-indices: state sovereignty, focus on key enabling technologies (KETs), and technological sovereignty. The findings reveal that European states trail dominant global players like the United States and China in digital performance, venture capital investments, and KET patents (such as AI, quantum computing, and blockchain). For example, the US and China dominate in technology start-up investments and patent generation, whereas EU states rely heavily on external technologies while exhibiting strong performance in data protection compliance and institutional governance.


* **Conclusions:** The authors conclude that European digital sovereignty cannot be achieved through isolationist autarky like that of autocratic states, but rather requires maintaining strategic autonomy and effective negotiating power. Practical implications include recommendations for policymakers and industry leaders to foster a unified European technology ecosystem, invest heavily in KETs, streamline administrative burdens, and strengthen digital skills training without abandoning core democratic and privacy values.
