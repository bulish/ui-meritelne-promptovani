## Summary

* **Goal:** This study evaluates the role of forest biomass exploitation for bioenergy as a climate change mitigation option by analyzing the concept of carbon neutrality, assessing the environmental impacts of a specific regional bioenergy production scenario, and reviewing economic valuation methods for ecosystem services.


* **Methods:** The research combines scoping literature reviews to identify carbon neutrality concepts and monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services, and a standard gate-to-grave Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) using OpenLCA software, the Ecoinvent 3.4 database, and ReCiPe 2016 (H) midpoint methodology based on primary questionnaire data from the Northwest region of Russia.


* **Main Results:** The evaluation of 1 MJ of domestic heat generation from wood pellets produced from sawmill residues showed global warming emissions of $0.015\text{ kg CO}_2\text{ eq}$. Across impact categories, pellet combustion and waste disposal was the most impactful stage for freshwater eutrophication ($5.85\times 10^{-6}\text{ kg P eq}$), terrestrial acidification ($8.29\times 10^{-5}\text{ kg SO}_2\text{ eq}$), fine particulate matter formation ($7.35\times 10^{-5}\text{ kg PM}_{2.5}\text{ eq}$), and ozone formation ($0.00012\text{ kg NO}_x\text{ eq}$ for both human health and terrestrial ecosystems). Conversely, pellet production contributed the most to global warming, marine eutrophication ($1.42\times 10^{-6}\text{ kg N eq}$), and fossil resource scarcity ($0.0045\text{ kg oil eq}$), while transportation had the least overall impact.


* **Conclusions:** Carbon neutrality is not an inherent property of biomass but depends on context, system boundaries, and methodological choices. To ensure sustainable climate change mitigation, bioenergy systems must be rigorously evaluated via LCA and paired with standardized economic and environmental frameworks rather than relying on generic carbon-neutral labels.
