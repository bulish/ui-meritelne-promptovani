1. The 150–200 word summary, organized under the headings Goal, Methods, Main results, and Conclusions.

* **Goal**: To investigate whether systematic patterns suggesting publication bias exist in published estimates of cybercrime-related costs, focusing on whether certain study types or smaller sample sizes report higher figures.


* **Methods**: A simplified meta-analytical approach was utilized, compiling a dataset of 20 publicly available studies from academic, governmental, and industry sources. Data was standardized into U.S. dollars using World Bank exchange rates, and a visual funnel plot was constructed mapping reported cost estimates against a bias proxy defined as the inverse of sample size ($1/n$).


* **Main results**: The empirical results reveal an asymmetric distribution, with smaller studies and industry-sponsored reports systematically concentrating at higher cost estimates, whereas academic and governmental studies present more conservative and methodologically transparent figures.


* **Conclusions**: The findings suggest the presence of publication bias and selective visibility driven by commercial or reputational incentives. Consequently, stakeholders should approach high-impact cybercrime statistics with critical scrutiny, prioritizing methodological transparency and independent research.
