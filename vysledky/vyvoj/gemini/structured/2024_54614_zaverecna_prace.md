1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: The research investigated the surface characteristics, density profiles, wettability, and color stability of European beech (*Fagus sylvatica* L.) subjected to one-sided surface charring under varied time–temperature regimes ($200\text{--}400\text{,}^{\circ}\text{C}$), as well as evaluated alternative chemical treatments and historical paint formulations for wood preservation.


* **Methods**: Radial and tangential beech specimens were charred using a contact heating system. Analyses included optical microscopy for color and roughness parameters ($L^*, a^*, b^*, \Delta E^*, S_a, S_z$), X-ray densitography for density profiles, water uptake tests according to standard procedures, and fire resistance evaluations. Additional studies examined oak wood discoloration using ammonia gas and iron nanoparticles, alongside a six-year Central European outdoor weathering test of linseed oil paints blended with plant resins.


* **Main results**: One-sided charring significantly reduced lightness ($L^*$) by approximately $50\%$ in radial and $42\%$ in tangential beech specimens, while decreasing surface roughness in radial samples. Mild charring affected the density profile and total carbohydrate content up to a depth of $2\text{ mm}$, whereas more severe regimes extended up to $4\text{--}6\text{ mm}$. A statistically significant decrease in water uptake (up to $56\%$) was recorded exclusively for radial groups. Furthermore, both ammonia and iron nanoparticle treatments caused distinct oak wood darkening strongly correlated with extractive content, and opaque linseed oil coatings exhibited superior weathering durability compared to lazure variants over six years.


* **Conclusions**: One-sided surface charring at optimal parameters improves the color stability, fire performance, and decay resistance of beech wood, although annual ring orientation and anatomical anisotropy heavily influence water uptake and surface cracking. Traditional opaque linseed oil systems and selective chemical modifications demonstrate strong potential for protecting exterior wood surfaces.
