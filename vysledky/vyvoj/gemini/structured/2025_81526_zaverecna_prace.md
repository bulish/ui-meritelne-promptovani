1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: This study investigated the dependency of participants in development projects in Zambia on foreign development assistance, exploring their underlying motivations, preferred forms of support, and the strategies they employ to secure external resources.


* **Methods**: The research adopted a qualitative design grounded in an ethnographic approach, utilizing 27 semi-structured interviews, 4 focus group discussions, participant observation, and document analysis with a total of 37 informants across multiple regions (Mongu, Kashitu, and Livingstone) in Zambia.


* **Main results**: Informants were primarily motivated by pragmatic economic and social expectations, such as increasing income, achieving individual or family self-improvement, and coping with climate change impacts. Rather than unrestricted financial aid, participants predominantly sought practical, tangible support like agricultural equipment, market access, and capacity building. To attract support, local actors strategically framed their requests using development terminology and narratives of vulnerability to align with donor expectations.


* **Conclusions**: Local actors in Zambia engage with external assistance not merely as passive recipients, but as strategic agents negotiating a system shaped by external priorities and local realities. Overcoming assistance dependency requires shifting away from short-term fixes toward equitable, long-term partnerships that foster true local autonomy and ownership.
