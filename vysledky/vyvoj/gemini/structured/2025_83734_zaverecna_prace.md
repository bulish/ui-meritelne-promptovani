1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: This study investigated how university students in South Korea and the Czech Republic perceive and experience work-study-life balance (WSLB), how they allocate time across various activities, and how these factors relate to their stress levels and overall well-being.


* **Methods**: A quantitative comparative design utilized an online questionnaire distributed to students at Pukyong National University (PKNU) in South Korea and Mendel University in the Czech Republic. Following data cleaning, the analytical sample comprised 141 employed Korean students and 132 employed Czech students. Statistical analyses were performed in IBM SPSS Statistics, applying independent-samples t-tests, Spearman correlations, multiple regressions, and chi-square tests.


* **Main results**: Korean students reported significantly higher study hours ($M = 6.60$ hours/day) compared to Czech students ($M = 5.62$ hours/day; $p < 0.001$, $d = 0.540$). Conversely, Czech students spent significantly more time on work/internships ($M = 4.25$ vs. $3.28$ hours/day; $p < 0.001$, $d = -0.571$) and household duties ($M = 1.73$ vs. $1.17$ hours/day; $p < 0.001$, $d = -0.675$). Czech students experienced significantly higher health-related strain ($p = 0.002$, $d = 0.40$), relationship interference ($p = 0.006$, $d = 0.36$), emotional exhaustion ($M = 3.55$ vs. $3.19$; $p = 0.013$, $d = 0.30$), and work-related stress ($M = 3.32$ vs. $2.45$; $p < 0.001$, $d = 0.893$). Meanwhile, Korean students exhibited significantly higher academic stress ($M = 4.11$ vs. $3.86$; $p = 0.006$, $d = -0.336$), anxiety/overload ($M = 3.89$ vs. $3.57$; $p = 0.020$, $d = -0.284$), and stress regarding social expectations and future uncertainty. Mental health service utilization also differed significantly ($\chi^2 = 40.300$, $p < 0.001$), with Czech students showing greater interest in seeking counseling. Overall, Czech students reported significantly higher satisfaction with their WSLB ($M = 2.89$ vs. $2.55$; $p = 0.018$, $d = 0.28$).


* **Conclusions**: University students in South Korea and the Czech Republic navigate distinct structural, cultural, and lifestyle challenges. Korean students face intense academic, societal, and future-oriented pressures, whereas Czech students encounter heavier burdens from combining employment with studies, leading to greater health strain and emotional exhaustion. Overall student well-being is not determined by a single factor, but emerges from complex interactions among academic structures, employment conditions, cultural norms, and individual coping capacities.
