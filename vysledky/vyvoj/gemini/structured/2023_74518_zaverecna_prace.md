1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: This research aims to explore and identify the potential positive impacts of banking sector evolution and development on innovation activities within 24 European Union (EU) countries (excluding Cyprus, Ireland, and Slovenia due to data shortages) over the period from 2010 to 2020.


* **Methods**: The study utilizes a panel dataset collected from World Bank open data. The baseline regression model examines the dependent variable, the logarithmic number of patent applications ($lpa$), against control variables—GDP per capita ($lgdp$), R&D expenditure as a percent of GDP ($rd$), tertiary education enrollment ratio ($ledu$), and governance quality ($gov$). Four banking development indicators, lagged by 1 year, are individually added to the models: Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP ($lbank$), Deposit money banks assets to GDP ($lasset$), Bank credit to bank deposits ($ldeposit$), and Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP ($lcredit$). The two-step system GMM method is employed to address potential endogeneity, heteroskedasticity, and autocorrelation.


* **Main Results**: Across all estimated models utilizing the two-step system GMM, the coefficients for banking sector development variables exhibit positive values and are highly significant. Specifically, a 1% increase in "Deposit money banks assets to GDP" leads to an expected 0.98% increase in patent applications, while increases in "Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP", "Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP", and "Bank credit to bank deposits" yield expected increases of 0.54%, 0.51%, and 0.12%, respectively.


* **Conclusions**: A well-developed banking system and comprehensive financial markets play a critical, positive role in supporting and fostering innovation activities within EU countries. The author recommends that EU policies prioritize enhancing these specific banking and credit ratios to create a more conducive environment for innovation.
