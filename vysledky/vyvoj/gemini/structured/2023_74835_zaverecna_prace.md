1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: This study aims to explore the patterns of mobile device and application usage among internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the Tigray and Amhara regions of Ethiopia, as well as to identify their reasons for smartphone usage and preferred mobile applications.


* **Methods**: The author conducted one-to-one interviews with 300 respondents aged 18–65 across Addis Ababa and conflict-free towns in the Amhara region (such as Woldia, Debre Birhan, Dessie, and Bahir Dar) using a chain referral sampling technique. Data analysis and predictive modeling (using Decision Tree and Random Forest algorithms) were implemented via Python and Google Colaboratory.


* **Main results**: Out of 300 participants, 216 (72%) had social media accounts, with Facebook (193 instances) and Telegram (166 instances) being the most preferred platforms. Primary reasons for smartphone usage included making regular calls and SMS (296 instances) and accessing the internet (215 instances). Furthermore, 160 IDPs (57.14%) used mobile phones to send or receive money. Both Decision Tree and Random Forest models achieved an accuracy score of 0.88 for predicting smartphone usage reasons.


* **Conclusions**: Mobile phones function as essential tools for communication, financial inclusion, and information access among IDPs. Demographic factors like income stability and duration of stay significantly influence mobile and social media usage trends, highlighting the need for targeted digital policies and support programs.
