1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: This study evaluates food security and nutritional conditions in Ghana by examining current trends across core dimensions, analyzing key systemic factors, assessing government policies, and measuring the nation's progress toward United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 1, 2, and 3 by 2030.


* **Methods**: The research utilizes secondary data sources, including national surveys like the Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS), reports from international organizations such as the FAO and WHO, data from Ghana's Ministry of Food and Agriculture, and relevant academic literature. The analysis employs descriptive statistics and trend analysis.


* **Main Results**: Based on the 2022 Global Food Security Index (GFSI), Ghana ranks 82nd out of 113 countries with an overall score of 52.6. Affordability remains the highest-scoring pillar at 59.9, though average food costs deteriorated significantly by 31.5 points since 2012. Food availability improved with farm infrastructure increasing by 48.2 points, but food loss scored 0.0 (ranking last globally). Quality and safety suffered from a drop in nutritional standards to 29.8 points due to the absence of a national nutrition plan, while sustainability and adaptation scored lowest at 45.1. Geographically, food insecurity affects 46.7% of northern populations compared to 13.8% in the south. Furthermore, 66% of young children suffer from anemia, and stunting affects 17.5% of children.


* **Conclusions**: Although Ghana has advanced in agricultural production stability and poverty reduction initiatives, it faces severe challenges including escalating food costs, widespread post-harvest food losses, poor water management, and a double burden of malnutrition. Consequently, Ghana remains off-track to fully achieve SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030 without enhanced infrastructure investments and structured national nutrition policies.
