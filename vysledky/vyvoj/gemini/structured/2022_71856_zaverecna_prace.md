1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: This study assesses the role of taxation as a catalyst for economic growth in Ghana, specifically examining the association between taxation and economic advancement, the linkage between capital expenditure and development, and the connection between revenue expenditure and economic expansion.


* **Methods**: The research utilized annual time-series data from 1990 to 2022 sourced from the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA), the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, and the World Development Indicators (WDI). Methodologies included the Augmented Dickey-Fuller unit root test, Johansen cointegration test, Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), and Granger causality test.


* **Main results**: The Johansen cointegration test revealed a long-term relationship where inflation rate and revenue expenditure exert a negative impact on Ghana's economic growth, whereas capital expenditure and total tax revenue exhibit a positive influence. The Granger causality test established a unidirectional short-term relationship showing that total tax revenue Granger-causes real GDP, whereas inflation, capital expenditure, and revenue expenditure do not Granger-cause GDP in the short run.


* **Conclusions**: The analysis concludes that taxes and economic growth in Ghana share a statistically significant positive causal link both short- and long-term. Consequently, the government should intensify revenue mobilization, broaden the tax base, and prioritize growth-fostering capital expenditures while managing the public sector wage bill.
