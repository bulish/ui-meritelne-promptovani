### 1. Summary

#### Goal

The study assesses the changes in the Czech retail labor market driven by technological advancements associated with the fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0), aiming to answer research questions regarding job impacts, industrial evolution extent, and why the retail sector should embrace these changes.

#### Methods

The research utilized a quantitative study design employing a structured descriptive survey via an online questionnaire distributed to a random sample of 399 retail workers in Brno and its surrounding areas. Statistical software tools, including Microsoft Excel, Gretl, and SPSS, were used to analyze demographic data, descriptive statistics, Cronbach's alpha reliability, and bivariate correlation using Pearson's r.

#### Main Results

Data analysis revealed a high positive correlation between Industry 4.0 implementation and its perceived impacts, $r(397) = 0.91$, $p < .001$. While 84% of respondents reported receiving management training before technological shifts, 71.6% noted changes in their workload. Furthermore, 85.7% believed new technologies reduce retail jobs, and 69.2% felt their job security was threatened.

#### Conclusions

The fourth industrial revolution significantly alters retail labor structures, posing threats to job security and requiring workers to acquire new skills. Proactive measures, such as management support, updated education models, and integrated stakeholder engagement, are crucial to help employees adapt and minimize disruptive effects.
