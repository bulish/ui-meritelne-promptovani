This study investigated the personal experiences, challenges, and coping mechanisms of LGBTQ+ community members living in Zimbabwe.

**Goal:** The research aimed to explore how LGBTQ+ individuals navigate daily life under systemic oppression, examine the impact of societal norms, cultural beliefs, and legal frameworks, and identify support systems and coping mechanisms.

**Methods:** A qualitative research design was utilized, incorporating in-depth, semi-structured interviews conducted via WhatsApp phone calls and Microsoft Teams with a total of $32$ participants aged between $20$ and $45$ years. Participants—comprising lesbians, gays, transgender, and intersex persons—were recruited using snowball sampling via local organizations like GALZ and PAKASIPITI. Data was analyzed using a critical interpretive approach combining thematic analysis, narrative inquiry, and a decolonizing framework.

**Main Results:** Participants experienced widespread societal stigma, workplace discrimination, family disownment, and extortion. Legal frameworks, specifically Section 73 of the Criminal Law Codification and Reform Act and Section 78(3) of the Constitution of Zimbabwe, criminalize same-sex relations and ban same-sex marriage, creating a climate of fear and vulnerability. Transgender individuals faced severe documentation barriers that mismatch their gender identity, hindering travel and employment. To cope, individuals utilized concealment, selective disclosure, digital support networks, and safe spaces provided by organizations such as GALZ.

**Conclusions:** LGBTQ+ individuals in Zimbabwe endure systemic oppression rooted in colonial laws, political rhetoric, and conservative religious and cultural norms. Despite these hostile environments, community-led initiatives and covert activism demonstrate profound resilience in forging dignity and survival.
