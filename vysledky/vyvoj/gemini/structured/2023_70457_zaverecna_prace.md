### Goal

This study aimed to investigate the factors influencing the dynamic response of materials used for musical instruments (primarily wood and wood-based composites) and to account for these factors when applying evaluation methods to describe their mechanical and vibro-acoustic properties. The research sought to extend material assessment toward modified properties, alternative materials, and a more refined selection of high-quality materials during early manufacturing stages.

### Methods

The research framework utilized the frequency resonance technique (FRT) across specimens with varying dimensions, moisture contents (MC), and material modifications (fungal degradation by *Kretzschmaria deusta* and thermal modification). Comparative evaluations were conducted using dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA), static three-point bending tests, and three-point impact bending tests combined with digital image correlation (DIC). Furthermore, numerical modal analysis was performed using ANSYS Mechanical APDL, and multimodal testing integrated longitudinal, bending, and torsional vibration modes.

### Main Results

Specimen slenderness and geometry significantly affected the determination of vibro-acoustic properties, where closely spaced modes risked overlapping or interchanging. For moisture content, increases above the fiber saturation point (FSP) did not significantly alter $MOE_{dyn}$ or $\tan\delta$ under FRT, whereas DMA exhibited continuous increases in $\tan\delta$. Fungal degradation drastically reduced the median of $MOE_{BLT}$ by 65% and increased $\tan\delta_{BLT}$ by over 288% in European beech. Thermal modification increased acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE) at higher temperatures, but non-destructive testing (NDT) bending stiffness showed weak linear correlations with impact bending strength (IBS), yielding coefficients of determination ($R^2$) generally below 0.5. Aggregated parameters ($ACE_{combi}$ and $RACE_{combi}$) effectively integrated multi-modal findings to differentiate material groups.

### Conclusions

Vibro-acoustic parameters are highly sensitive to specimen geometry, mode shape, and order, meaning damping coefficients and ACE values cannot be freely interchanged across different modes. While FRT provides robust non-destructive measurements for dynamic elastic moduli and allows for powerful conversion relationships with DMA ($R^2 = 0.84$), NDT alone cannot reliably predict complex dynamic fracture behaviors like maximum deflection or impact strength without considering failure modes and loading rates. Integrating multi-modal frequency spectra via aggregated descriptors provides a comprehensive tool for advanced material quality assessment.
