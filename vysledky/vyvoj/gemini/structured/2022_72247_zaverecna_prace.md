**Goal**
This study aimed to examine Israel’s legal obligations under international law regarding Palestinian minors in the West Bank and contrast them with the practical realities these juveniles face within the Israeli military court system.

**Methods**
The research utilized a case study approach combining qualitative and quantitative data. It reviewed multiple international legal texts and treaties alongside sworn testimonies, affidavits, and statistical reports primarily gathered by non-governmental organizations like Defense for Children International - Palestine (DCI-P), UNICEF, and various human rights groups.

**Main results**
The findings reveal a stark divergence between international legal obligations and actual practice. An estimated 500 to 700 Palestinian minors encounter the military judicial system annually. Evidence demonstrates widespread systemic ill-treatment, including frequent night arrests, tight physical restraints, blindfolding, physical and verbal abuse, and prolonged interrogation without legal counsel or parental presence. These conditions heavily drive coerced confessions, yielding conviction rates approaching 99%.

**Conclusions**
Recent military order amendments and the establishment of a military juvenile court have failed to deliver meaningful protection or curb institutionalized abuses. Consequently, systematic violations of international law persist, causing severe, long-lasting psychological trauma and educational disruptions for Palestinian children.
