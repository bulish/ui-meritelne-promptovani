**Goal**
This study maps the role of student movements in Thailand's pro-democracy protests from 2020 to 2021. It examines how youth activists mobilized, articulated demands, and challenged existing political structures, while placing these movements within Thailand’s broader political system and public discourse.

**Methods**
The research uses a qualitative interpretative case study approach guided by Social Movement Theory (SMT). It relies on data sources including academic literature, reports from international and Thai media outlets, Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) documents, and human rights watchdogs, which are systematically categorized under the SMT concepts of resource mobilization, political opportunity structures, and framing processes.

**Main results**
Student movements relied heavily on decentralized networks, digital platforms (such as Twitter, Facebook, and Telegram), and creative cultural references to coordinate protests and build solidarity. Protests were initially triggered by the dissolution of the Future Forward Party (FFP) in early 2020 and were fueled by broader economic and political grievances. The government responded largely through repressive measures, including legal prosecutions under Article 112 (lèse-majesté), emergency decrees, and police crackdowns. Despite state suppression, the movement expanded public discourse by breaking long-standing taboos to openly criticize the monarchy and military-backed government, ultimately influencing the 2023 general election where the reform-oriented Move Forward Party (MFP) won the most seats.

**Conclusions**
Student movements acted as crucial drivers of mobilization and agenda-setting, successfully shifting public and electoral discourse in Thailand. However, their immediate institutional impact remained limited due to the persistent structural constraints of Thailand's semi-authoritarian political system.
