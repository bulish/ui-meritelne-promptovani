### Goal

The goal of this diploma thesis was to investigate existing forest machine operator qualification standards across Europe, and to develop, establish, and pilot-test harmonized European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) occupational standards and qualifications for harvesters, forwarders, and skidders aligned with the European Qualifications Framework (EQF).

### Methods

The author combined a desk-top literature review (analyzing over 50 documents) with field research, which included 30 stakeholder meetings, focus groups, and interviews. A qualitative mapping and data analysis methodology was applied in three stages (content analysis, comparative analysis, and thematic/interpretive analysis) across data sets from the United Kingdom, the Czech Republic, and Slovenia. Under the umbrella of ABA International and supported by an Erasmus+ grant, standard-setting events and 14 practical pilot tests were conducted primarily in Ireland involving 65 successful candidate certifications.

### Main results

The study identified an increasing EU-wide demand for skilled operators coupled with high accident and fatality rates, alongside significant variability in national qualifications. Through mapping and harmonization, common structures were established, resulting in new Pan-European competence levels and assessment tools (records of assessment) rated at EQF level 4. Pilot testing validated the assessment criteria, leading to the successful live testing and market introduction of 65 certified operators in Ireland.

### Conclusions

The hypothesis that quality standards and independent assessments can be unified into an international framework was proven correct. The newly established EFMO qualifications successfully enhance operator safety, reduce barriers to transnational labor mobility, and improve the global competitiveness of the European forestry workforce.

* **Methods:** "In total, over 50 documents were reviewed and 30 stakeholder meetings were held (not including numerous local meetings) with the support of the Erasmus + EFMO project."


* **Methods:** "The qualitative methodology applied here consisted of three stages: Stage 1-was undertaken as a content analysis... Stage 2-required a comparative analysis... Stage 3-involved a combination of thematic (patterns) and interpretive (meaning) analysis..."

* **Conclusions:** "I fully endorse and recommend the new EFMO qualifications for use in operational forestry practice for the following key reasons: Due to high standards and rigorous independent assessment, they can help to improve forestry safety standards and safety culture..."
