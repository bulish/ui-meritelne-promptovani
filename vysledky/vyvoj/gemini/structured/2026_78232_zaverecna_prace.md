1. **Goal**: The overarching goal of the study is to evaluate the environmental impacts of residential heat generation from wood pellets derived from sawmill residues in the Arkhangelsk area (Northwest region of Russia), specifically addressing the regional research gap in bioenergy life cycle assessments.



**Methods**: The study applies a standard Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) methodology complying with ISO 14040 and 14044 standards. Using OpenLCA version 2.1.1 and the Ecoinvent 3.4 database, a gate-to-grave framework was modeled with system boundaries encompassing sawmill residue collection, pellet production, packed pellet distribution (20 km transport by a light commercial vehicle), and thermal energy conversion (25-kW pellet furnace) alongside waste disposal. The ReCiPe 2016 (H) Midpoint v. 1.1 method was used for Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) across eight impact categories, supported by three sensitivity analyses.

**Main results**: Producing 1 MJ of thermal energy resulted in global warming emissions of $0.015~kg~CO_2$ eq. The pellet combustion and waste disposal stage contributed most significantly to five impact categories (freshwater eutrophication, terrestrial acidification, fine particulate matter formation, and both ozone formation categories), whereas the pellet production stage dominated global warming ($0.015~kg~CO_2$ eq), marine eutrophication ($1.42e-6~kg~N$ eq), and fossil resource scarcity ($0.0045~kg~oil$ eq). Transportation had the lowest impact, never exceeding 17% in any category. Sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness: utilizing natural gas for drying increased global warming and fossil resource scarcity emissions, while extending transport distance to 100 km caused a 50% increase in those same categories.

**Conclusions**: Residential heat production from sawmill-residue wood pellets in Northwest Russia exhibits a favorable environmental profile and low impact, positioning it as a viable climate change mitigation alternative. Supply chain choices—particularly regarding drying energy sources and transport distances—substantially alter overall environmental performance.
