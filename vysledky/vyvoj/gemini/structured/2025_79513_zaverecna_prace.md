### 1. Summary

#### Goal

This thesis aims to evaluate and compare the real-world operational performance, efficiency, and key performance indicators of two distinct photovoltaic (PV) power plants (Power Plant A and Power Plant B) located in close geographic proximity in the Czech Republic, using data collected over a two-year monitoring period (2023 and 2024).

#### Methods

The methodology aligns with the ČSN EN IEC 61724-1 ed. 2 standard. Retrospective operational datasets were gathered via SolarEdge monitoring platforms. Key performance indicators—such as Specific Yield, Performance Ratio (PR), Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF), self-consumption, energy export, and grid dependency—were calculated alongside spatial parameters (e.g., panel area per kWp and specific surface area per kWh). Real-world metrics were further compared against theoretical PVGIS simulation outputs.

#### Main results

Power Plant A (39.8 kWp capacity, $25^\circ$ tilt) achieved a high self-consumption rate of $74.84\%$, a low grid dependency of $41.35\%$, and a stable performance ratio of $0.615$ (2023) to $0.622$ (2024). Power Plant B (139.44 kWp capacity, $15^\circ$ tilt) served an industrial facility with higher energy demand, showing an increase in its performance ratio from $0.656$ in 2023 to $0.770$ in 2024, and a capacity utilization factor rising from $9.57\%$ to $11.23\%$. However, Power Plant B experienced recurring weekend overproduction and localized autumn drops in efficiency (e.g., October 2024).

#### Conclusions

Both power plants operate efficiently within their respective structural constraints. Power Plant A requires only minor software-level adjustments and adaptive load-shifting to manage spring energy surpluses. Conversely, Power Plant B would benefit from hardware and operational upgrades—including a medium-sized battery energy storage system (BESS), soiling sensors, and anti-soiling maintenance routines—to mitigate weekend overproduction and seasonal efficiency losses.
