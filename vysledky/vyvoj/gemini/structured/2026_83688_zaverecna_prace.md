1. **Summary**

* **Goal**: To examine the possible impact of early retirement and retirement age on the size of the shadow economy across seven Central European countries (Austria, the Czech Republic, Germany, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia, and Slovenia) between 2010 and 2024, assess associated tax losses, and measure overall shadow economy levels.


* **Methods**: The study applies a quantitative empirical design using secondary macroeconomic data and structural equation modeling (SEM) via the MIMIC (Multiple Indicators Multiple Causes) framework in Stata 19. The latent variable for the shadow economy ($SE$) is analyzed using causal factors—including GDP per capita, self-employment, unemployment, government effectiveness, personal income tax (PIT), corporate income tax (CIT), social security contributions (SSC), net migration, and men's and women's retirement ages ($RA\_m, RA\_w$)—alongside observable indicators like GDP growth and labor force participation.


* **Main results**: In most analyzed countries (Austria, Czechia, Germany, Poland, Slovakia, and Slovenia), retirement age did not show a statistically significant impact on the shadow economy. An exception occurred in Hungary, where the retirement age of women demonstrated a statistically significant negative effect ($coeff. = -5.273322, P>\vert{}z\vert{} = 0.000$), linked to the "Women 40" program. Across the region, fiscal factors such as social security contributions and personal income tax frequently emerged as significant determinants.


* **Conclusions**: Retirement age is generally not a primary independent driver of the shadow economy, meaning pension reforms alone are insufficient to alter informal economic activity without addressing broader national institutional designs. Instead, country-specific tax burdens, social protection systems, and regulatory frameworks play a central role in shaping informal market dynamics.
