1. Summary
Goal: To investigate the challenges and discrimination experienced by LGBTQ individuals in Zimbabwe, examine how societal, cultural and legal factors shape their lives, and identify coping mechanisms, support systems and resilience factors.
Methods: The study used a qualitative design based on 18 in-depth interviews with LGBTQ community members aged 18–35 years. Participants were recruited through LGBTQ organizations and referrals. Interviews were conducted mainly via WhatsApp, with some through Microsoft Teams, and analysed using thematic analysis and narrative inquiry.     2026_83485_zaverecna_prace
Main results: Participants described pervasive stigma and discrimination across communities, workplaces, religious settings and interactions with authorities. Workplace discrimination included job loss, fear of termination and concealment of sexual orientation. Religious experiences commonly involved rejection, humiliation, condemnation and pressure to conceal identity. Participants also reported fear of arrest, police abuse, family rejection, homelessness, blackmail, loss of livelihood and mental-health difficulties. Concealment of sexuality was described as the most prevalent coping strategy, while selective disclosure to trusted friends and LGBTQ community organizations provided support, safety and belonging. Despite systemic oppression, participants demonstrated resilience through social networks, private digital spaces and community-led support.
Conclusions: The authors conclude that legal repression, cultural stigma and socio-religious norms jointly marginalize LGBTQ people in Zimbabwe. They recommend greater societal sensitization, expanded queer-focused mental-health services, stronger protections for transgender and intersex people, and policies promoting inclusion and acceptance.     2026_83485_zaverecna_prace
