**Goal**  
The study examines whether banking sector development affects innovation activities in EU countries during 2010–2020, focusing on the relationship between banking/financial development and patent applications.

**Methods**  
The research uses balanced panel data for 24 of 27 EU countries from the World Bank Open Data for 2010–2020. Banking-sector variables are lagged by one year. Innovation is measured by the logarithm of patent applications. The analysis uses dynamic panel-data methods, with two-step system GMM applied to address endogeneity, heteroskedasticity, and autocorrelation.

**Main results**  
Across Models 2–5, all four banking-development indicators have positive and highly significant coefficients. A 1% increase in Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP is associated with a 0.54% increase in patent applications; a 1% increase in Deposit money banks assets to GDP with a 0.98% increase; a 1% increase in Bank credit to bank deposits with a 0.12% increase; and a 1% increase in Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP with a 0.51% increase. The reported coefficients are significant at the 1% level.

**Conclusions**  
The authors conclude that banking-sector and financial-market development support innovation in EU countries. They recommend prioritizing increases in the four banking ratios, ranked by estimated positive impact: Deposit money banks assets to GDP, Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP, Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP, and Bank credit to bank deposits.
