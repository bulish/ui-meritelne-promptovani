Summary
Goal:
The study compares income inequality across all 27 EU member states from 2007 to 2023 using three complementary measures, examining changes over time and differences across income groups.     2024_78494_zaverecna_prace
Methods:
The analysis uses Eurostat’s 2024 “Distribution of income by quantiles” data, focusing on national equivalised income shares across deciles. The uniform period is 2007–2023. Inequality was calculated with the Gini coefficient, Atkinson Index (ε = 0.5, 1 and 2), and Generalized Entropy indices (α = 0, 1 and 2), using the “ineq” library in R.     2024_78494_zaverecna_prace     2024_78494_zaverecna_prace
Main results:
Inequality changed substantially across countries despite minimal change in the EU average. Romania’s Gini fell from 0.375 in 2007 to 0.305 in 2023, while Poland’s fell from 0.315 to 0.265; Slovakia reached 0.215. Conversely, Sweden’s Gini rose from 0.229 to 0.286 and Malta’s from 0.258 to 0.321. Romania’s lowest decile gained 0.2 percentage points while its top decile lost approximately -6.3; Malta’s top decile gained +5.3 percentage points.     2024_78494_zaverecna_prace     2024_78494_zaverecna_prace
Conclusions:
The authors conclude that multiple measures are necessary because Atkinson and GE reveal distributional differences overlooked by Gini. They recommend cohesive EU-wide strategies, stronger redistributive mechanisms, investment in education, and inclusive labour markets.     2024_78494_zaverecna_prace
