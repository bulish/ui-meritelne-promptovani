**Goal:**  
To advance understanding of forest biomass exploitation for bioenergy as a climate-change mitigation option by examining carbon neutrality (CN), environmental impacts, and monetary valuation of ecosystem services (ES).

**Methods:**  
The thesis combines four studies: scoping reviews of CN and ES valuation, a life cycle assessment (LCA) of residential heat production from sawmill-residue wood pellets in Northwest Russia, and a qualitative synthesis. The CN review covered literature published from 1990 to July 2020 and analyzed 39 studies; the ES review covered literature from 2010 to March 2021 and included 16 studies. The LCA used primary data from the Arkhangelsk area and assessed 1 MJ of heat using ReCiPe 2016 (H) Midpoint v. 1.1.

**Main results:**  
No commonly accepted CN definition exists; eight distinct CN concepts were identified, with CN depending on feedstock origin, temporal and spatial boundaries, and scenario assumptions. For 1 MJ of heat, global warming was **0.015 kg CO2 eq**, while freshwater eutrophication was **5.85e-6 kg P eq** and fossil resource scarcity **0.0045 kg oil eq**. Combustion and waste disposal dominated five of eight impact categories, while transportation never exceeded 17% of any category. Increasing transport from 20 km to 100 km increased global warming and fossil resource scarcity by 50%. The ES review identified over 20 monetary valuation approaches and substantial terminology inconsistencies.

**Conclusions:**  
Forest biomass can contribute to climate-change mitigation, but its sustainability is context-specific and requires rigorous LCA. Supply-chain optimization and standardized ES valuation terminology are recommended for more integrated environmental and economic assessment.
