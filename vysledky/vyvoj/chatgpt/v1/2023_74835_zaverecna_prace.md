Summary
Goal:
To examine smartphone use among internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by the northern Ethiopian war, focusing on reasons for smartphone use, commonly used applications, usage patterns, and predictive modelling.
Methods:
The study interviewed 300 respondents, aged 18–65, who had migrated from northern Ethiopia because of conflict. Data were collected beginning in November 2022 in Addis Ababa and selected towns in Amhara using chain-referral sampling. The analysis used descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and machine-learning models (decision tree and random forest) on categorical survey data.     2023_74835_zaverecna_prace(1)
Main results:
Smartphone use had increased since displacement for 205 (68.33%) participants. The principal reported uses included calls (296; 98.67%), internet (215; 71.67%), taking pictures (194; 64.67%), music (192; 64%), and video (171; 57%). 216 (72%) participants had social-media accounts. Facebook was the most used application (193; 64.33%), followed by Telegram (166; 55.33%) and WhatsApp (135; 37.3%). Social media was mainly used to meet friends/family (208) and obtain information (204). Stable-income respondents were approximately five times more likely to report increased smartphone use.     2023_74835_zaverecna_prace(1)     2023_74835_zaverecna_prace(1)     2023_74835_zaverecna_prace(1)
Conclusions:
Smartphones were important for communication, information, and entertainment.
