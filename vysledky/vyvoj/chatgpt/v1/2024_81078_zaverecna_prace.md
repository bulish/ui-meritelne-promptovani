**Goal**  
The study aimed to comprehensively assess food security and nutritional status in Ghana, identify major influencing factors, evaluate government interventions, and examine progress toward SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030.

**Methods**  
The study used secondary data from Ghanaian national surveys and government sources, reports from FAO, WHO, and MoFA, and relevant academic research. Descriptive statistics and trend analysis were used to examine food security, nutrition, policy interventions, and changes over time.

**Main results**  
Ghana’s 2022 GFSI overall score was 52.6, ranking 83rd globally and 3rd of 28 Sub-Saharan African countries. Availability improved to 52.4, including a 48.2-point increase in farm infrastructure to 58.6 and scores of 91.8 for volatility of agricultural production and 86.8 for sufficiency of supply. However, food loss scored 0.0, ranking 113th globally. Affordability declined 4.4 points to 59.9, while 49.1% of the population, approximately 15.1 million people, faced food insecurity in the first quarter of 2022. Nutritional standards fell to 29.8, while dietary diversity scored 46.1. Food safety improved to 63.3. Sustainability and adaptation remained weakest at 45.1. Anaemia affected 66% of young children in a 2014 survey.

**Conclusions**  
The authors conclude that Ghana has made uneven progress but remains vulnerable to food-price pressures, nutritional deficiencies, post-harvest losses, and weak climate adaptation. They recommend investment in post-harvest infrastructure, a national nutrition policy, stronger social protection, and climate-smart agriculture.
