Summary
Goal: The study assesses the role of taxation in Ghana’s economic growth and examines the relationships of capital expenditure and revenue expenditure with economic expansion.     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace
Methods: It uses annual time-series data for 1990–2022 from the Ghana Revenue Authority, Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, and World Development Indicators. The analysis applies VAR lag analysis, the Augmented Dickey-Fuller unit root test, Johansen cointegration, Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), and Granger causality tests.     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace
Main results: The Johansen test identified long-run cointegration, with the reported equation showing coefficients of 0.205 for capital expenditure, −0.091 for revenue expenditure, and 0.020 for total tax revenue. Thus, capital expenditure and tax revenue had positive long-run associations with GDP, while revenue expenditure had a negative association. The VECM reported a total-tax-revenue coefficient of 0.079 (p=0.016) and an error-correction coefficient of −0.043 (p=0.023), indicating a roughly 4.3% adjustment speed. Granger causality showed a short-term effect of total tax revenue on GDP (F=3.905, p=0.037), while the other tested expenditure relationships were not significant.     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace
Conclusions: The authors conclude that taxation and economic growth have a statistically significant positive causal link. They recommend expanding the tax base, improving tax administration, prioritizing productive and capital expenditure, and managing the public-sector wage bill.     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace
