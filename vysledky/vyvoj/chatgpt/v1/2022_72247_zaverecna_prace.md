Summary
Goal:
The thesis examines Israel’s international-law obligations toward Palestinian minors in the West Bank and contrasts these obligations with their treatment by Israeli authorities, while assessing recent military-justice reforms.     2022_72247_zaverecna_prace
Methods:
It uses a qualitative and quantitative case-study approach. Legal obligations were examined through relevant international legal texts, while evidence on conditions on the ground was drawn primarily from Defense for Children International–Palestine interviews and sworn testimonies, corroborated with data from Israeli authorities and other organizations. Most empirical material dates from the preceding ten years.     2022_72247_zaverecna_prace
Main results:
The thesis identifies repeated discrepancies between international protections and practice. Among DCI-P testimonies, 60% of minors reported arrest between midnight and 5am; this decreased to 41.7% in 2012–2015 and 38.2% in 2015. During interrogation, 57% reported restraints in 2008–2012, 36.4% in 2012–2015, while physical violence was reported in 37% of earlier cases. Threats affected 52% in 2008–2012, falling to 22.4% in 2012–2015. The thesis characterizes the broader evidence as showing systematic physical and psychological abuse.     2022_72247_zaverecna_prace
Conclusions:
The reforms represent some improvement but remain inadequate because exemptions limit their scope, abuse before detention remains insufficiently addressed, and implementation diverges from formal procedures. The thesis also emphasizes inadequate post-release educational and psychological support.     2022_72247_zaverecna_prace
