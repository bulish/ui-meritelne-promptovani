Goal
The dissertation aims to define digital sovereignty in the European value context, quantify it, develop a country-level Digital Sovereignty Index (DSI), and derive measures for strengthening EU states’ digital sovereignty.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace(1)
Methods
The study combines literature synthesis and structured qualitative interviews with quantitative analysis of publicly available secondary data. A model comprising three sub-indices—state sovereignty, key enabling technologies (KETs), and technological sovereignty—was operationalised through 10 sub-components and 30 parameters. Missing data, normalisation, weighting, aggregation, sensitivity analysis, simulation, and comparison with existing indices were used to construct and validate the DSI.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace(1)
Main results
The DSI ranks the USA first, followed by Ireland and Norway; all EU-27 states are in the top 50. China ranks 31st, largely because high digitalisation scores compensate for lower sovereignty values. A stress test with 1,000 simulations showed the index remained essentially stable under parameter perturbation. In a 150-period simulation, EU-state ranks initially ranged from 2–50 (mean 25.08; standard deviation 13.69); from period 43, the mean converged to 14 (standard deviation 7.94). The DSI showed coefficients of determination of 86% with FSI, 77% with BLI, and 57% with EFI.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace(1)     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace(1)
Conclusions
The authors conclude that digital sovereignty can be defined and quantitatively measured through the DSI, providing a basis for comparing states and evaluating policy measures.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace(1)
