Summary
Goal: To examine how university students in South Korea and the Czech Republic perceive balance among academic demands, work responsibilities, and personal life, and how this balance relates to well-being.
Methods: A quantitative, cross-sectional design used an online questionnaire. The sample comprised 141 Korean students from Pukyong National University and 132 Czech students from Mendel University in Brno. Data were collected in 2025–2026 and analyzed with t-tests, Spearman correlations, multiple regression, and chi-square tests.
Main results: Korean students spent more time studying (6.60 vs. 5.62 hours/day), whereas Czech students spent more time working/interning (4.25 vs. 3.28 hours/day); both groups had a median of 7 hours/day sleep. Korean students reported higher academic stress (M = 4.11 vs. 3.86), social-expectation stress (3.84 vs. 2.95), and future-uncertainty stress (3.48 vs. 2.79), while Czech students reported higher work stress (3.32 vs. 2.45). Czech students had higher emotional exhaustion and perceived inability to cope; Koreans reported anxiety/overload. Mental-health service use differed significantly (χ² = 40.300, df = 3, p < 0.001). Czech students reported higher WSLB satisfaction (M = 2.75 vs. 2.47; p = .018; Cohen’s d = 0.295).
Conclusions: Work–study–life balance and well-being differed by national context, reflecting interacting academic, employment, cultural, and individual factors.
