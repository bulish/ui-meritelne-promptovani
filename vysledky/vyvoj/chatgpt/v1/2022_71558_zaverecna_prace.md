
Summary
Goal: The thesis aimed to investigate forest machine operator qualification standards and develop European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) occupational standards as a basis for European qualifications.
Methods: The study combined literature/secondary research with field research involving operators and trainers. Over 50 documents were reviewed and 30 stakeholder meetings held. National standards from the UK and Ireland, Czech Republic, and Slovenia were mapped and qualitatively analysed using content, comparative, thematic and interpretive approaches. EFMO qualifications were developed and pilot-tested, mainly in Ireland, during 2019–2021.
Main results: National standards varied substantially, with skills and knowledge gaps incorporated into harmonised EFMO standards. Five core competency areas were identified for harvester operations; forwarder and forestry tractor/skidder analyses showed similar patterns. Three Pan-European qualification levels were developed and rated at EQF level 4. Fourteen pilot tests were undertaken; parameters were a minimum of four tests with the last two assessment durations consistent. By March 2022, ABA had certified 65 machine operators in Ireland; certification was independently assessed, graded A+ to F, and valid for five years. Two of three qualifications had been live-tested and were in market use.
Conclusions: The authors conclude EFMO provides enhanced national standards and recommend its use to improve safety, qualification recognition, labour mobility, and forestry competitiveness.
