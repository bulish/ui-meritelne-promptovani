Summary
Goal: To investigate factors affecting the dynamic response of materials for musical instruments, primarily wood and wood-based composites, and improve the reliable assessment of their mechanical and vibro-acoustic properties.     2023_70457_zaverecna_prace
Methods: The thesis compiled five experimental studies using solid wood and wood-based composites representing European species and plywood. The studies examined different specimen dimensions and material states using the Frequency Resonance Technique (FRT), complemented by numerical modal analysis and static, cyclic, and impact bending tests; results were compared across measurement approaches.     2023_70457_zaverecna_prace
Main results: Specimen geometry, particularly slenderness and cross-sectional shape, affected vibro-acoustic parameters and could cause mode misidentification. Fungal degradation reduced ACE by approximately −74%, while thermal modification increased ACE with treatment temperature, with species-dependent significance. FRT and DMA yielded strongly correlated MOEdyn values (R² = 0.84) but tan δ could not be reliably converted between methods. Non-destructive bending stiffness was not a reliable general predictor of strength. ACEcombi and RACEcombi differentiated tested materials; their correlations were R² = 0.91 for microphone data and R² = 0.84 for vibrometer data.     2023_70457_zaverecna_prace
Conclusions: Reliable material evaluation requires accounting for specimen geometry, vibration mode, sensor, and measurement method. ACEcombi offers a more robust representation, whereas RACEcombi provides finer material differentiation.     2023_70457_zaverecna_prace
