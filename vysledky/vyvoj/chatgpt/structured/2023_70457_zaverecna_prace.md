1. Summary (181 words)
Goal: The work investigates factors influencing the dynamic response of materials for musical instruments, primarily wood and wood-based composites, and aims to improve assessment of their mechanical and vibro-acoustic properties across different specimen dimensions, materials, and material states.
Methods: The studies combined the Frequency Resonance Technique (FRT), Dynamic Mechanical Analysis (DMA), numerical modal analysis, and static, cyclic, and impact three-point bending tests. Specimens included European wood species and plywood. FRT measurements used free–free boundary conditions and microphone, accelerometer, or laser Doppler vibrometer sensing; dynamic moduli, damping, ACE, and RACE were derived from measured vibration modes. Material modifications included fungal degradation and thermal treatment.
Main results: Specimen geometry significantly influenced vibro-acoustic parameters, with specimen slenderness > 48 producing negligible differences between MOEdyn from first bending and longitudinal modes. Fungal degradation caused an approximately 74% reduction in ACE, while thermal modification increased ACE in a species-dependent manner. MOEdyn from DMA and FRT showed a strong linear correlation (R² = 0.84), whereas tan δ could not be reliably converted between methods. The aggregated ACEcombi and RACEcombi differentiated tested materials.
Conclusions: Bending modes were the most stable. ACEcombi provided more robust representation, while RACEcombi offered greater material discrimination.
