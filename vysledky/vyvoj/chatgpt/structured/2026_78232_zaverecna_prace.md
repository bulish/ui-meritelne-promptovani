1. Summary
Goal: The thesis aimed to advance understanding of forest biomass exploitation for bioenergy as an option for climate change (CC) mitigation, focusing on carbon neutrality (CN), environmental impacts, and monetary evaluation of ecosystem services (ES).
Methods: The thesis combined four studies using different approaches: scoping reviews for CN and ES valuation, a standard Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for residential heat production, and a qualitative synthesis for the short communication. The CN review analyzed 39 articles selected from 5699 initially identified publications. The LCA followed ISO 14040 and 14044 guidelines and assessed heat production from wood pellets made from sawmill residues in Northwest Russia.
Main results: No universally accepted CN definition was identified; eight distinct CN concepts were distinguished, and CN status depended on biomass origin, system boundaries, and scenario assumptions. The LCA demonstrated low environmental impact, while supply-chain decisions such as transportation distance and drying methods significantly affected performance. The ES review identified over 20 monetary valuation methods.
Conclusions: Forest biomass can contribute to CC mitigation, but CN is not an inherent property and sustainability depends on rigorous assessment. The thesis recommends LCA and greater standardization of ES valuation methods to support integrated environmental and economic assessment.
