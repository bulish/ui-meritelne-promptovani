1. Summary
Goal: The study investigates how technological advancements associated with the fourth industrial revolution affect the retail labour market in Brno, Czechia, focusing on jobs, future industry development, and the need to embrace technological change. Methods: A quantitative research design used a structured descriptive survey of retail workers. Primary data were collected through a Google online questionnaire. Using Slovin’s formula with N = 577,026 and e = 0.05, the required sample was 399.723, rounded to 400 retail workers; 399 valid responses were ultimately analysed. Random sampling was used, and Excel and SPSS supported data analysis. Reliability analysis produced Cronbach’s alpha values of 0.74 for implementation and 0.76 for impact perception. Pearson’s correlation showed a significant positive relationship between implementation and impact, r (397) = 0.91, p < .001. Main results: 71.6% reported changes in workload, 82.9% expected substantial operational changes, 66.9% expected fewer tasks, and 85.7% believed Industry 4.0 would reduce retail jobs. 72% reported needing new skills. Conclusions: Industry 4.0 is substantially reshaping retail labour; the thesis recommends soft-skills development, modified education models, and integrated stakeholder engagement to mitigate disruptive effects.
