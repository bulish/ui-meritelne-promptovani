Goal: The dissertation aims to define digital sovereignty from a European value perspective, develop a quantitative model and comparative Digital Sovereignty Index (DSI), classify states, and identify fields of action for strengthening EU digital sovereignty.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace
Methods: A qualitative definition was derived through historical and literature analysis and refined through structured interviews with five experts. The resulting model comprises three sub-indices—state sovereignty, KETs, and technological sovereignty—with 10 sub-components and 30 parameters based on secondary data. Data were analysed using regression, normalisation, weighting and aggregation procedures; missing values were addressed through imputation. The model and DSI were subjected to verification, validation, sensitivity analysis and a stochastic stress test.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace
Main results: The DSI can be calculated and used to rank states. The model shows significant correlations with the BLI, ESI, FSI, DDI and SDGI, and withstands stochastic parameter perturbation. The top 50 are predominantly Western-model democracies, led by the USA, Ireland and Norway; all EU-27 states rank within the top 50.     2024_72596_zaverecna_prace
