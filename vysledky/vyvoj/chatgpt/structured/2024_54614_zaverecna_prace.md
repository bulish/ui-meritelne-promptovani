1. Summary
Goal: The dissertation investigates historical and traditional wood-preservation methods used before industrial chemical preservatives, combining a literature-based evaluation with experimental studies of charring, ammonia treatment, iron nanoparticles, and historical oil coatings. The experimental objectives were to assess surface properties, density profile, wettability, colour stability, and weathering durability.
Methods: One-sided beech specimens were surface-charred using controlled time-temperature combinations of 200–400 °C; density profiles were measured by X-ray densitography, wettability by contact-angle measurements, water uptake according to EN-927-5, and decay resistance using fungal tests. Oak heartwood was treated with ammonia gas or iron nanoparticles and subjected to accelerated ageing in dry and wet modes according to ASTM G 155. Historical coatings containing chromium oxide green, linseed oil, and dammar, mastic, or copal were exposed outdoors for 6 years and assessed using colour, gloss, water contact angle, and ATR-FTIR measurements.
Main results: Charring modified surface properties and reduced fungal decay-related weight loss. Ammonia and iron treatments caused significant colour changes, with extractive content correlated with colour change. Opaque historical coatings showed substantially greater colour stability than lazure coatings.
Conclusions: The experiments demonstrate that selected historical techniques can modify wood properties and provide potentially useful preservation effects, although performance depends strongly on treatment conditions and application.
