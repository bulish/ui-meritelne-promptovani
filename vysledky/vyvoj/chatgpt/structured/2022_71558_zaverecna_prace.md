
1. Summary (184 words)
Goal: The thesis aimed to investigate existing forest machine operator qualification standards and develop European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) occupational standards as a basis for European qualifications for harvesters, forwarders and tractors/skidders.
Methods: The study combined a literature review and primary field research, including interviews, meetings and focus groups. In total, over 50 documents were reviewed and 30 stakeholder meetings were held. National standards from the United Kingdom, Czech Republic and Slovenia were mapped using qualitative content, comparative, thematic and interpretive analysis. Learning outcomes were developed from performance criteria and underpinning knowledge and aligned with the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). The resulting standards were refined through expert review, standard setting and field pilot testing.
Main results: The analysis identified substantial variation between national standards and gaps in skills and knowledge. Common competence structures were established and harmonised EFMO standards were developed for three machine types. Fourteen pilot tests were undertaken, followed by live testing; as of March 2022, ABA had certified 65 successful machine operators in Ireland. The certification was independently assessed, graded from A+ to F (fail), and valid for five years.
Conclusions: The thesis concludes that the EFMO qualifications were successfully developed, implemented and placed on the market, achieving the stated objectives and supporting improved safety, qualification recognition and transnational labour mobility.
