1. Summary
Goal: The study investigates smartphone use among internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by the northern Ethiopian war, focusing on reasons for smartphone use, commonly used applications, usage patterns, and prediction of smartphone-use reasons and preferred applications.
Methods: Data were collected through one-to-one interviews with 300 respondents aged 18–65 in Addis Ababa and selected locations in the Amhara region. Interviews were conducted in Amharic and Tigrigna using chain referral sampling. The analysis used descriptive statistics, percentages, correlation analysis, chi-square feature selection, oversampling, and machine-learning models. Decision tree and Random forest models were implemented for prediction.
Main results: The main smartphone uses were making calls and sending SMS (296 instances), accessing the internet (215), taking pictures (194), and listening to music (192). The most commonly used applications were Facebook 193 (64.33%), Telegram 166 (55.33%), and WhatsApp 135 (37.3%); 216 (72%) participants had social media accounts. For predicting smartphone-use reasons, both models yielded the same result; the Random forest achieved 88 % of accuracy score, while the decision tree achieved 88% accuracy score. For application prediction, the decision tree achieved 89 % accuracy score.
Conclusions: Smartphone use is important for communication, information, entertainment, and social interaction. Duration of stay and income source influenced usage patterns, with those having stable income approximately five times more likely to experience increased smartphone usage.
