1. Summary
Goal: The thesis examines how university students in South Korea and the Czech Republic perceive and experience the balance between academic demands, work responsibilities, and personal life, and how this balance relates to overall well-being. Methods: A quantitative, comparative cross-sectional design used an online questionnaire covering demographics, time allocation, work-study-life balance, well-being and stress, cultural/social perceptions, and life satisfaction. Data were analyzed using independent-samples t-tests, Spearman correlations, multiple regression analysis, and chi-square tests. Main results: Korean students spent significantly more time studying, while Czech students spent significantly more time working and on household duties; sleep duration did not differ significantly. Despite these differences, perceived time availability and ability to rest were similar. Czech students reported stronger health and relationship impacts, greater work-related strain, emotional exhaustion, and coping difficulties, whereas Korean students reported higher academic, social, and future-related stress. Korean students also reported stronger societal and family pressure concerning academic success and performance. Overall WSLB satisfaction was significantly higher among Czech students (M = 2.68 vs. M = 2.48; p = .018). Conclusions: Well-being reflected the interaction of academic structures, employment conditions, cultural norms, and individual coping capacities rather than a single factor.     2025_83734_zaverecna_prace
