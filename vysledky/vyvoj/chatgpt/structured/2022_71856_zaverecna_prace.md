1. Summary
Goal: The thesis examines the role of taxation in Ghana’s economic growth, specifically assessing associations among total tax revenue, capital expenditure, revenue expenditure, inflation, and GDP, while investigating their causal relationships. Methods: It uses annual time-series data for 1990–2022 from the Ghana Revenue Authority, Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, and World Development Indicators. The analysis applies the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test, Johansen cointegration test, Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), VAR lag analysis, and Granger causality test. Main results: All variables had unit roots at levels but became stationary after first differencing. Johansen testing identified cointegration up to Rank 3, with the long-run equation GDP = -0.122(inflation)+0.205(capital expenditure)-0.091(revenue expenditure) +0.020(total tax revenue)-163. In the GDP VECM, total tax revenue was significant (0.079; p=0.016) and the error correction term was -0.043 (p=0.023), indicating approximately 4.3% adjustment toward equilibrium. Granger causality showed total tax revenue Granger-caused GDP (F=3.905; p=0.037). Conclusion: Tax revenue has a positive long-run influence and short-term predictive relationship with Ghanaian economic growth, while revenue expenditure has a negative long-run association and capital expenditure a positive long-run association.
