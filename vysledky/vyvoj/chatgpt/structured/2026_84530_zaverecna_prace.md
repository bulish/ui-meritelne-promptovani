1. Summary (188 words)
Goal: The thesis examines the role of student movements in Thailand’s 2020–2021 pro-democracy protests, focusing on how they mobilized, formulated demands, interacted with the political environment, and influenced political discourse and the 2023 general election. Methods: It uses a qualitative, interpretative case study guided by Social Movement Theory (SMT), applying three analytical perspectives: resource mobilization, political opportunity structures, and framing. The analysis systematically examines secondary sources, including academic literature, media reports, NGO documents, and human-rights watchdog materials. Main results: Student movements mobilized through digital platforms including Facebook, Twitter, Telegram, and Line, decentralized coordination, educational institutions, diverse participant skills, small-scale donations, crowdfunding, and transnational networks such as the Milk Tea Alliance. Diagnostic, prognostic, and motivational framing connected demands to democracy, equality, and generational justice, while expanding debate over the monarchy. Government responses relied primarily on legal and repressive measures, including arrests, emergency powers, restrictions on assembly, surveillance, and force. The movement significantly influenced political discourse and electoral behavior; in 2023, the Move Forward Party won 151 out of 500 seats, while voter participation exceeded 75%. Conclusion: Student movements achieved substantial discursive and mobilizational effects but limited immediate institutional change; their demands nevertheless continued within electoral politics and reshaped political debate.
