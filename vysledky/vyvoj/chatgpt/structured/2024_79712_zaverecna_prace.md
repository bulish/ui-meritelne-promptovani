1. Summary (184 words)
Goal: The thesis aimed to develop a user-friendly text annotation application that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to support collaboration, improve annotation quality, and reduce manual workload in Named Entity Recognition (NER) and sentiment analysis. Methods: The system was developed through requirements gathering, UI design, system design, technology and model selection, implementation, and evaluation. It integrated GPT-4, BERT, and TinyBERT, with semi-automated annotation, manual refinement, fine-tuning, role-based collaboration, comments, and dataset import/export. Evaluation compared model outputs with gold-standard datasets using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. For sentiment analysis, 300 samples were used, with 50% labelled as positive and 50% as negative. Main results: Testing found that automation reduced average annotation time; GPT-4 excelled in zero-shot sentiment analysis, while TinyBERT provided precise token-level suggestions for structured NER datasets. In one NER document, TinyBERT achieved 100% accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-Score, compared with GPT-4’s 88.89% accuracy, 50% precision, 50% recall, and 50% F1-Score. Conclusions: The thesis concludes that the system can reduce manual labour while supporting high-quality annotation through an intuitive, customizable, collaborative, and semi-automated workflow, although GPT-4 hallucinations and computational constraints remain limitations.
