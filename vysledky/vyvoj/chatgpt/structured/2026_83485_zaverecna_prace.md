1. Summary (187 words)
Goal: The study investigated the challenges and discrimination faced by LGBTQ individuals in Zimbabwe, examined how societal norms, cultural beliefs, and legal frameworks shape their lived experiences, and identified coping mechanisms, support systems, and resilience factors contributing to well-being and empowerment.  
Methods: A qualitative study collected data through in-depth, semi-structured interviews conducted primarily via WhatsApp voice calls and, for some participants, Microsoft Teams. The study included 24 participants aged between 18 and 28 years, representing diverse sexual-orientation and gender-identity groups. Interviews lasted approximately 18–60 minutes. Data were analyzed using a critical interpretive approach combining thematic analysis and narrative inquiry.  
Main results: Participants described persistent stigma and discrimination in workplaces, families, religious settings, and wider communities. Reported consequences included family disownment, social exclusion, blackmail and extortion, violence and abuse, employment and educational losses, and fear of legal repercussions. Transgender participants additionally experienced difficulties arising from documentation that did not correspond with their gender identity. Concealing sexuality was identified as the most prevalent coping strategy, although it imposed psychological costs. LGBTQ centres and trusted social networks provided important safe spaces and support.  
Conclusions: The study concludes that legal, cultural, religious, and social factors jointly sustain marginalization, while community support and adaptive strategies foster resilience.
