Summary
This study examines whether banking-sector development supports innovation in EU countries during 2010–2020. Using balanced panel data for 24 EU countries, the author measures innovation by the logarithm of patent applications and banking development through four indicators: private credit by deposit money banks to GDP, deposit money bank assets to GDP, bank credit to deposits, and private credit supplied by banks and other financial institutions. Control variables include GDP per capita, R&D expenditure, tertiary education, and governance quality. The analysis initially applies OLS and diagnostic tests, then uses two-step system GMM to address endogeneity and heteroskedasticity. Across Models 2–5, all four banking-development indicators have positive and statistically significant coefficients, supporting the hypothesis that banking-sector development is positively associated with innovation. The strongest estimated effect is associated with deposit money bank assets to GDP, followed by private bank credit to GDP, private credit from banks and other financial institutions, and bank credit to deposits. The study concludes that a well-functioning banking and broader financial system can provide resources that facilitate innovation, while acknowledging data limitations and recommending further research incorporating other financial markets.
