The thesis investigates the operational performance of two photovoltaic (PV) power plants by comparing their energy production, consumption, efficiency, and dependence on the electricity grid. The methodology evaluates key performance indicators, including total PV generation, on-site consumption, energy exports, self-sufficiency, grid dependency, and performance ratio, using operational data and solar-irradiation information.

The results show strong seasonal variation in PV production. Output is lowest in winter because of reduced solar irradiation and shorter daylight, while spring and summer provide the highest generation. Power Plant A is substantially more self-sufficient: it produced 86.38 MWh and consumed 74.84% of its generation on-site, whereas the larger Power Plant B generated 254.06 MWh and used 77.68% on-site. Nevertheless, Plant B's much higher industrial demand resulted in greater overall grid dependence.

The study concludes that both systems operate close to their design constraints. Plant A requires mainly minor software improvements, while Plant B offers greater opportunities for optimization through mechanical adjustments, environmental monitoring, cleaning, and operational planning.
