Summary

This study compares work–study–life balance among university students in South Korea and the Czech Republic, focusing on time allocation, well-being, stress, cultural expectations, and life satisfaction. Using an online questionnaire, the study analyzed 141 Korean and 132 Czech students who combined university study with employment or internships.

The findings show clear differences in how students allocate their time. Korean students spent more time studying, while Czech students devoted more time to paid work, personal activities, and household duties; sleep duration was similar. Despite these differences, both groups reported comparable perceptions of available time and ability to rest. Czech students experienced greater health and relationship impacts from study–work demands, as well as more work-related stress, emotional exhaustion, and coping difficulties. Korean students reported higher academic, social-expectation, and future-related stress and perceived stronger family and societal pressure concerning achievement.

Overall, Czech students were more satisfied with their work–study–life balance, although both groups identified areas needing improvement.
