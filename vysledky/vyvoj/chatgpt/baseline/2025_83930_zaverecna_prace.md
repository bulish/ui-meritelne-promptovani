The thesis examines how Czech foreign policy toward the Srebrenica genocide evolved from 1995 to 2025, using a qualitative case study, document analysis, comparative analysis with EU policy, fieldwork, and expert interviews. It identifies a shift from cautious and limited engagement toward a more consistent, principled policy centered on genocide recognition, international justice, remembrance, and human rights. A major turning point came in 2005, when the Czech Ministry of Foreign Affairs officially recognized Srebrenica as genocide, preceding the EU’s formal recognition in 2009. Czech policy subsequently strengthened through support for international criminal justice, participation in commemorations, educational initiatives, and backing for the 2024 UN General Assembly resolution on Srebrenica.

The thesis attributes this evolution to domestic political leadership, historical memory, and Czechia’s participation in the UN, NATO, and EU. Overall, Czech policy increasingly converged with EU positions, although the EU maintains more institutionalized mechanisms for education and genocide prevention. Domestic political changes have nevertheless produced occasional inconsistencies.
