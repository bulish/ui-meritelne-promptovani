The thesis examines Vietnam’s wood export industry, with particular attention to opportunities and barriers in the European market. It finds that Vietnam’s wood sector has expanded substantially, supported by international trade agreements, growing global demand, relatively competitive labor costs, and increasing processing capabilities. The European Union is identified as an important market with considerable unmet demand for Vietnamese wood products, while the EVFTA offers improved tariff access. However, the thesis emphasizes that market expansion is constrained by strict EU requirements concerning product quality, legal timber, traceability, environmental protection, packaging, and rules of origin. Vietnamese enterprises also face limited raw-material supplies, dependence on imports, small-scale production, insufficient certification, and weaknesses in human resources, technology, marketing, and international business capabilities. The study therefore argues that export growth requires coordinated improvements rather than relying solely on tariff advantages. Proposed measures include developing sustainable domestic plantations and higher-quality local resources, improving certification and quality-management systems, upgrading technology and workforce skills, strengthening cooperation among firms, expanding export promotion and e-commerce, and adapting production to EVFTA origin requirements. The thesis ultimately recommends stronger government and industry coordination and greater integration of Vietnamese producers into European and global supply networks.
