The thesis examines how internally displaced people (IDPs) affected by the war in northern Ethiopia use smartphones and digital technologies. Based on interviews with 300 respondents aged 18–65 in Addis Ababa and several towns in the Amhara region, the study finds that mobile phones are important mainly for communication, internet access, photography, and entertainment. Calls and SMS were the most frequent use, followed by internet access. Most respondents considered mobile devices helpful, and many reported that phones assisted their movement to their current locations.     2023_74835_zaverecna_prace
Social media was also widely adopted: 72% of participants used at least one studied platform, with Facebook and Telegram the most popular, followed by WhatsApp. Respondents mainly used social media to communicate with family and friends and obtain information. Mobile banking was another important application. However, poor internet connectivity, limited electricity for charging, and device costs constrained digital access. Smartphone use generally increased after displacement, although longer stays and lack of income were associated with lower increases.     2023_74835_zaverecna_prace
