The study analyzes Ghana’s food security and nutritional status using secondary data and descriptive/trend analysis across food availability, affordability, quality and safety, sustainability, and adaptation. It finds a mixed picture: food availability improved modestly, supported by greater agricultural production stability, but affordability deteriorated as food prices rose, the currency depreciated, and social safety nets weakened. Nutritional quality remains problematic, with poor dietary diversity and protein quality and the absence of a national nutrition policy. Sustainability and adaptation constitute the weakest dimension because of inadequate water management, limited irrigation, weak disaster preparedness, and insufficient climate-smart agricultural capacity. The study also identifies a double burden of malnutrition: undernutrition, including stunting and wasting, coexists with obesity and diet-related non-communicable diseases, while micronutrient deficiencies remain particularly serious in northern and rural areas. Government interventions such as Planting for Food and Jobs, the School Feeding Programme, One Village One Dam, and NAFCO have produced some gains but face implementation, financing, infrastructure, and regional-equity problems. Overall, Ghana is making progress but remains off track for several SDG 1, 2, and 3 targets.     2024_81078_zaverecna_prace
