Summary
The thesis examines how taxation and government expenditure relate to economic growth in Ghana using annual time-series data from 1990–2022. It models real GDP growth against total tax revenue, capital expenditure, revenue expenditure, and inflation, drawing data from the Ghana Revenue Authority, Ministry of Finance, and World Development Indicators. The analysis combines Augmented Dickey-Fuller tests, VAR lag selection, Johansen cointegration, a Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), and Granger causality tests.     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace
The results indicate a long-term relationship among the variables. Johansen cointegration results associate tax revenue and capital expenditure positively with economic growth, while inflation and revenue expenditure are negatively associated with GDP. The VECM estimates a 4.3% annual adjustment toward long-run equilibrium and finds lagged changes in total tax revenue significantly related to GDP in the short run. Granger causality identifies a unidirectional relationship between total tax revenue and economic growth. Capital and revenue expenditure show no significant short-run causal relationship with GDP. The thesis therefore concludes that taxation has a statistically significant positive causal link with Ghanaian economic growth and recommends stronger domestic revenue mobilization, greater emphasis on productive capital spending, and tighter management of public-sector wages.     2022_71856_zaverecna_prace
