Using Eurostat income-distribution data organized into deciles, the analysis compares changes in inequality and examines which parts of the income distribution contributed to those changes. Overall, EU-wide inequality remained relatively stable, but this average concealed substantial differences between countries. Romania and Poland recorded some of the largest reductions in inequality, while Slovakia and Slovenia remained among the most equal countries. In contrast, inequality increased notably in Sweden and Malta, with changes particularly associated with greater income concentration at the upper end. Bulgaria and Lithuania consistently showed high inequality across several measures. The comparison also demonstrates that different indices reveal different aspects of inequality: the Gini provides a broad overview, the Atkinson index is more sensitive to lower-income groups, and GE measures can emphasize upper-income disparities and provide additional distributional detail. The study therefore concludes that multiple measures are necessary for a fuller assessment of inequality across the EU.
