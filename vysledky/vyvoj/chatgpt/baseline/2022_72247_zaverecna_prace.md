Summary
The thesis examines Israel’s legal obligations toward Palestinian minors in the West Bank and compares these obligations with their treatment under the Israeli military justice system. Using international legal instruments, interviews, testimonies, and recent reports, it identifies substantial differences between formal protections and conditions experienced in practice. The study finds that Palestinian children face arrest and detention procedures involving night arrests, physical and psychological abuse, limited access to parents and lawyers, prolonged detention, and restrictions on education and family contact. It also highlights the transfer of detainees from the West Bank to prisons inside Israel and argues that several practices conflict with international protections concerning fair treatment, detention, education, and the prohibition of torture. The thesis recognizes reforms, including the creation of a military juvenile court, reduced detention periods, increased recognition of minors’ rights, and requirements concerning parental notification and interrogation procedures. However, it concludes that these reforms have had limited practical impact, particularly because security-related exceptions remain extensive and problems occurring before detention are largely unaddressed. The author therefore identifies a persistent gap between international legal standards, formal procedures, and their implementation on the ground.     2022_72247_zaverecna_prace
