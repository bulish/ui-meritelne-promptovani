The study examines how Fourth Industrial Revolution (Industry 4.0) technologies are changing the retail labour market in Brno, Czechia, using questionnaire responses from retail employees. The findings show substantial technological change: 71.6% of respondents reported changes in their workload, while 82.9% believed technology would significantly alter how retail jobs are performed. Automation has already taken over tasks such as pricing, invoicing, inventory management, ordering, and stock-taking, and 66.9% of respondents expected to have fewer tasks as a result. Job security was a major concern: 85.7% believed Industry 4.0 would reduce the number of retail jobs, and 69% considered the future of retail employment to be at risk. At the same time, 72% said that acquiring new skills would be necessary to adapt. Although most respondents reported receiving training for existing technologies, many remained uncertain about whether they possessed sufficient skills. Only 43% believed technological change would improve work-life balance.
