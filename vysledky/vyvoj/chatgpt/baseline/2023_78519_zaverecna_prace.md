Summary (183 words)
The thesis investigates whether a fuzzy-logic expert system can improve movie recommendations compared with traditional recommender approaches. It reviews collaborative, content-based, and hybrid filtering, emphasizing that conventional systems can struggle with uncertainty and imprecision in user preferences. The study develops a fuzzy-logic recommendation model in which user preferences are represented through linguistic categories and membership functions, processed through fuzzy rules, and converted into recommendation scores. A content-based machine-learning model is also developed using text preprocessing, stemming, bag-of-words vectorization, and cosine similarity. Both approaches are evaluated on a large movie dataset after preprocessing and feature transformation.
The results show that the fuzzy-logic model achieves higher overall accuracy (0.98) than the content-based model (0.94). However, its F1 score (0.02), precision (0.02), and recall (0.03) remain very low. The content-based model also has a low F1 score (0.02), but achieves slightly higher precision (0.01) and recall (0.06). The discussion therefore concludes that accuracy alone favors fuzzy logic, while precision and recall make the content-based model more suitable for recommendations. Nevertheless, the conclusion highlights fuzzy logic's potential for handling uncertainty, adapting to preferences, and improving transparency.
