Goal: This thesis investigates and compares the operational parameters of two industrial photovoltaic power plants—Power Plant A in Sentice and Power Plant B near Čebín—with the aim of evaluating their electricity production, on-site consumption, grid interaction, and operational effectiveness.

Methods: The author applies monitoring indicators and calculation procedures based on ČSN EN IEC 61724. The comparison covers energy production and consumption, electricity drawn from and supplied to the grid, capacity utilization, performance ratio (PR), irradiance, panel area, installed capacity, and module orientation and tilt. Power Plant A comprises 94 panels over 195.552 m² and uses a 25° tilt; Power Plant B comprises 366 panels over 715.53 m² and uses a 15° tilt.

Main results: Average annual solar irradiation was 1313.4 kWh/m² at Plant A and 1277.4 kWh/m² at Plant B, a 2.8% difference. Plant A’s PR reached 0.622 in 2024. Photovoltaic generation was insufficient to meet each facility’s demand throughout the year, especially during lower-irradiance periods; demand patterns and intensive operations increased grid reliance. Plant A covered 25.16% of its electricity consumption with photovoltaic generation.

Conclusions: Continuous monitoring enables performance assessment and operational optimization, but seasonal solar variability and demand exceeding generation constrain self-sufficiency.
