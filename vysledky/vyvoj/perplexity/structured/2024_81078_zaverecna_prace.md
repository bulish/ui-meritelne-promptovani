Goal: This study examines food security and nutritional status in Ghana, identifying food-system constraints, assessing government interventions, and evaluating progress toward SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030. Methods: It uses secondary data from Ghana Statistical Service Demographic and Health Surveys, FAO, WHO, the Ministry of Food and Agriculture, and academic sources, analysed through descriptive statistics and trend analysis. Main results: Ghana’s 2022 Global Food Security Index score was 52.6/100 (83rd of 113 countries; 3rd of 28 in Sub-Saharan Africa). Affordability, its strongest pillar, declined by 4.4 points since 2012; the change in average food costs declined by 31.5 points. Availability rose by 9.4 points, including a 48.2-point increase in farm infrastructure, but food loss scored 0, ranking Ghana 113th. Quality and Safety scored 50.5; nutritional standards fell from 57.1 to 29.8 amid the absence of a national nutrition plan. Sustainability and Adaptation was weakest at 45.1. Conclusions: Ghana has made incremental gains, but rising prices, inadequate post-harvest infrastructure, weak climate resilience, low dietary diversity, and regional inequities constrain progress. The study recommends investments in storage and processing, a national nutrition strategy, expanded social protection, climate-smart agriculture, and targeted northern interventions.
