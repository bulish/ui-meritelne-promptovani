Summary
Goal: This thesis assesses how Fourth Industrial Revolution (Industry 4.0) technologies affect the Czech retail labour market, particularly workers in Brno, and identifies measures to manage these effects. Methods: The author conducted a quantitative online questionnaire survey of retail workers in Brno and surrounding areas. Of 406 responses, 399 were retained for analysis. The questionnaire measured implementation of technological changes and employees’ impact perceptions using five-point Likert scales; analyses used Excel and SPSS. Internal consistency was acceptable (Cronbach’s Alpha: 0.74 for implementation, 0.76 for impact perception). Main results: 71.6% reported technology-related workload change; 81.4% were informed before changes and 84% received prior training. However, only 43.8% believed they had sufficient skills, although 70.5% felt able to adapt. Employees expected substantial disruption: 85.7% anticipated fewer retail jobs, 82.9% expected changes in work methods, 69.2% saw job security as threatened, 72.7% expected to need new skills, and 66.9% anticipated fewer tasks. Reported technologies included self-checkouts, online shopping, electronic price tags, automated invoicing, and inventory systems. Conclusions: Industry 4.0 is reshaping retail work while intensifying concerns about labour substitution; the thesis recommends continuous training, management support, soft-skill development, educational reform, and stakeholder cooperation.
