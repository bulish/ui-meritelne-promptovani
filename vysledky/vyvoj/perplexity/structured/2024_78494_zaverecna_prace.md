Goal: This thesis compares income inequality across the EU-27 from 2007 to 2023 using the Gini coefficient, Lorenz curves, Atkinson index, and Generalized Entropy (GE) indices. It aims to identify national trends, explain changes through decile income shares, and assess how sensitivity parameters alter interpretations of inequality.

Methods: The analysis uses Eurostat’s Distribution of income by quantiles data, specifically shares of national equivalised income by deciles; Croatian data before 2010 are supplemented from the Croatian Bureau of Statistics. Calculations were conducted with the R ineq library. Atkinson indices use ε = 0.5, 1, and 2; GE measures use α = 0, 1, and 2.

Main results: EU-average Gini inequality changed little, from 0.291 in 2007 to 0.288 in 2023, concealing divergent national patterns. Romania’s Gini declined from 0.375 to 0.305 and Poland’s from 0.315 to 0.265, alongside lower-decile gains and top-decile losses. Slovakia reached the lowest 2023 Gini, 0.215. Bulgaria (0.361) and Lithuania (0.348) remained highly unequal, while Malta and Sweden recorded strong increases; Sweden’s GE(2.0) rose by 65.7%.

Conclusions: Multiple measures are necessary: Gini provides an overall view, Atkinson emphasizes lower-income disparities, and GE identifies top-end concentration. Policies should reflect country-specific conditions.
