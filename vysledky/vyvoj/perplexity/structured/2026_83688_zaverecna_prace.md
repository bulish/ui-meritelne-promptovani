Summary
Goal: This study investigates the possible impact of early retirement on the size of the shadow economy in Austria, the Czech Republic, Germany, Hungary, Poland, the Slovak Republic, and Slovenia, while also assessing potential tax losses and the overall shadow-economy level from 2010–2024.

Methods: It uses a quantitative, country-specific MIMIC (Multiple Indicators Multiple Causes) model estimated in Stata 19 with the SEM module. The shadow economy is a latent variable measured through GDP growth and labour-force participation; explanatory variables include GDP per capita, unemployment, self-employment, corporate and personal income tax, social-security contributions, government effectiveness, statutory retirement age for men and women, and net migration. Main results: Retirement age is statistically insignificant in all countries except Hungary, where women’s retirement age has a significant negative effect on the shadow economy. Personal income tax is positively associated with the shadow economy in Czechia, Hungary, and Slovakia. Social-security contributions are significant in all countries except Slovakia and are negative in five of six significant cases.

Conclusions: Pension parameters alone have limited explanatory power; institutional quality, taxation, social-protection links, and formalised self-employment are more important determinants.
