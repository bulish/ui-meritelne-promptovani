Goal: To explore the reasons for smartphone use, the most commonly used mobile applications, and patterns of mobile device and application use among internally displaced people (IDPs) from northern Ethiopia.

Methods: The study conducted one-to-one interviews with 300 respondents aged 18–65 in Addis Ababa and towns in the Amhara region (Bahir Dar, Debre Birhan, Dessie, Debre Sina, Woldia) using chain-referral sampling starting in November 2022. Data were analyzed with descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and machine-learning models (Decision tree, Random forest) implemented in Python.

Main results: Calls and SMS were the dominant smartphone functions (296 instances; 98.67%), followed by internet access (215; 71.67%), taking pictures (194; 64.67%), and music (192; 64%). Mobile usage had increased since displacement for 205 participants (68.33%). Seventy-two percent (216) had at least one social-media account; Facebook (193) and Telegram (166) were the most used platforms. Decision tree and Random forest models achieved approximately 88–89% accuracy in predicting usage reasons and preferred applications.

Conclusions: Smartphones are essential communication and information tools for IDPs, with usage patterns shaped by age, income, and duration of stay. The author recommends larger samples and expanded geographic coverage to improve model generalizability and inform digital-inclusion policies.
