Summary
Goal: This doctoral thesis examines factors affecting the dynamic response and acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE) of wood and wood-based materials for musical instruments, aiming to improve material assessment beyond single-mode measurements.

Methods: Norway spruce, European beech, ash, oak, linden, alder, larch, Norway maple, and pine, birch, and poplar plywood were tested using frequency resonance technique (FRT), dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA), static, cyclic, and impact three-point bending, and numerical modal analysis. Samples varied in dimensions, geometry, moisture content (MC), wood condition, and thermal treatment at 180 °C or 220 °C.

Main results: Specimen geometry, especially slenderness and cross-sectional shape, influenced vibro-acoustic parameters more than specimen size; slender solid wood specimens with slenderness 48 showed negligible differences between MOEdyn from first bending and longitudinal modes. FRT found no significant change in MOEdyn or tan δ above the fibre saturation point, whereas DMA showed increasing tan δ.
