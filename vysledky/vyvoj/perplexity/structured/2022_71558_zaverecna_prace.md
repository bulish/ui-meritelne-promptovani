
Summary (192 words)

Goal: This thesis investigated existing forest machine operator qualification standards and developed and established European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) occupational standards for tractors/skidders, forwarders, and harvesters, aligned with the European Qualification Framework (EQF).

Methods: The study combined a literature review and field research, including interviews, meetings, and focus-group discussions; over 50 documents were reviewed and 30 stakeholder meetings held. National standards from the United Kingdom, Czech Republic, and Slovenia were mapped using content, comparative, thematic, and interpretive analyses. Performance criteria and underpinning knowledge were cross-referenced and reorganised into harmonised learning outcomes. Stakeholders, an EFMO Expert Group, ABA International, and training providers then contributed to standard setting, pilot testing, and qualification development.

Main results: Five common competence areas were identified for each machine type: work planning and preparation, base-machine pre-use inspection and maintenance, safety checks, operations, and post-use inspection. Three EFMO qualifications were developed and rated at EQF level 4. Fourteen pilot tests were conducted, followed by 65 live candidate tests; by March 2022, ABA had certified 65 successful machine operators in Ireland. Harvester and forwarder qualifications were live tested and placed on the market, while the tractor/skidder qualification required further testing.

Conclusions: The objectives were achieved, supporting the use of EFMO qualifications to improve safety, recognition, and worker mobility.
