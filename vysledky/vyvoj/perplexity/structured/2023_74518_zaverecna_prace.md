Goal: This thesis investigates whether banking sector development positively affects innovation activities in 24 of 27 EU countries during 2010–2020; Cyprus, Ireland, and Slovenia were excluded because of substantial data shortages. Innovation is measured as the logarithm of the number of patent applications (lpa).

Methods: The study uses balanced panel data from the World Bank Open Data, with banking indicators lagged by one year (2009–2019). Banking development is represented separately by lbank, lasset, ldeposit, and lcredit; controls are lgdp, rd, ledu, and gov. After pooled OLS and tests for multicollinearity, heteroskedasticity, and autocorrelation, the analysis applies two-step system GMM with lagged lpa, 24 country groups, 240 observations, and 21–23 instruments.

Main results: All four lagged banking indicators are positive and significant at 1%: lbank = 0.54, lasset = 0.98, ldeposit = 0.12, and lcredit = 0.51. Hansen-test probabilities range from 0.20 to 0.48, while Arellano–Bond AR(2) probabilities range from 0.35 to 0.36.

Conclusions: Banking-sector development is positively associated with patenting activity. The thesis recommends prioritizing deposit-money-bank assets to GDP, followed by private bank credit, broader private credit, and bank credit to deposits.
