Goal: To evaluate the environmental context of forest biomass exploitation for bioenergy by analyzing carbon neutrality (CN) concepts, quantifying life-cycle impacts of pellet-based heat, and reviewing monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services (ES).

Methods: The thesis comprises four studies: (1) a scoping review of 39 articles on CN of forest biomass; (2) a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of residential heat production from wood pellets in the Arkhangelsk area, Russia, using OpenLCA 2.1.1, Ecoinvent 3.4, and ReCiPe 2016 (H) Midpoint v. 1.1 across eight impact categories with 1 MJ of heat as the functional unit; (3) a scoping review of ES monetary valuation methods (16 studies); and (4) a short communication synthesizing findings.

Main results: The CN review identified eight distinct concepts (e.g., carbon cycle neutrality, inherent CN, life cycle CN) and concluded CN is not an inherent property of biomass. The LCA showed global warming emissions of 0.015 kg CO₂ eq per 1 MJ of heat; pellet combustion dominated five impact categories, while pellet production most influenced global warming, marine eutrophication, and fossil resource scarcity. Increasing transportation distance from 20 km to 100 km raised global warming and fossil resource scarcity by 50%. The ES review identified over 20 valuation approaches lacking consistent terminology.

Conclusions: Forest biomass bioenergy may support climate mitigation only when CN status, life-cycle emissions, and economic trade-offs are assessed within clearly defined spatial, temporal, and scenario boundaries using standardized methods like LCA.
