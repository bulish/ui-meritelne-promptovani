Goal: To assess the environmental sustainability of forest biomass for bioenergy by analyzing carbon neutrality (CN) concepts, quantifying life-cycle impacts of pellet-based heat, and reviewing monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services (ES).

Methods: The thesis comprises four studies: a scoping review of 39 articles (1990–2020) on CN; a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of residential heat from wood pellets in Arkhangelsk, Russia, using primary industry data, OpenLCA 2.1.1, Ecoinvent 3.4, and ReCiPe 2016 (H) across eight impact categories with 1 MJ of heat as the functional unit; a scoping review of 16 studies on ES monetary valuation; and a short communication synthesizing findings.

Main results: The CN review identified eight distinct concepts and concluded CN is not an inherent property of biomass. The LCA showed global warming emissions of 0.015 kg CO₂ eq per 1 MJ of heat. Combustion and waste disposal dominated five of eight impact categories; pellet production most influenced global warming, marine eutrophication, and fossil resource scarcity. Increasing transportation distance from 20 km to 100 km raised global warming and fossil resource scarcity by 50%. The ES review identified over 20 valuation approaches lacking consistent terminology.

Conclusions: Forest biomass bioenergy may support climate mitigation only when CN status, life-cycle emissions, and economic trade-offs are rigorously assessed within clearly defined spatial, temporal, and scenario boundaries using standardized methods like LCA; the authors recommend harmonized environmental and economic assessment to inform policy.
