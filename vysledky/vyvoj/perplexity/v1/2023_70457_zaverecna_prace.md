Goal
To determine how specimen dimensions, moisture content (MC), material modification, vibration mode, and measurement technique affect the reliable use of acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE) for evaluating wood and wood-based materials for musical instruments.

Methods
The thesis studied solid woods and plywood across varying specimen geometries, MC levels, fungal degradation, and thermal modification. It used frequency resonance technique (FRT), dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA), bending-based mechanical evaluation, and numerical modal analysis to compare dynamic modulus, damping, ACE, and vibration spectra.

Main results
Specimen geometry—particularly slenderness and cross-sectional shape—affected vibro-acoustic evaluation more strongly than size. In solid wood with slenderness 48, differences between MOEdyn derived from first bending and first longitudinal modes were negligible; plywood with slenderness 85 still showed material-dependent differences. Above the fibre saturation point, FRT found no significant further change in MOEdyn or tan δ, whereas DMA found continued tan δ increases. Conclusions
ACE and tan δ obtained from different mode shapes, mode orders, sensors, or methods are not generally interchangeable. The authors recommend multimodal ACEcombi and RACEcombi descriptors, which integrate longitudinal, bending, and torsional responses for a more comprehensive differentiation of materials.
