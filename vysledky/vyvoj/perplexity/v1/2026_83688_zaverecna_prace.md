Summary
Goal: To examine whether early retirement affects the size of the shadow economy in Austria, the Czech Republic, Germany, Hungary, Poland, the Slovak Republic, and Slovenia, and to assess related tax losses and overall shadow-economy levels.

Methods: The study uses secondary macroeconomic data for 2010–2024 and applies country-specific MIMIC (Multiple Indicators Multiple Causes) models, treating the shadow economy as a latent variable estimated through GDP growth and labour-force participation.

Main results: Retirement age showed no statistically significant impact on the shadow economy in any country except Hungary. In Hungary, women’s retirement age had a statistically significant negative effect, attributed to the Women 40 programme, under which only formal employment counts toward pension eligibility. Personal income tax was positively associated with the shadow economy in Czechia, Hungary, and Slovakia. Social-security contributions were statistically significant in all countries except Slovakia; in five of six significant cases, higher contributions were associated with a smaller shadow economy. Self-employment had a significant negative effect in Austria, Germany, and Poland, while government effectiveness was positively associated with shadow activity in Germany, Hungary, and Poland.

Conclusions: Retirement-age parameters alone have limited explanatory power for the shadow economy. National pension institutions, social-protection entitlements, taxation, and formalised self-employment are more influential, and further research should examine differentiated pension rights and benefits.
