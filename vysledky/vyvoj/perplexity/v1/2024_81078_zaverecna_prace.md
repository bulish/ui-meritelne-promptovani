Goal
To analyse food security and nutritional conditions in Ghana, assess influencing factors and government interventions, and evaluate progress toward SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030.

Methods
The study synthesised secondary data from Ghana Statistical Service Demographic and Health Surveys, FAO, WHO, the Ministry of Food and Agriculture, and academic sources using descriptive and trend analysis.

Main results
In the 2022 Global Food Security Index, Ghana scored 52.6/100 and ranked 83rd of 113 countries, while ranking 3rd of 28 in Sub-Saharan Africa. Affordability scored 59.9 but declined by 4.4 points since 2012; the “Change in average food costs” indicator declined by 31.5 points. Availability scored 52.4, increasing 9.4 points; farm infrastructure reached 58.6 after a 48.2-point increase, and “Volatility of agricultural production” and “Sufficiency of supply” scored 91.8 and 86.8. However, food loss scored 0, ranking 113th globally. Quality and Safety scored 50.5; nutritional standards fell from 57.1 in 2021 to 29.8 in 2022. Sustainability and Adaptation was weakest at 45.1, with Water scoring 27.6.

Conclusions
Incremental gains in availability have been offset by worsening affordability, food loss, inadequate nutrition policy, and weak climate resilience. The study recommends post-harvest investment, a national nutrition strategy, expanded social protection, climate-smart agriculture, and targeted northern interventions.
