Goal
To monitor two industrial photovoltaic power plants and compare their operational parameters, electricity production, consumption, and interaction with the external grid.

Methods
The study compared Power Plant A in Sentice and Power Plant B near Čebín using operational and technical data for 2023 and 2024. Evaluation followed indicators defined by ČSN EN IEC 61724, including energy production and consumption, self-consumption, electricity drawn from and delivered to the grid, solar irradiation, panel area, installed capacity, capacity utilization, and performance ratio (PR).

Main results
Power Plant A comprises 94 panels over 195.552 m², whereas Power Plant B comprises 366 panels over 715.53 m². Plant A has a 25° module tilt; Plant B has a 15° tilt. Average annual solar irradiation was 1313.4 kWh/m² for Plant A and 1277.4 kWh/m² for Plant B, making irradiation at Plant A 2.8% higher; daily averages were 3.598 kWh/m²/day and 3.500 kWh/m²/day, respectively. In 2024, Plant A’s PR rose to 0.622. In 2023, the panel-area-related value was 0.24217 m²/kWh/kWp for Plant A and 0.854 m²/kWh/kWp for Plant B; in 2024 it fell to 0.23940 and 0.727 m²/kWh/kWp. Production did not fully meet facility demand throughout the year, particularly during lower-irradiance periods.

Conclusions
The comparison indicates that continuous monitoring is necessary to assess actual PV performance and support operational optimization. Seasonal irradiation and high, inflexible industrial demand limit the plants’ ability to achieve complete electricity self-sufficiency.
