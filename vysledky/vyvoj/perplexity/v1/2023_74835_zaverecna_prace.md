Goal
To examine reasons for smartphone use, most-used mobile applications, and usage patterns among internally displaced people (IDPs) from northern Ethiopia.

Methods
One-to-one interviews with 300 adults (18–65) in Addis Ababa and Amhara towns (Bahir Dar, Debre Birhan, Dessie, Debre Sina, Woldia) using chain-referral sampling from November 2022. Data were analyzed with descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and machine-learning models (Decision tree, Random forest) in Python.

Main results
Calls and SMS were the dominant smartphone functions (296 instances; 98.67%), followed by internet access (215; 71.67%), taking pictures (194; 64.67%), and music (192; 64%). Mobile usage had increased since displacement for 205 participants (68.33%). Seventy-two percent (216) had at least one social-media account; Facebook (193) and Telegram (166) were the most used platforms. Decision tree and Random forest models achieved approximately 88–89% accuracy in predicting usage reasons and preferred applications.

Conclusions
Smartphones are essential communication and information tools for IDPs, with usage shaped by age, income, and duration of stay. The authors recommend larger, more diverse samples and expanded geographic coverage to improve model generalizability and inform digital-inclusion policies for conflict-affected populations.
