Goal
To examine the relationship between banking-sector development and innovation activities in EU countries from 2010 to 2020.

Methods
The study used a balanced panel of 24 of 27 EU countries; Cyprus, Ireland, and Slovenia were excluded because of data shortages. Innovation was measured by the logarithm of patent applications (lpa). Banking indicators were lagged by one year, and the analysis used pooled OLS and two-step system GMM for dynamic short-panel data.

Main results
The two-step system GMM estimates showed positive, statistically significant associations between all four banking-development indicators and patent applications. The coefficient for private credit by deposit money banks to GDP (lbank_t_1) was 0.54 (z = 3.95, P>z = 0.00***). Deposit money banks assets to GDP (lasset_t_1) had the largest coefficient, 0.98 (z = 3.83, P>z = 0.00***). Bank credit to bank deposits (ldeposit_t_1) had a coefficient of 0.12 (z = 3.80, P>z = 0.00***), while private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP (lcredit_t_1) was 0.51 (z = 4.08, P>z = 0.00***). Across these models, the number of groups was 24, observations were 240, and Hansen-test probabilities ranged from 0.20 to 0.48.

Conclusions
Banking-sector development is positively associated with innovation activities. The author recommends prioritizing deposit money banks assets to GDP, followed by private credit by deposit money banks to GDP, broader private credit, and bank credit to bank deposits.
