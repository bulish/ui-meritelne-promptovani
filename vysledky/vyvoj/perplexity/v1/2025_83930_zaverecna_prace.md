Goal: Analyze the evolution of the Czech Republic’s foreign policy toward the Srebrenica genocide from 1995 to 2025 and assess its alignment with the European Union.

Methods: Qualitative case study using literature review, secondary data analysis of official statements (Czech Ministry of Foreign Affairs, UN, EU, ICC) from 1995–2025, media sources, and three semi-structured interviews with experts from academia, diplomacy, and the Srebrenica Memorial Center. Field research included site visits in Bosnia and Herzegovina. A comparative analysis across seven categories evaluated Czech–EU policy alignment.

Main results: Czech policy shifted from cautious diplomatic engagement in 1995 to explicit recognition of the Srebrenica massacre as genocide in 2005. The Czech Republic supported the 2024 UN General Assembly resolution (adopted with 84 votes in favor, 19 against, 68 abstentions) designating July 11 as the “International Day of Reflection and Remembrance for the 1995 Srebrenica Genocide.” Czech officials consistently backed ICTY prosecutions (including life sentences for Radovan Karadžić and Ratko Mladić) and contributed financially to the ICC’s Trust Fund for Victims (CZK 1,000,000 in 2024). Commemorations occurred at the 10th, 15th, 20th, and 25th anniversaries, with high-level visits to the Srebrenica-Potočari Memorial Center.

Conclusions: Czech foreign policy increasingly converges with EU norms on genocide recognition and transitional justice, driven by human-rights traditions, post-communist identity, and international engagement. The authors recommend sustained educational initiatives and stronger Czech institutional involvement in memory projects to counter denial and promote reconciliation.
