Goal
To assess how Fourth Industrial Revolution technologies affect the retail labour market in Czechia, Brno, and to propose ways to manage their effects on workers.

Methods
The study used a quantitative questionnaire survey of retail workers in Brno and surrounding areas. Of 406 submitted questionnaires, 399 final responses were analysed using Excel and SPSS.

Main results
Technology had changed workload for 71.6% of respondents; 81.4% were informed before workplace changes and 84% received training beforehand. Only 43.8% agreed that they had enough skills to cope with technological changes, while 50.9% were neutral; 70.5% reported being able to adapt well. Employees anticipated extensive labour-market effects: 85.7% expected fewer retail jobs, 82.9% expected changes in how work is performed, 69.2% believed technology would threaten job security, 72.7% expected to need new skills, and 66.9% expected fewer tasks. Open-ended responses identified online shopping, electronic price tags, network communication, invoicing, inventory systems, and self-checkout as replacements or reductions of manual retail tasks. Conclusions
The thesis concludes that Industry 4.0 is already reshaping Czech retail work and may reduce routine jobs while increasing skill demands. It recommends continuous training, management support, acquisition of cognitive and soft skills, modified education models, and integrated stakeholder engagement.
