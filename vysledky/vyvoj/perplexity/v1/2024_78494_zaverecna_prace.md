Goal
To compare income inequality across the EU-27 during 2007–2023 using the Gini coefficient, Lorenz curves, Atkinson index, and Generalized Entropy (GE) indices, and to assess how inequality changed across income deciles.

Methods
The study analysed Eurostat data on shares of national equivalised income by decile for EU member states, supplemented with Croatian Bureau of Statistics data before 2010. Inequality was calculated using the R ineq library; Atkinson indices used ε = 0.5, 1, and 2, while GE measures used α = 0, 1, and 2.

Main results
EU-average Gini inequality was broadly stable, falling from 0.291 in 2007 to 0.288 in 2023, but national trends diverged. Romania’s Gini declined from 0.375 to 0.305 and Poland’s from 0.315 to 0.265; in Romania, the lowest decile gained 0.2 percentage points while the top decile lost approximately -6.3. Slovakia had the lowest 2023 Gini, 0.215. Bulgaria (0.361) and Lithuania (0.348) ranked among the most unequal in 2023. Malta’s Gini rose from 0.258 to 0.321; Sweden’s increased from 0.229 to 0.286, with GE(2.0) rising by 65.7%.

Conclusions
The Gini alone masks distributional differences: Atkinson indices expose lower-income disparities and GE indices identify top-end concentration. The thesis recommends country-specific redistributive mechanisms, education investment, and inclusive labour-market policies.
