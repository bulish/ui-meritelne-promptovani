Summary
This thesis examines how key opinion leaders (KOLs) influence Vietnamese consumers’ intentions to purchase food and beverages. It combines 12 in-depth interviews with an online questionnaire completed by 210 Vietnamese respondents, supported by social-media observation and statistical analysis. Most respondents reported purchasing or trying a food, drink, restaurant, or shop after encountering a KOL’s review, while interest in following social-media trends was moderate and strongest among younger consumers. Facebook was the most common platform for following KOLs, followed by TikTok and YouTube.

The findings identify trustworthiness as the strongest influence on purchase intention, followed by consumers’ fondness for the KOL and the authenticity of the content. Expertise, follower comments, popularity, and private-life scandals also affect decisions, although their influence varies. KOL appearance has comparatively little effect. The thesis recommends that food-and-beverage businesses prioritize credible KOLs with relevant audiences, focus on Facebook and TikTok, create authentic and accurate content, monitor follower reactions, and assess reputational risks. However, the results should be interpreted cautiously because the sample is limited and disproportionately represents younger respondents.
