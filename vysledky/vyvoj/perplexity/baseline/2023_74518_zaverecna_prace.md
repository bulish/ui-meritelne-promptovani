This thesis examines how banking-sector development relates to innovation activities in 24 European Union countries between 2010 and 2020. Using panel data from the World Bank, it measures innovation by the logarithm of patent applications and banking development through four lagged indicators: private credit by deposit-money banks relative to GDP, deposit-money-bank assets relative to GDP, bank credit relative to deposits, and private credit supplied by banks and other financial institutions relative to GDP. GDP per capita, research-and-development expenditure, tertiary-school enrollment, and governance quality are included as controls.

The study first applies pooled OLS and diagnostic tests, then uses two-step system GMM to address potential endogeneity and heteroskedasticity in the dynamic panel. Across four specifications, every banking-development measure has a positive and statistically significant coefficient. The strongest estimated association concerns deposit-money-bank assets to GDP, followed by private bank credit to GDP, broader private credit, and bank credit relative to deposits. Diagnostic results report no second-order serial correlation and acceptable Hansen tests for instrument validity. The thesis concludes that stronger banking and financial systems are associated with greater innovation, while acknowledging data limitations and recommending future research that distinguishes banking, stock-market, insurance, and bond-market development.
