Summary
The article, Monitoring of Photovoltaic Power Plants and Comparison of Their Operational Parameters, evaluates two photovoltaic installations serving industrial facilities in South Moravia, Czechia. It combines technical descriptions of the plants with operational monitoring and comparison of electricity generation, consumption, grid exchange, and performance indicators. The study applies measurement and evaluation procedures based on the ČSN EN IEC 61724 standard, including energy yield, capacity utilization, efficiency, and the relationship between photovoltaic production and on-site demand. Power Plant A, located in Sentice, uses roof-mounted modules installed at a 25° tilt, while Power Plant B, near Čebín, has a different orientation and a 15° tilt. The results show that photovoltaic output varies substantially with irradiation, season, system configuration, and the facilities’ operating schedules. Both plants can cover a meaningful share of demand during high-production periods, but neither consistently supplies all electricity required. Power Plant A obtained 25.16% of its consumed electricity from its photovoltaic system, while the comparison indicates greater dependence on the external grid at that site. Seasonal deficits, especially outside periods of strong solar irradiation, remain the main limitation. The study concludes that monitoring is essential for assessing plant performance and optimizing energy use.
