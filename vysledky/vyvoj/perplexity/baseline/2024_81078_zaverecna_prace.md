Summary
This thesis analyzes food security and nutritional status in Ghana using secondary data and descriptive and trend analyses across availability, affordability, quality and safety, and sustainability. It finds a mixed trajectory: national food availability improved, with Ghana’s availability score reaching 52.4 and farm infrastructure rising substantially, while agricultural-production volatility and supply sufficiency remained relatively strong. However, access deteriorated as food prices increased, contributing to widespread food insecurity and exposing weaknesses in social-protection coverage. Food losses, poor supply-chain infrastructure, limited cold storage, and inadequate access to agricultural inputs continue to undermine the benefits of increased production. Nutritional outcomes are also concerning. Ghana’s quality-and-safety score was 50.5, dietary diversity remained limited, and nutritional standards fell sharply because the country lacked a national nutrition plan. Anemia, stunting, wasting, micronutrient deficiencies, and increasing obesity illustrate a persistent double burden of malnutrition. Sustainability and adaptation were the weakest dimension, reflecting poor water management, limited irrigation, weak disaster preparedness, and insufficient climate-adaptation policies. Government programs—including Planting for Food and Jobs, school feeding, One Village, One Dam, and NAFCO—have produced partial gains but face funding, implementation, and infrastructure constraints. Overall, Ghana remains off track for fully achieving SDGs 1, 2, and 3 by 2030.
