Summary
This study examines how the Fourth Industrial Revolution is changing work in the Czech retail sector. It defines Industry 4.0 as the integration of digital, physical, and biological technologies, including artificial intelligence, robotics, the Internet of Things, online platforms, and automation. The research uses a quantitative questionnaire survey of 399 retail workers in Brno and surrounding areas, supported by descriptive analysis in Excel and SPSS. Respondents generally reported that technology had changed their workload, while most said they were informed about workplace changes and received preparatory training. However, fewer than half believed they currently possessed sufficient skills to manage technological change, despite generally expressing confidence in their ability to adapt. Employees viewed the consequences cautiously: 85.7% expected fewer retail jobs, 69.2% perceived a threat to job security, 82.9% anticipated major changes in how work is performed, and 72.7% believed new skills would be necessary. Open-ended responses identified self-checkouts, automated inventory, electronic pricing, online ordering, and delivery services as major changes. The study therefore recommends continuous training, stronger managerial support, soft-skill development, educational reform, and cooperation among employers, policymakers, and educational institutions.
