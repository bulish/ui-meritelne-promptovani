This diploma thesis examines Vietnam’s wood industry and exports, with the goal of assessing the current situation, identifying factors influencing export performance, drawing lessons from China, Malaysia, and Indonesia, and proposing solutions to expand wood exports to Europe, especially the EU. The study uses synthesis, analysis, comparison, document research, and logical methods, reviewing EU market characteristics, trade rules, export data, and the effects of the EVFTA and VPA/FLEGT agreements.

Vietnam’s wood and wood-products exports reached USD 8.52 billion in 2019, increasing 17.8% over the previous year. Total wood-sector export turnover reached USD 12.37 billion in 2020, up 16.2% from 2019. Vietnam exports wood products to more than 120 countries, but exports to Europe remain below potential: annual EU demand is reported at up to 85 billion US dollars, whereas Vietnam exports approximately 700–800 million US dollars annually. Key constraints include shortages of legal raw materials, limited forest certification, small-scale production, restricted financial resources, insufficient technology and human-resource quality, and strict EU requirements concerning traceability, legality, quality, packaging, and environmental protection.

The author concludes that Vietnam can expand its European wood exports by improving legality, sustainability, product quality, productivity, competitiveness, and compliance with EVFTA and VPA/FLEGT requirements. Recommended measures include domestic resource development, company standardization, export promotion, enterprise cooperation, risk management, and improved access to EU distribution channels.
