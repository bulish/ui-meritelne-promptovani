Summary
This thesis examines whether forest biomass can provide a sustainable alternative to fossil fuels for climate-change mitigation. It combines four studies addressing carbon neutrality, life-cycle impacts, and ecosystem-service valuation. A scoping review of 39 publications identifies eight different meanings of biomass carbon neutrality, including carbon-cycle, inherent, offset, residue-based, substitution, and life-cycle neutrality. The thesis argues that carbon neutrality is not an intrinsic property of biomass: conclusions depend on feedstock origin, spatial and temporal boundaries, supply-chain emissions, and the counterfactual scenario.

A life-cycle assessment of residential heat production in Russia evaluates pellets made from sawmill residues. For 1 MJ of heat, the system produces 0.015 kg CO₂-equivalent in global-warming impact. Pellet production contributes most to global warming, marine eutrophication, and fossil-resource scarcity, whereas combustion and ash disposal dominate several other impact categories. Transportation is the least influential stage under the 20-km baseline, but extending transport to 100 km substantially increases impacts.

The thesis also reviews more than 20 monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services. Overall, it concludes that forest-biomass bioenergy may support climate mitigation only when feedstocks, life-cycle emissions, ecological effects, and economic trade-offs are assessed within clearly defined and comparable boundaries.
