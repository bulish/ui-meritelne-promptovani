Goal: This bachelor’s thesis analyzes the Czech Republic’s foreign policy on genocide, focusing on Srebrenica, to trace its evolution from 1995 to 2025, identify the historical, political, and international factors shaping it, and assess alignment with the European Union.

Methods: A qualitative case study combined a literature review, secondary data analysis of official statements and media, fieldwork in Bosnia and Herzegovina (March–April 2025), three semi-structured interviews analyzed through open coding, and a comparative analysis across seven categories.

Main results: Czech policy evolved from initial hesitation to commitment to recognition, justice, and remembrance. In 2005 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs officially recognized Srebrenica as genocide, before the European Parliament’s 2009 recognition. Czechia supported the ICTY and ICC and voted for the 2024 UN General Assembly resolution (84 in favor, 19 against, 68 abstentions). Czech humanism, experience of communism, Václav Havel’s leadership, and UN, NATO, and EU ties shaped this course, while Miloš Zeman was less engaged. Alignment with the EU is strong, but the EU has more institutionalized education and prevention mechanisms, such as Erasmus+ and the Genocide Network.

Conclusions: Even a small post-communist state can advance international justice and memory, though domestic politics created inconsistencies.
