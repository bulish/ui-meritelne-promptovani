Summary

Goal: The thesis compares income inequality across all 27 EU member states from 2007 to 2023 using three measures (Gini coefficient, Atkinson index, Generalized Entropy indices). It also aims to identify drivers of change via shifts in income shares across deciles and to compare the measures’ results.

Methods: Data come from Eurostat’s “Distribution of income by quantiles”, using the share of national equivalised income per decile. Croatia’s pre-2010 data are supplemented by the Croatian Bureau of Statistics. Calculations use the “ineq” R library. Atkinson indices use ε = 0.5, 1 and 2; GE indices use α = 0 (MLD), 1 (Theil) and 2. Lorenz curve differences and percentage changes are examined.

Main results: Romania’s Gini fell from 0.375 to 0.305 and Poland’s from 0.315 to 0.265, while Sweden’s rose from 0.229 to 0.286 and Malta’s from 0.258 to 0.321. Slovakia was the most equal in 2023 (0.215). The EU Gini was nearly unchanged (0.291 to 0.288). In Sweden, GE(2.0) rose 65.7%, A(0.5) 61.3% and Gini 25.1%. Bulgaria’s Atkinson index rose from 0.106 (ε = 0.5) to 0.351 (ε = 2.0).

Conclusions: The stable EU average masks divergent national paths. Multiple measures reveal where inequality is concentrated, which the Gini alone overlooks.
