Summary

Goal: The thesis aimed to advance understanding of forest biomass for bioenergy as a climate change (CC) mitigation option by analyzing carbon neutrality (CN), assessing environmental impacts of pellet-based heat, and identifying monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services (ES).

Methods: Two scoping reviews analyzed 39 articles on CN and 16 on ES valuation. A life cycle assessment (LCA) followed ISO 14040/14044 (ReCiPe 2016 (H) Midpoint), using 1 MJ of heat as functional unit, for pellets from sawmill residues in Arkhangelsk burned in a 25 kW furnace, plus three sensitivity analyses.

Main results: No commonly accepted CN definition; eight concepts were identified, and CN status depended on temporal and spatial boundaries, feedstock origin, and scenario assumptions. LCA yielded 0.015 kg CO2 eq global warming per MJ. Combustion and waste disposal dominated five of eight categories; pellet production dominated global warming, marine eutrophication and fossil resource scarcity; transportation never exceeded 17%. Natural gas drying raised global warming by 6.5%; 100 km transport raised global warming and fossil resource scarcity by 50%. Over twenty valuation approaches showed inconsistent terminology.

Conclusions: CN is not inherent to biomass; LCA is recommended. Forest biomass can aid CC mitigation if rigorously assessed; standardized valuation terminology is needed.
