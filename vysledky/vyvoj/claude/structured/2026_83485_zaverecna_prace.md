Summary

Goal: This master’s thesis (Mendel University in Brno) investigates the personal experiences of LGBTQ community members in Zimbabwe, covering the challenges and discrimination they face, the impact of societal norms, cultural beliefs and legal frameworks, and their coping mechanisms and support systems. It is framed by Minority Stress Theory.

Methods: A qualitative design was used. Participants were recruited by snowball sampling, with initial referrals from GALZ and PAKASIPITI. Eighteen semi-structured interviews of about 15–30 minutes were conducted by WhatsApp voice calls and Microsoft Teams. Data were analysed with thematic analysis and narrative inquiry under a decolonizing stance. Participants were aged 18–35.

Main results: Participants described misconceptions about LGBTQ identities, workplace discrimination (including football-team dismissals), and rejection in religious institutions. They also reported family disownment, blackmail and extortion by police and civilians, verbal, physical and sexual violence, and documentation barriers for transgender people. Reported coping strategies were concealing sexuality, selective disclosure, safe spaces (GALZ, PAKASIPITI, TRANSSMART), private social media groups, and substance use.

Conclusions: Anti-LGBTQ laws, religion and culture drive stigma and discrimination. The thesis recommends societal sensitization, more mental health facilities, legal gender-marker change, and an end to hate speech.

Note: the sample table lists 17 participants against 18 interviews in the text.
