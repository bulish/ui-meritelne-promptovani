Summary

Goal: The thesis examines whether banking sector development is positively associated with innovation activities (patent applications) in EU countries from 2010 to 2020.

Methods: The study uses balanced panel data on 24 of 27 EU countries (Cyprus, Ireland and Slovenia dropped for data shortage) from the World Bank. The dependent variable is the log of patent applications (lpa). Banking development is measured by four 1-year-lagged indicators: Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP, Deposit money banks assets to GDP, Bank credit to bank deposits, and Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP. Controls are GDP per capita, R&D expenditure, tertiary enrollment, and governance quality. Five models were estimated with pooled OLS, tested for multicollinearity (VIF), heteroskedasticity (White’s test) and autocorrelation (Wooldridge test), then re-estimated with two-step system GMM.

Main results: All VIFs were below 10, White’s test detected heteroskedasticity, and no autocorrelation was found. In the GMM models, the banking coefficients were 0.54 (private credit by banks), 0.98 (bank assets), 0.12 (credit to deposits) and 0.51 (private credit including other institutions), all significant at 1%. The AR(2) and Hansen tests were passed.

Conclusions: A developed banking system supports innovation. The author ranks bank assets to GDP highest, followed by the two private credit ratios and credit to deposits.
