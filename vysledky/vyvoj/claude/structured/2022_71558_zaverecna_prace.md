Summary (about 182 words)

Goal: The thesis investigated existing forest machine operator qualification standards. It aimed to develop and establish European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) occupational standards as a basis for European qualifications (harvester, forwarder, tractor/skidder).

Methods: A literature review (over 50 documents) and field research (30 stakeholder meetings) were combined with a three-stage qualitative analysis (content, comparative, thematic/interpretive). The analysis mapped UK, Czech and Slovenian national occupational standards (NOS), with UK standards as the benchmark. Learning outcomes were aligned with the European Qualification Framework (EQF), and the standards were verified in 14 pilot tests, mainly in Ireland.

Main results: Demand for skilled operators and machinery accidents were increasing. National standards were highly variable, and no European qualifications existed. UK and Czech harvester standards were skewed toward knowledge criteria, almost twice the practical performance criteria, while Slovenian standards were more balanced. Five core harvester competency areas were identified. 65 operators were certified in Ireland. Harvester and forwarder qualifications were live tested, whereas the skidder qualification needs at least one more pilot test.

Conclusions: The objectives were achieved, and the qualifications were rated at EQF level 4. The author recommends them for improving safety culture, recognition of qualifications and transnational labour mobility.
