Summary

Goal: This diploma thesis assesses how fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0) technologies are changing the Czech retail labour market, focusing on retail workers in Brno and South Moravia.

Methods: A quantitative design was used. A Google Forms questionnaire, distributed in Czech, collected responses. Slovin’s formula (e = 0.05, N = 577,026) gave a target sample of 400. 406 workers responded and 399 valid responses were analysed with Excel and SPSS. Cronbach’s alpha was 0.74 for implementation (5 items) and 0.76 for impact perception (7 items). Pearson’s correlation was applied.

Main results: 71.6% agreed that new technology changed their workload, and 84% agreed management trains them before changes. Only 43.8% felt they had enough skills. 85.7% expected fewer retail jobs, 69.2% saw job security threatened, and 72.7% expected to need new skills. Only 43.3% expected better work-life balance. Implementation and impact correlated strongly, r(397) = 0.91, p < .001. Czechia’s DESI rank fell from 18th to 19th (2021–2022).

Conclusions: The author concludes that Industry 4.0 will substantially reshape retail work. Recommendations include acquiring soft skills, modifying education models, and integrated stakeholder engagement.
