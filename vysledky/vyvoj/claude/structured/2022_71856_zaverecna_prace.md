Summary

Goal: The thesis assesses taxation as a catalyst for economic growth in Ghana, examining the association between taxation and growth, between capital expenditure and growth, and between revenue expenditure and growth.

Methods: Annual data for 1990–2022 came from the Ghana Revenue Authority, the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, and World Development Indicators. Variables were GDP growth, total tax revenue, capital expenditure, revenue expenditure, and inflation. The author used VAR lag selection (lag 3), Augmented Dickey-Fuller stationarity tests, the Johansen cointegration test, a Vector Error Correction Model (VECM), and Granger causality tests.

Main results: All variables had unit roots at levels and were stationary after first differencing, i.e. I(1). Johansen tests indicated cointegration up to rank 3. The long-run equation showed inflation (-0.122) and revenue expenditure (-0.091) reducing growth, while capital expenditure (0.205) and tax revenue (0.020) raised it. The VECM with GDP as dependent variable gave an error correction term of -0.043 (p = 0.023), about 4.3% adjustment per period. Granger tests showed total tax revenue Granger-causes GDP (p = 0.037), with no causality in reverse or for other variables.

Conclusions: The author concludes taxes and growth have a statistically significant positive causal link and recommends intensifying revenue mobilization, prioritizing capital spending, and managing the public wage bill.
