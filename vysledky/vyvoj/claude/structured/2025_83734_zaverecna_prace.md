Summary

Goal: This diploma thesis examines how university students in South Korea (Pukyong National University, PKNU) and the Czech Republic (Mendel University) perceive and experience work–study–life balance (WSLB) and how it relates to their wellbeing. Methods: A quantitative, comparative, cross-sectional online questionnaire (Google Forms) covered time allocation, WSLB conflict, stress, emotional wellbeing, cultural perceptions and life satisfaction. Only students with a job or internship were analysed. Analyses used SPSS: independent-samples t-tests, Spearman correlations, multiple regression and chi-square tests. Main results: Korean students studied more (largest effect), while Czech students spent more time on work, personal time and household duties. Korean students reported higher academic, social-expectation and future-uncertainty stress, and higher anxiety. Czech students reported higher work stress, emotional exhaustion, inability to cope, health strain and relationship interference. Lack of personal time was the strongest predictor of emotional exhaustion in Czech students (β = 0.495). Mental-health service use differed (χ² = 40.300, p < .001). Czech students had higher life satisfaction (M = 2.75 vs. 2.47; p = .018; d = 0.295). Conclusions: Wellbeing arises from interaction of academic structures, employment, cultural norms and coping capacities.
