Summary

Goal: The thesis examines whether early retirement (statutory retirement age) affects the size of the shadow economy in seven Central European countries (Austria, Czechia, Germany, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia). It also aims to assess tax losses from informal employment after early retirement and to measure the overall shadow economy level for 2010-2024.

Methods: The study applies a MIMIC model, with the shadow economy as a latent variable, estimated in Stata 19 (SEM module) by Maximum Likelihood, separately for each country. Causes include GDP per capita, self-employment, unemployment, government effectiveness, PIT, CIT, social security contributions (SSC), retirement age for men and women, and net migration. Indicators are GDP growth and labour force participation. Data come from the World Bank and OECD.

Main results: Retirement age was statistically insignificant everywhere except Hungary, where women’s retirement age had a significant negative effect (coefficient -5.27, p = 0.000), linked to the “Women 40” programme. SSC was significant in all countries except Slovakia, mostly negative (Hungary positive). Self-employment was negative in Austria, Germany and Poland. PIT was positive in Czechia, Hungary and Slovakia.

Conclusions: Retirement age is not a key determinant; tax-related and institutional factors dominate, so pension reforms alone are unlikely to reduce the shadow economy.
