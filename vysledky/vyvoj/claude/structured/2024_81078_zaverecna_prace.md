Goal: The thesis analyses food security and nutritional status in Ghana, examining current trends, influencing factors, government policies and interventions, and progress toward SDGs 1, 2 and 3 by 2030.

Methods: It uses secondary data (national surveys, FAO, WHO and MoFA reports, academic research) with descriptive and trend analysis, mainly of Global Food Security Index (GFSI) 2022 indicators compared with 2012.

Main results: Ghana scored 52.6 overall, ranking 3rd of 28 Sub-Saharan countries. Affordability fell 4.4 points since 2012, with the food-cost indicator down 31.5. Availability rose 9.4 points and farm infrastructure 48.2, yet food loss scored 0.0. Nutritional standards dropped from 57.1 to 29.8 without a national nutrition plan, and sustainability and adaptation was weakest (45.1). Food insecurity affected 49.1% of Ghanaians in 2022, with 46.7% in northern versus 13.8% in southern regions. Programmes such as Planting for Food and Jobs and One Village, One Dam showed gains but also implementation gaps.

Conclusions: Progress is incremental. SDG 1 is off track, SDG 2 partially on track, and SDG 3 mixed. The author recommends reducing post-harvest loss, adopting a national nutrition strategy, scaling up LEAP, strengthening climate adaptation, and targeting regional disparities.
