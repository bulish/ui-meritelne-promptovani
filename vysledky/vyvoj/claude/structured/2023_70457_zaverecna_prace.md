Summary

Goal: The thesis extends the description of acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE) by studying the factors that affect its reliable determination. It aims to make the assessment of materials for musical instruments more comprehensive and robust.

Methods: The frequency resonance technique (FRT) was applied to solid woods (spruce, beech, ash, oak, linden, alder, larch, maple) and pine, birch and poplar plywood. Specimens ranged from 2400 × 160 × 50 mm to 61 × 8.4 × 1.7 mm, with moisture content from ≈4% to ≈80%. Materials were modified by Kretzschmaria deusta fungal degradation and by thermal treatment at 180 and 220 °C. FRT was compared with dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) and static, oscillatory and impact bending tests. ANSYS modal analysis supported mode identification.

Results: Specimen size affected ACE, but geometry (slenderness) mattered more. Above the fibre saturation point, FRT showed no significant change in MOEdyn or tan δ, whereas DMA tan δ kept rising. MOEdyn from FRT and DMA correlated at R² = 0.84. Fungal degradation reduced ACE by ≈74%. Thermal modification raised ACE in a species-dependent way. The relationship between impact bending strength and MOEdyn was weak, and tan δ was not interchangeable between modes. The aggregated parameters ACEcombi and RACEcombi were introduced.

Conclusions: Of the 14 hypotheses, 13 were accepted and 1 (H4c) was rejected. ACEcombi and RACEcombi differentiate materials and describe vibro-acoustic properties more comprehensively than a single mode.
