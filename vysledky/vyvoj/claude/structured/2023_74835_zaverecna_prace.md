Summary

Goal: The thesis examines why internally displaced persons (IDPs) from northern Ethiopia use smartphones, which mobile applications they use most, and their usage patterns, in Tigray and Amhara-region contexts and Addis Ababa. It also aims to build models predicting reasons for smartphone use and most-used applications.

Methods: Using chain referral sampling, 300 IDPs aged 18-65 were interviewed one-to-one from November 2022 in Amharic and Tigrigna, with questionnaires entered via Google Forms. Data were cleaned in Python (Google Colaboratory), analyzed through descriptive statistics and correlation, and modeled with decision tree and random forest classifiers after chi-square feature selection (top 10 features), oversampling, and GridSearchCV tuning.

Main results: Calls/SMS (296), internet (215), pictures (194) and music (192) were top uses. 205 (68.33%) reported rising mobile use after displacement; 216 (72%) had social media accounts, most commonly Facebook (193) and Telegram (166). Challenges included internet problems, power shortages and device costs. Accuracy was about 87-89%; both models matched for reasons of use, while the decision tree performed best for applications (89%).

Conclusions: Phones are vital for communication and information. Income and shorter stay were associated with increased use. The author recommends larger samples, nationwide research and digital literacy and policy initiatives.
