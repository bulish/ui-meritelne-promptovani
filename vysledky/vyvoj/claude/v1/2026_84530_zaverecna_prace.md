Goal
To examine the role of student movements in Thailand’s 2020–2021 pro-democracy protests: their mobilization, demands, government responses, and reflection in political discourse.

Methods
A qualitative, interpretative single-case study applying Social Movement Theory (resource mobilization, political opportunity structure, framing) to secondary sources (academic literature, media, NGO reports), plus comparison with 2023 election results.

Main results
Students mobilized through Facebook, Twitter, Telegram and LINE, decentralized networks, campus flash mobs and small donations; a Free Youth Telegram group hit its 200,000-subscriber cap. The Future Forward Party’s February 2020 dissolution triggered protests in a largely closed political system. Demands (prime minister’s resignation, parliament’s dissolution, new constitution) expanded to monarchy reform. The government responded with emergency powers, prosecutions, force and platform restrictions; parliament rejected constitutional amendment in March 2021. In 2023, the Move Forward Party won 151 of 500 seats (turnout above 75%, nearly 40 million votes) but was dissolved in 2024. Freedom House scores moved from 29/100 (2021) to 36/100 (2023), then 34/100 (2024).

Conclusions
Student movements were key mobilizers and significantly shaped public discourse and electoral behavior, though institutional change stayed limited. Even in constrained settings, such movements can reshape narratives; further comparative research is recommended.
