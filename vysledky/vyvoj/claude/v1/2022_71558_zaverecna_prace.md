Goal
The thesis investigates the state of forest machine operator qualification standards in Europe and develops European Forest Machine Operator (EFMO) occupational standards, aligned with the EQF, as a basis for Pan-European qualifications for harvester, forwarder and skidder operation.

Methods
Literature review and stakeholder interviews and meetings (over 50 documents, 30 meetings) preceded a qualitative mapping of UK, Czech and Slovenian national standards (content, comparative, thematic and interpretive analysis). The harmonised standards were then standard-set and pilot-tested, mainly in Ireland, with 14 pilot tests, followed by live tests.

Main results
Standards varied widely: UK harvester operation requires 87 learning outcomes (54 base machine plus 33 harvester), whereas Czech standards list 17 for harvester work. UK and Czech standards were skewed towards knowledge criteria, almost twice the practical criteria, while Slovenian standards were more balanced. Harmonised harvester, forwarder and skidder standards each use five competence levels. Pilot tests (3 to 7 hours, 6 to 23 participants) ran from 2019 to 2021; harvester testing finished first, forwarder took seven pilot tests, and skidder needs at least one more. Sixty-five candidates were certified in Ireland as of March 2022; certification lasts five years and is graded A+ to F.

Conclusions
The authors conclude that the EFMO qualifications, rated at EQF level 4, are enhanced versions of national standards and are in use on the market. They endorse their use to improve safety culture, recognition of operators’ qualifications, and European labour competitiveness.
