Goal
The thesis analyses how key opinion leaders (KOLs) influence Vietnamese consumers’ food and beverage (F&B) purchase intentions, identifies the KOL factors that matter most, and derives recommendations for F&B businesses.

Methods
Mixed methods: in-depth interviews (n = 12, February 2024), an online questionnaire (n = 210, 1–15 February 2024) with Likert scales, and the author’s observation of social media. Data were analysed in Excel, including Chi-Square tests of independence.

Main results
Facebook was the most used platform for following KOLs (71.9%), followed by TikTok (58.1%), YouTube (46.7%) and Instagram (27.6%). 81.4% had bought or tried something after a KOL review; this was not associated with gender (p = 0.245222). Following social-media trends was associated with age (p = 0.000082): mean 3.49 for under-24s versus 2.19 for those over 44. Factor means (1–5): fondness for KOLs 4.13, trustworthiness 4.01, authentic content 3.84, expertise 3.38, followers’ reactions 3.34, scandals 3.15, popularity 3.07, appearance 2.33.

Conclusions
KOLs significantly shape Vietnamese F&B purchasing, and trustworthiness underpins all influential factors. The author recommends that businesses use Facebook and TikTok, choose trustworthy KOLs, prioritise authentic content, and treat appearance as low priority. Trend-following suits mainly younger customers. Limitations include a sample skewed towards ages 18–24, small size, and translation issues.
