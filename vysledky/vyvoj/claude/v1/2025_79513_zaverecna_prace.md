Goal
To evaluate and compare the real-world performance of two photovoltaic power plants with different capacities and configurations.

Methods
The study used SolarEdge monitoring data from two nearby industrial plants over 2023–2024. Power Plant A has 39.8 kWp, a 25° tilt and serves food processing. Power Plant B has 139.44 kWp, a 15° tilt, serves metal processing and was commissioned in March 2023. KPIs were calculated following ČSN EN IEC 61724-1 ed. 2 and compared with PVGIS simulations.

Main results

Energy use: Self-sufficiency was 58.65% for A vs 46.68% for B. Utilization of own production was 74.84% vs 77.68%, and grid dependency was 41.35% vs 53.32%.
Performance ratio: It rose from 0.615 to 0.622 (A) and from 0.656 to 0.770 (B). Both stayed below the 0.75–0.85 typical for well-functioning systems.
Specific yield: It increased from 807.493 to 816.839 kWh/kWp (A) and from 838.043 to 983.976 kWh/kWp (B).
Capacity utilization factor: It went from 9.22% to 9.32% (A) and from 9.57% to 11.23% (B).
Anomalies: Plant B showed weekend overproduction and a sharp production drop in October 2024. PVGIS generally tracked real seasonal output.

Conclusions
Both systems operate almost efficiently. Plant A needs only software-level load shifting of its cooling. Plant B would benefit from:

a battery storing about 10–15% of daily output, or load-shifting control
soiling sensors
cleaning before autumn
hydrophobic coatings
