Goal
The thesis extends the description of acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE) by studying factors that affect its determination (specimen dimensions, material state, vibrational mode, sensor and method). It aims to build a more robust assessment of materials for musical instruments.

Methods
Specimens of European solid woods and plywood (largest 2400 × 160 × 50 mm; smallest 61 × 8.4 × 1.7 mm) were tested across five papers. Conditions included several moisture contents, fungal degradation and thermal modification. The frequency resonance technique (FRT) was supported by numerical modal analysis, DMA, and static, oscillatory and impact bending tests.

Main results

Specimens with slenderness >48 showed negligible MOEdyn differences between first bending and longitudinal modes.
Above FSP, MOEdyn and tan δ did not change in FRT, whereas DMA tan δ kept rising.
FRT MOEdyn was 24–28% higher than DMA (R² = 0.84).
Fungal degradation lowered ACE by ≈74%.
Thermal modification increased ACE, species-dependently.
IBS correlated only weakly with MOEdyn.
Bending intervals were ≈2.76 : 5.40, set by geometry.
ACEcombi and RACEcombi separated materials; spruce had KL 12.88 and KT 3.04.

Conclusions
All hypotheses except H4c were accepted. ACE and RACE distinguish species, modifications and solid wood versus plywood. The aggregated ACEcombi and RACEcombi give a fuller description. Bending-stiffness NDT is not a reliable general strength predictor. A KT > 3 threshold is proposed for resonance wood.
