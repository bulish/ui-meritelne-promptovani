Summary

Goal
To examine whether early retirement affects the size of the shadow economy in Austria, Czechia, Germany, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia and Slovenia.

Methods
Quantitative analysis of secondary World Bank and OECD data for 2010–2024, using a MIMIC model (shadow economy as a latent variable) estimated separately for each country.

Main results
Statutory retirement age had no statistically significant effect on the shadow economy in six countries. In Hungary, women’s retirement age had a significant negative effect (coefficient −5.27, p = 0.000), while men’s did not. Social security contributions were significant everywhere except Slovakia, negatively in five countries but positively in Hungary. Self-employment was significantly negative in Austria, Germany and Poland; personal income tax significantly positive in Czechia, Hungary and Slovakia; government effectiveness significantly positive in Germany, Hungary and Poland. The migration–retirement hypothesis was not confirmed.

Conclusions
Retirement age is not a key determinant of the shadow economy in Central Europe; its effect depends on national pension design. The authors conclude that pension reforms alone are unlikely to affect informal activity without complementary institutional measures, and recommend a comprehensive approach focused on institutional quality and incentives within the formal sector.
