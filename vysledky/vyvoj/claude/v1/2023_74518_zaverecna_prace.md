Goal
The thesis examines whether banking sector development is positively associated with innovation activities in European Union countries from 2010 to 2020, with innovation measured by patent applications.

Methods
The study uses a balanced panel of 24 EU countries (Cyprus, Ireland and Slovenia excluded for data shortage), drawn from World Bank data. Five models were estimated using pooled OLS and then a two-step system GMM. Banking development variables enter with a 1-year lag. Controls are GDP per capita, R&D expenditure, tertiary enrollment and governance quality.

Main results
The GMM results show significant positive coefficients for all four banking indicators: Deposit money banks assets to GDP (0.98), Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP (0.54), Private credit by deposit money banks and other financial institutions to GDP (0.51), and Bank credit to bank deposits (0.12). OLS R-squared was 0.41 for Model 1 and 0.46–0.48 for Models 2–5. Mean VIF was below 10 in all models. White’s test indicated heteroskedasticity in all models. The Wooldridge test found no autocorrelation. Arellano-Bond AR(2) and Hansen tests supported valid models. R&D expenditure showed a negative coefficient (-0.12 in Model 1).

Conclusions
A developed banking system supports innovation, and financial market development appears to encourage it too. The author recommends that the EU banking system prioritize increasing these ratios, ranked by impact: Deposit money banks assets to GDP, Private credit by deposit money banks to GDP, Private credit by banks and other financial institutions to GDP, then Bank credit to bank deposits.
