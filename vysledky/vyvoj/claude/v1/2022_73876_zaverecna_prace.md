Goal
The thesis assesses how fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0) technologies change the Czech retail labour market and work environment, addressing job effects, the industry’s future, and why retail should embrace the change.

Methods
A quantitative questionnaire survey of randomly sampled retail workers in Brno and South Moravia. Of 406 responses, 399 were usable (sample target 400 via Slovin’s formula). Data were analysed in Excel and SPSS using descriptive statistics, Cronbach’s alpha and Pearson correlation.

Main results
Respondents were 82.95% female, 49.9% aged 20–30, and 84.71% full-time; 74.7% knew Industry 4.0 “just a little”. Implementation: 71.6% reported workload changed; 84% received training before changes; only 43.8% felt they had enough skills; 70.5% could adapt well (mean 3.92, SD 0.62). Impact perceptions (mean 3.83, SD 0.53): 85.7% expect fewer retail jobs; 82.9% expect work methods to change; 69.2% see job security threatened; 72.7% need new skills; 66.9% expect fewer tasks; 43.3% expect better work-life balance. Implementation and impact correlated strongly, r(397) = 0.91, p < .001. Czechia’s DESI rank fell from 18th to 19th (2021–2022).

Conclusions
Industry 4.0 will substantially reshape retail labour. The author recommends developing soft skills, modifying education models, and stakeholder engagement among government, agencies, employers and employees.
