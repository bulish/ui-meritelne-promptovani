Goal
The thesis examines Vietnam’s wood exports to Europe, identifies factors affecting export performance, draws lessons from China, Malaysia and Indonesia, and proposes solutions for increasing exports to the EU.

Methods
The author uses document research, synthesis, analysis and comparison of secondary data (mainly Vietnam Customs statistics, 2018-2020), plus a review of other countries’ wood export models.

Main results
Vietnam’s wood exports reached USD 12.37 billion in 2020, up 16.2%. Exports to Europe rose from USD 38.9 billion (2018) to 41.54 billion (2019) and 56.45 billion (2020), a 35.9% increase. The EU is Vietnam’s fourth-largest wood market overall and second-largest for wood commodities alone. EU demand reaches USD 85 billion annually, yet Vietnam sends only USD 700-800 million. EU tariffs on wood range from 0 to 5.6%. Imported wood material prices rise 10-30% yearly, outpacing the 10% rise in finished-product prices. Vietnam has 85% planted concentrated forest and 237,386 hectares with sustainable forest management certificates. Under EVFTA, the EU eliminates 85.6% of tariff lines immediately and 99.2% within seven years. Challenges include FSC/CoC certification costs, raw material shortages, and traceability requirements under VPA/FLEGT.

Conclusions
The author concludes that synchronized solutions are essential: securing domestic raw materials, enterprise linking, quality standards, export promotion, certification, traceability and access to EU distribution channels. Firms should shift from small-scale to large-scale production and exploit EVFTA and VPA/FLEGT.
