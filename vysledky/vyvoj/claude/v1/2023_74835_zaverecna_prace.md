Goal
The thesis explores why internally displaced persons (IDPs) from northern Ethiopia use smartphones, which mobile applications they use most, and what usage patterns they show. It also builds models predicting reasons for use and preferred applications.

Methods
Interviews were conducted with 300 IDPs aged 18–65 from November 2022, selected by chain referral sampling in Addis Ababa and Amhara towns. Data were analyzed with descriptive statistics and correlation. Decision tree and random forest classifiers were built for prediction.

Main results

54% of respondents had no formal education; 84% were jobless after displacement.
68.33% reported rising mobile use since migration; 71.33% found phones helpful.
Main uses: calls/SMS 296, internet 215, pictures 194, music 192.
72% had social media accounts; Facebook (193), Telegram (166) and WhatsApp (135) led.
160 respondents used phones for money transfers.
Top challenges: internet problems (57.67% strongly agreed), power shortage, device cost.
Longer stay (over one year) lowered, and having income raised, the likelihood of increased use.
Models: both reached 88% accuracy for usage reasons; for applications, decision tree reached 89% versus random forest 88%.

Conclusions
Phones are vital for communication and information among IDPs, shaped by income and length of stay. The author recommends larger studies, nationwide research, and a digital technology policy with digital literacy programs, affordable smartphones, and internet access.
