Goal
To advance understanding of forest biomass for bioenergy as a climate change (CC) mitigation option by examining carbon neutrality (CN), environmental impacts, and ecosystem service (ES) valuation.

Methods
A compilation of four publications: two scoping reviews (39 CN articles from 5699 identified; 16 ES articles from 109), an ISO-compliant life cycle assessment (LCA) of residential heat from sawmill-residue pellets in Northwest Russia, and a qualitative synthesis.

Main results
No accepted CN definition exists; eight distinct concepts were identified, and CN status depends on feedstock origin, temporal and spatial boundaries, and scenario assumptions. The LCA gave 0.015 kg CO2 eq per MJ of heat. Combustion and waste disposal dominated five of eight impact categories (74.5% of fine particulate matter formation); pellet production dominated global warming, marine eutrophication (85.7%), and fossil resource scarcity (49.4%); transportation never exceeded 17%. Raising transport distance from 20 to 100 km increased global warming and fossil resource scarcity by 50%. Over twenty monetary ES valuation approaches showed inconsistent terminology and hybridization.

Conclusions
Forest biomass can contribute to CC mitigation, but CN is context specific, so LCA is required. Supply-chain optimization, notably shorter transport distances and sustainable sourcing, is recommended, alongside standardized valuation terminology.
