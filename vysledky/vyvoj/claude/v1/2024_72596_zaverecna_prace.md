Goal
The dissertation aims to define digital sovereignty scientifically for EU states, measure it quantitatively, and derive recommendations for strengthening it.

Methods
Based on literature analysis, five expert interviews and secondary data, the author built a Digital Sovereignty Index (DSI). It covers 212 states and 30 parameters, organised in three sub-indices: state sovereignty, key enabling technologies (KETs) and technological sovereignty. The index was validated by stress testing, simulation and comparison with existing indices.

Main results
Equal weighting, raw data and mean imputation gave the most stable index. All EU-27 states rank in the DSI top 50; the USA (1), Ireland (2) and Norway (3) lead. Stress testing (±20%) showed robust rankings. Correlations: FSI R² 0.86, BLI 0.77, SDG ranks 0.71, EFI 0.57, NCSI 0.59, ESI overall 0.63. EU states split into Group A (7 states), Group B (13) and Group C (5). Treated as one state, the EU would rank 4th. The USA’s start-up investment is 135,649 million USD, about 17-fold the EU total of 8,146 million USD.

Conclusions
Digital sovereignty can be defined and measured. Recommended priorities are start-up investment, ICT specialists, R&D and patents (Group A); ICT trade, AI and university–industry cooperation (Group B); and universities, open data and digital trade (Group C). A joint EU digital policy is recommended.
