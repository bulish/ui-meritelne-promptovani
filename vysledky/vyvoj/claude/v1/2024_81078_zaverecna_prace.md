Summary (about 195 words)

Goal
Analyse food security and nutritional status in Ghana: trends, influencing factors, policy effectiveness, and progress toward SDGs 1, 2 and 3 by 2030.

Methods
Descriptive and trend analysis of secondary data (national surveys, FAO/WHO and ministry reports, academic studies), mainly 2022 Global Food Security Index (GFSI) scores compared with 2012.

Main results
Ghana scored 52.6/100 in GFSI 2022, 3rd of 28 Sub-Saharan countries. Affordability (59.9) fell 4.4 points since 2012, with food costs down 31.5 points. Availability (52.4) rose 9.4 points, farm infrastructure by 48.2, yet food loss scored 0.0 (113th). Nutritional standards fell from 57.1 (2021) to 29.8, with no national nutrition plan. Sustainability and Adaptation was weakest (45.1). Food insecurity was 46.7% in northern versus 13.8% in southern regions. Only 213 of 570 planned One Village, One Dam projects were functional. SDG 1 is off track; SDG 2 is partially on track.

Conclusions
Progress is incremental: affordability and nutritional standards worsened, and a double burden of undernutrition and overnutrition persists, with micronutrient deficiencies most prevalent in northern and rural areas. Recommended: cut post-harvest losses through infrastructure investment, adopt a national nutrition strategy, expand inflation-adjusted social protection (LEAP), strengthen climate adaptation, and target regional disparities.
