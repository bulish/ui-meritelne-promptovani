This thesis examines how the fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0) is changing work in Czech retail, focusing on Brno. The literature review traces the four industrial revolutions, describes the physical, digital and biological drivers of Industry 4.0, and notes that Czechia ranked 18th and then 19th in the EU’s digital index (DESI) in 2021 and 2022, with weak infrastructure and big-data scores. The author surveyed 399 valid retail-worker responses, of which 82.95% were from women and 65.66% from people with high-school education.

Employees reported that technology had changed their workload (71.6%) and that management informs and trains them beforehand (about 81% and 84%). Only 43.8% felt they had enough skills. They expected fewer jobs (85.7%), changed working methods (82.9%), fewer tasks (66.9%), threatened job security (69.2%) and a need for new skills (72.7%). Only 43.3% expected better work-life balance. Implementation and impact perceptions correlated strongly (r = 0.91). The author recommends developing soft skills, updating education models, and coordinating government, employers and employment agencies.
