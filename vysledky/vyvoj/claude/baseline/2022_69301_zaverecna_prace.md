Summary

This thesis examines how Vietnam can expand wood exports to Europe. The author argues that exporting drives growth by earning foreign currency, restructuring the economy, creating jobs and building business ties. Vietnam’s strengths include cheap, skilled labor, a tropical climate suited to plantation forestry, and a diversified product range (outdoor furniture, indoor furniture, handicrafts, wood chips). Exports reached about USD 12.37 billion in 2020, and the EU is a large market, yet Vietnam supplies only USD 700–800 million of its annual wood demand of roughly USD 85 billion. The EVFTA, CPTPP and the VPA/FLEGT timber-legality agreement are presented as major opportunities. Obstacles include raw-material shortages, rising input prices, costly FSC/CoC certification, strict EU quality and packaging rules, traceability costs, and small, under-resourced firms. Lessons are drawn from China, Malaysia and Indonesia. The author proposes quality testing for EU standards, more domestic plantation timber, company standards and linkages, export promotion, risk management, digital transparency, and entry into EU distribution channels. The conclusion stresses synchronized solutions and a shift from small- to large-scale production.
