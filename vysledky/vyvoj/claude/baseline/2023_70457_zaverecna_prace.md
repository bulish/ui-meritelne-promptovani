This doctoral thesis from Mendel University in Brno examines how to judge the suitability of wood and wood-based materials for musical instruments. It centres on acoustic conversion efficiency (ACE), the ratio of radiation to damping, and on the frequency resonance technique (FRT), which measures natural vibration modes. Across five papers, the author shows that specimen size matters, but geometry (slenderness, cross-section) matters far more, and can even cause misidentified modes. Moisture above the fibre saturation point barely changes FRT results, whereas dynamic mechanical analysis shows rising damping. Fungal decay cut ACE by about 74%, and thermal modification raised ACE in a species-dependent way. Dynamic stiffness correlated well with bending strength but only weakly with impact strength. Damping (tan δ) varied strongly between modes and sensors, so it was not interchangeable. To address this, the thesis proposes aggregated parameters, ACEcombi and RACEcombi, combining longitudinal, bending and torsional modes. These separated solid wood from plywood, and RACEcombi distinguished even between plywood types. A torsional radiation threshold (KT > 3) is suggested for resonance wood.
