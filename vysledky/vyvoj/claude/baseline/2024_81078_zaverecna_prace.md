Summary (about 190 words)

This master’s thesis assesses food security and nutritional status in Ghana using secondary data and descriptive and trend analysis, chiefly the 2022 Global Food Security Index (GFSI). Ghana scores 52.6 overall, ranking 83rd of 113 countries but 3rd among 28 Sub-Saharan African countries. Availability improved by 9.4 points since 2012, driven by farm infrastructure, yet food loss scores zero. Affordability, the strongest pillar, fell 4.4 points as food-cost indicators dropped 31.5 points. Quality and safety is weak: nutritional standards fell from 57.1 to 29.8 amid no national nutrition plan. Sustainability and adaptation is the weakest pillar (45.1), reflecting poor water management and limited climate-adaptation commitment. Food insecurity is far higher in the north (46.7%) than the south (13.8%). Government programmes (Planting for Food and Jobs, School Feeding, One Village One Dam, NAFCO) show gains but suffer from input shortages, uneven funding and quality problems; only about 37% of planned dams are functional. Ghana is judged off track on SDG 1, partly on track on SDG 2, and mixed on SDG 3. The author recommends cutting post-harvest losses, adopting a national nutrition strategy, scaling social protection, and targeting northern regions.
