This is a 126-page PhD thesis that bundles four published articles, not a single article. I summarized the body chapters only and skipped the abstract and the “Summary” chapter. I did not open the linked DOI full texts (the sandbox has no network access). The reprinted articles in Chapters 3–6 serve as the full text.

Summary (about 165 words)

This doctoral thesis, a compilation of four articles, asks whether forest biomass burned for bioenergy is a viable climate-mitigation option. A scoping review found eight competing definitions of carbon neutrality and concluded that neutrality is not an inherent property of biomass. It depends on feedstock origin and on temporal, spatial and scenario assumptions, so life cycle assessment (LCA) is recommended. A gate-to-grave LCA of household heat from sawmill-residue pellets in Northwest Russia gave 0.015 kg CO₂-eq per MJ. Combustion dominated five of eight impact categories. Pellet production dominated global warming, marine eutrophication and fossil-resource scarcity, and transport mattered least. Natural-gas drying instead of biomass raised global-warming impact, showing the weight of supply-chain choices. A second scoping review identified over twenty monetary valuation methods for ecosystem services, with inconsistent terminology, and called for standardization. The author concludes that forest bioenergy can contribute to mitigation, particularly versus fossil fuels, but only with rigorous, transparent assessment. Caveats: the thesis performs no full monetary valuation, and its fossil-fuel comparison draws on other studies.
