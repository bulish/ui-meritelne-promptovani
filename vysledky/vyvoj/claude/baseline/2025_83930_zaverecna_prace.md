Summary (about 170 words)

This bachelor’s thesis (Mendel University, 2025) traces how Czech foreign policy toward the 1995 Srebrenica genocide evolved from 1995 to 2025, which factors shaped it, and how closely it matches EU positions. Using a qualitative case study, literature review, and secondary analysis of official statements, UN and EU documents, plus email and personal communications with diplomats and Srebrenica experts, the author finds a shift from reserved, hesitant early responses to firm recognition and remembrance. Key milestones include Czechia’s co-sponsorship of UN Security Council Resolution 1019 in 1995, the Foreign Ministry’s 2005 recognition of genocide, anniversary commemorations, support for the ICTY and ICC, and a 2024 vote for the UN General Assembly remembrance resolution. Influencing factors include Czech humanist tradition, communist-era experience, Václav Havel’s leadership, and later membership in NATO, the UN and the EU, with President Zeman’s low profile as a contrast. Czechia aligns strongly with the EU, even recognizing genocide before the EU’s 2009 resolution, but lags in education and prevention, where its efforts remain ad hoc.
