Summary

This bachelor thesis examines how Key Opinion Leaders (KOLs) shape food and beverage purchasing in Vietnam. The author combined 12 semi-structured interviews with an online survey of 210 respondents, analysed in Excel with Chi-Square tests. About 81% of respondents had bought a product or visited a venue after seeing a KOL review, with no significant gender association (p = 0.245). Interest in following social media trends declined with age (p < 0.001), and younger respondents scored highest. Facebook and TikTok were the main platforms for following KOLs. Among influencing factors, consumers’ fondness for a KOL (mean 4.13) and trustworthiness (4.01) scored highest, followed by authentic content (3.84), while appearance scored lowest (2.33). Popularity, expertise, follower interaction and scandals had moderate effects. The author concludes that trustworthiness underpins all other factors. Recommendations include choosing trustworthy KOLs, using Facebook and TikTok, controlling content authenticity, and following trends mainly when targeting young consumers. Stated limitations are a sample skewed toward ages 18–24 and urban residents, a small sample size, and translation issues.
