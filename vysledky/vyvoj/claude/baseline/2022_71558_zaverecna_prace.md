Summary

This diploma thesis addresses the absence of Pan-European qualifications for forest machine operators (harvesters, forwarders, skidders), despite rising demand for skilled operators, high accident rates and strong cross-border labour mobility. A literature review showed that national standards are highly variable, sometimes non-existent or non-compulsory, and typically assessed on a simple pass/fail basis. The author mapped national occupational standards from the UK (benchmark), Czech Republic and Slovenia using content, comparative, thematic and interpretive analysis. UK and Czech standards leaned toward knowledge criteria over practical skills, while Slovenia’s were more balanced and its skidder standards more advanced, likely reflecting mountainous terrain. The results were harmonised into five common competence levels, from work planning to post-use inspection, with outcomes rated at EQF level 4. With ABA International and an Erasmus+ stakeholder group, the author developed a certification scheme with safety-critical criteria and graded assessment. Fourteen pilot tests, mostly in Ireland, refined the standards. Harvester and forwarder qualifications went live, with 65 operators certified by March 2022; the skidder qualification awaited a further pilot. The author endorses the qualifications for improving safety culture, recognition and labour mobility.
