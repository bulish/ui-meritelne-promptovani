Summary (≈185 words)

This diploma thesis by Iskandar Mamasoliev (Mendel University in Brno, 2024) compares income inequality across all 27 EU member states from 2007 to 2023, using Eurostat decile income-share data. It applies three families of measures: the Gini coefficient (with Lorenz curves), the Atkinson index (ε = 0.5, 1, 2), and Generalized Entropy indices (α = 0, 1, 2), calculated with R’s “ineq” package. The author argues no single metric captures inequality’s full complexity. Results show the EU average Gini was essentially stable (0.291 to 0.288), masking sharp national divergence. Romania, Poland, Croatia and Ireland reduced inequality, with Lorenz differences showing top deciles losing share. Sweden and Malta saw large increases driven by top-decile gains. Bulgaria and Lithuania remain among the most unequal, while Slovakia, Slovenia and Czechia are the most equal. Atkinson indices expose lower-end disadvantage (e.g., Romania), whereas GE(2) highlights top-end concentration (e.g., Bulgaria, Sweden). The measures correlate strongly (0.87–0.99) but diverge in where they locate inequality. The author concludes that complementary measures and nationally tailored policies, such as redistribution, education and inclusive labour markets, are needed.
