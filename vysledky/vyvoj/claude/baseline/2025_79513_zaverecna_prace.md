Summary (≈170 words)

This master’s thesis evaluates and compares the real-world performance of two nearby industrial rooftop photovoltaic plants in the Tišnov area of the Czech Republic, using SolarEdge monitoring data from 2023–2024 and KPIs following IEC 61724-1. Plant A (39.8 kWp, 25° tilt) serves a food-processing facility with constant cooling demand; Plant B (139.44 kWp, 15° tilt) serves a two-shift metalworking factory. Plant A reached 58.65% self-sufficiency and 41.35% grid dependency, versus 46.68% and 53.32% for Plant B, though B used a slightly larger share of its output on-site (77.68% vs 74.84%). Performance ratio was stable at Plant A (0.615 to 0.622) but rose from 0.656 to 0.770 at Plant B. PVGIS simulations proved a reliable seasonal baseline, with deviations attributed mainly to weather variability; Plant B’s sharp October 2024 drop pointed to soiling or shading. The author concludes Plant A needs only software-level load shifting, while Plant B would benefit from load management, a battery storing roughly 10–15% of daily output (not simulated), soiling sensors, and hydrophobic coatings.
