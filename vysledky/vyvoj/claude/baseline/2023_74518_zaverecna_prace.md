Summary

This diploma thesis examines whether banking sector development fosters innovation in European Union countries. It uses panel data for 24 of 27 EU states from 2010 to 2020, drawn from World Bank sources. Cyprus, Ireland and Slovenia were excluded because of missing data. Innovation is measured by the number of patent applications. Banking development is captured by four indicators, each lagged one year: bank credit to bank deposits, deposit money bank assets to GDP, and private credit to GDP by deposit money banks, with and without other financial institutions. Controls are GDP per capita, R&D expenditure, tertiary enrollment and governance quality. Pooled OLS diagnostics revealed heteroskedasticity, so the author estimated five models with two-step system GMM, which passed the Arellano-Bond AR(2) and Hansen tests. All four banking indicators had positive, highly significant effects on patenting. The coefficients were largest for bank assets to GDP (0.98), then private bank credit (0.54), private credit including other institutions (0.51) and bank credit to deposits (0.12). R&D expenditure unexpectedly showed a negative effect. The author concludes that a developed banking system and broader financial development support innovation, while noting data limitations.
